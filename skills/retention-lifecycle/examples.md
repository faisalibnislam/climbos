Fictional business: **Ember & Grind**, a UK online coffee roaster selling 250 g bags at £9.50. Confirmed facts: 1,840 customers in 12 months, 2,300 email subscribers with recorded consent, 23% of first-time buyers ordered again within 90 days, median 31 days between first and second order, 45% gross margin, free UK delivery over £25. Numbers are invented for illustration.

### Good (excerpt)

> **Leak:** second purchase. 77% of first-time buyers never reorder within 90 days; those who reach a second order have a median of 31 days between orders.
>
> **Flow 1, post-purchase to second order.** Trigger: first order shipped. Basis: existing customers (soft opt-in at checkout, opt-out in every email); transactional message 1 needs none. Exclude: reordered, open complaint. Exit: second order, unsubscribe, reply.
> 1. Day 0 (transactional): dispatch, brew guide for the roast they bought.
> 2. Day 6: "How's the [roast] tasting?" from Sam, the head roaster; one-question reply ask.
> 3. Day 24 (a week before median use-up): "Running low?" with a one-click reorder of the same bag; no discount.
> 4. Day 34, no order: two roasts similar to theirs; one CTA.
> Metric: second-order rate within 45 days (baseline from data). Guardrails: unsubscribe and complaint rates.
>
> **Review ask:** every customer, day 10, direct link to the review platform; no incentive. Sam replies to every review.
>
> **Win-back:** no order in 62 days (2× cycle). Check-in with the new seasonal roast → one-click "why did you stop?" → free delivery on any one bag [owner to decide; costs about £3 of the £4.28 gross profit on one bag]. Sunset after 6 months with no clicks or orders, after a "keep emailing you?" message.

**Why it's good:** it names the leak from data, times messages to the real use-up cycle, states the consent basis, sets exclusions and exits, protects margin and keeps the review ask non-selective.

### Bad

> Send a weekly newsletter to everyone, plus a 20% off code in the welcome email and again every month to keep people engaged. Only send review requests to customers who rated us 5 stars in our survey. Offer a free bag for every 5-star review. Add everyone who bought to SMS too. Aim for 50% open rates.

**Why it's bad:** no trigger or cycle, discounts that train people to wait, review gating and incentives tied to sentiment, SMS without a separate opt-in, and success judged on opens.
