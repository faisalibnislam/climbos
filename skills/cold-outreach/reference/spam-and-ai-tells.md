## Reference: deliverability patterns, deception patterns and AI tells

### Deliverability (fail the draft if present)

- Any link, image, attachment or tracking pixel in step 1 (the privacy-notice pointer in a UK/EU footer is the only exception).
- More than one link in any later step, or a link to anywhere other than the sender's own domain, a shortened URL, or link text that does not match the destination.
- HTML formatting: bold, colours, buttons, tables, bullet lists, signatures with logos or social icons.
- Subject in ALL CAPS, with emojis, "$", "!!!", "free", "guarantee", "act now", "urgent", "limited time", "100%", or with "Re:" or "Fwd:" on a first touch.
- Body over 125 words in step 1, or over 60 words in a follow-up.
- The same body text across many mailboxes with only merge fields changed.
- Calendar links or meeting-booking pages in a cold step.

### Deception (always fail)

- Implying a prior conversation, meeting or relationship that did not happen ("as discussed", "following up on our call", "Re: our chat").
- Claiming a referral or mutual connection that is not in the confirmed facts.
- False urgency or scarcity: invented deadlines, "only 2 spots left", expiring discounts that do not exist.
- Pretending to be a customer, a fan or a neutral third party.
- Hiding that the email is a sales message, or disguising the sender.
- Fake "breakup" pressure ("this is your last chance").

### AI tells and clichés (rewrite)

**Openers:** I hope this email finds you well · I hope you're doing well · I came across your website · I wanted to reach out · My name is X and I · Quick question (as an opener) · I know you're busy

**Hype and AI vocabulary:** game-changer · unlock · leverage · supercharge · skyrocket · elevate · empower · transform · seamless · cutting-edge · innovative solution · synergy · delve · navigate the complexities · landscape · journey · pivotal · robust · tailored solutions · take your business to the next level

**Structures:** not just X, but Y · it's not about X, it's about Y · whether you're X or Y · say goodbye to X · that's where we come in · the result? · rule-of-three adjective lists · heavy em-dash use · every sentence the same length

**Follow-up clichés:** just bumping this · circling back · just following up · did you get a chance to · per my last email · floating this to the top of your inbox

**Flattery:** loved your recent post · huge fan of what you're doing · I'm impressed by · as a savvy business owner

### Better patterns

- Open with the observation as a plain statement: "Your booking page only takes phone calls."
- State the consequence carefully: "That usually means evening enquiries go to voicemail."
- Give proof in one sentence with a number or mechanism from the facts.
- Ask a question they can answer with one word: "Want the example?"
