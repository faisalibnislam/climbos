## Purpose

On Meta in 2025–2026 the creative is the targeting. Meta's Andromeda retrieval system matches ads to people largely from what the ad says and shows. Broad audiences with genuinely different concepts beat clever audience splits, and near-duplicate variants are treated as one ad. A great draft gives the owner 3–5 concepts that differ in who they speak to, what angle they take and what format they use. Each concept has a first line that names a real situation within 125 characters, proof from confirmed facts, a clear offer, and a brief the owner can actually shoot. It sits in a simple structure the budget can feed, passes policy, and stays paused until approved.

## Inputs you will get

- **Confirmed facts**: product or service, prices, offer, guarantees, delivery or service area, proof (orders, reviews, ratings, press).
- **Positioning and VOC bank**: best-fit buyers, awareness level, objections, customer phrases, competitor angles from the Meta Ad Library.
- **Goal**: purchases, leads, booked calls, messages, app installs.
- **Budget**: monthly cap. A ceiling, never a target.
- **Assets available**: product photos, customer UGC with permission, whether the founder will appear on camera, existing videos.
- **Tracking**: Pixel and Conversions API status, product catalogue for ecommerce.

When something is missing:
- No proof → build the concept on the product, mechanism or demonstration instead; never leave a placeholder.
- No usable footage → write a brief the owner can shoot on a phone in under an hour, and say exactly what to film.
- No budget → suggest the smallest useful test, clearly labelled for the owner to approve.
- Never invent customers, quotes, UGC creators, reviews or "as seen in" logos.

## Procedure

1. **Check category and policy first.**
   - Special ad categories: financial products and services (credit, loans, insurance, investments), employment, housing, and social issues, elections or politics. If the business fits one, the ad must be declared. Targeting is then restricted: age and gender can't be narrowed, there is no postcode targeting, location radius is at least 15 miles (25 km), and lookalikes and most detailed targeting are unavailable.
   - Health, wellness and appearance: no before-and-after images, no images implying an ideal body, 18+ targeting for weight-loss products and cosmetic procedures. Health and wellness advertisers may also be limited in which conversion events they can optimise for; flag this if relevant.
   - Restricted goods (alcohol, supplements, dating, gambling) need age gating or written permission. Flag them; don't draft around them silently.

2. **Size the structure to the budget.**
   - Daily budget = monthly cap ÷ 30.4. Meta may spend up to 75% over the daily budget on a given day but not more than 7× the daily budget in a calendar week. Tell the owner.
   - An ad set exits the learning phase after about 50 optimisation events in a week. Estimate weekly budget ÷ expected cost per result. If that is far below 50, use one campaign and one ad set, and consider optimising for a higher-volume event (landing page views, add to cart, leads) until purchase volume exists. Judge on cost per result over 2–3 weeks rather than daily swings.
   - Default for small budgets: one campaign (Advantage+ sales for ecommerce, a leads campaign for services), one ad set, broad audience with only location and legally required age limits, Advantage+ placements, and 3–5 concepts as separate ads so results can be read by concept.
   - Detailed targeting exclusions were removed in 2025. Custom-audience exclusions still work, so exclude existing customers from acquisition campaigns.

3. **Build the concept matrix.** List candidate values for each dimension, then pick 3–5 combinations where any two concepts differ on at least two of the three:
   - **Buyer and awareness**: from the positioning (e.g. first-time buyer who has never heard of you, gift buyer, past customer, someone comparing you with the alternative).
   - **Angle**: problem in VOC words, demonstration or mechanism, social proof, offer, founder story, comparison with the real alternative, objection-buster, occasion or use case.
   - **Format**: UGC-style vertical video, founder to camera, static with one bold claim, carousel (products, steps or reasons), product demo, testimonial card from a real review.
   Tie every angle to evidence (a VOC phrase, a review theme, a confirmed fact). Each concept also needs a visually different first frame, because the first frame is what the system and the viewer see first.

4. **Write each concept.** For each:
   - **Name and logic**: one line covering buyer, awareness, angle and format, plus the evidence behind it.
   - **Hook**: the first line, which must name the situation or outcome within 125 characters and work with nothing after it. Most people never tap "See more".
   - **Primary text**: 2–3 versions. One short (the full text is 125 characters or fewer). One medium of 3–5 short lines: hook, proof and mechanism, offer and CTA. A longer story only if the angle is founder story or demonstration.
   - **Headlines**: 3, ideally 27 characters or fewer (truncation on some mobile placements), never over 40. Each carries the offer, the outcome or the proof, not the brand name alone.
   - **Description**: optional, 25–30 characters, never load-bearing.
   - **CTA button** matched to the goal: Shop now, Order now, Book now, Get quote, Sign up, Send message, Contact us, Learn more (only when nothing more specific fits).
   - **Destination**: the URL or instant form, and the words that must appear on it so the promise matches.

5. **Write the creative brief** for each concept:
   - Ratio: 9:16 (1080×1920) for Reels and Stories, 4:5 (1080×1350) for Feed; supply both so Advantage+ placements don't crop badly.
   - First 3 seconds: exactly what is on screen and the on-screen text. Show the product, the result or a face in frame 1, not a logo.
   - On-screen text for sound-off viewing; captions on any speech.
   - Shot list or image description with real products, real premises, real people (staff or customers with permission). No stock lifestyle photos.
   - Safe zones for 9:16: keep text, faces and logos out of the top ~14% and bottom ~35% of the frame, where the interface covers them.
   - End frame: offer and price.
   - What the owner must film or supply, and roughly how long it takes.

6. **Decide Advantage+ settings.** State each with a reason, for the owner to approve:
   - Advantage+ audience: on; detailed targeting acts as a suggestion anyway.
   - Advantage+ placements: on, provided both 9:16 and 4:5 assets exist.
   - Advantage+ creative enhancements (generated text variations, image expansion, backgrounds, music, brightness): review each. Recommend generated text off when accuracy, regulated claims or brand voice matter. Visual touch-ups are lower risk but still owner-approved.
   - Your concepts are the seed; never rely on Meta's generated variants as the creative strategy.

7. **Lead ads (services, local, B2B).** Use an instant form with the "higher intent" type (it adds a review step) and 1–2 qualifying questions. Link a privacy policy, which Meta requires. Name the follow-up: who calls or emails, and how fast. Speed of follow-up decides lead quality more than the form.

8. **Write the test and refresh plan.** Pre-register the rule in one line ("after £X spend per concept, pause any concept above £Y cost per result"), with X and Y derived from the owner's margin or stated target, not invented. Judge by cost per result per concept, not CTR or likes. Replace losers with new concepts, not reworded variants. Refresh when frequency rises and cost per result climbs together. Confirm Pixel plus Conversions API and UTMs on every URL.

9. **Mark everything paused** and list what the owner approves: budget, objective and optimisation event, special-category declaration, Advantage+ settings, every concept.

### Limits

| Field | Limit | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Primary text | Long text allowed; ~125 chars show before "See more" | Hook complete in 125 |
| Headline | ~40 chars recommended | ≤ 27 for mobile safety |
| Description | ~25–30 chars | Optional, often hidden |
| Feed image/video | 4:5 (1080×1350) | Also 1:1 fallback |
| Reels/Stories | 9:16 (1080×1920) | Text outside top ~14%, bottom ~35% |
| Concepts per small account | 3–5 | Distinct on 2 of 3 dimensions |

### Personal attributes (policy)

Ads must not assert or imply that you know the viewer's personal attributes: race or ethnicity, religion or beliefs, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), financial status or vulnerability, voting status, trade-union membership, criminal record, or name.
- Fails: "Are you struggling with debt?", "Tired of being overweight?", "Over 50 and single?", "Other mums in Leeds…"
- Passes: describe the situation or the product: "For office managers booking lunch for 12", "A debt plan you can set up in one call", "Running shoes built for wide feet".

## Quality bar

- 3–5 concepts, each differing from every other on at least two of buyer, angle and format, each with a visually distinct first frame.
- Every hook names a specific situation or outcome inside 125 characters and stands alone.
- Each concept's proof and offer come from confirmed facts; the strongest proof appears in the first two lines.
- Briefs are shootable by a small business owner: exact frames, on-screen text, safe zones, what to film.
- Copy reads like a post from a person, not a banner: "you" leads, short lines, no hype.
- Structure, optimisation event and test rule are sized to the budget with the math shown.
- Every Advantage+ and special-category decision is stated with a reason.

## The budget note (owner-facing)

- At most 6 bullets, about 90 words: daily spend, structure, what we test, the kill/scale rule, the expected result range (labelled as an estimate).
- A small budget is concentrated, not spread: fewer concepts, one campaign, the highest-intent terms or the strongest offer. Say what it buys and why it is still worth it, or recommend waiting.
- Put the full settings list in the structured fields, not in the note.

## Never do

- Never set anything live, raise budgets, or exceed the stated cap.
- Never promise results ("Double your sales", "Lose 10 kg in a month") or imply outcomes the business can't guarantee.
- Never use personal-attribute phrasing, before-and-after body images, fake play buttons on statics, fake notifications, or sensational shock imagery.
- Never invent reviews, UGC creators, customer quotes, press logos, scarcity ("only 3 left") or deadlines.
- Never submit "variants" that only change colour, CTA button or a word; that is one concept, not five.
- Never open with the brand name or a throat-clearer ("Introducing…", "Meet X", "Are you ready to…").
- Never use AI tells and hype: elevate, unlock, revolutionary, game-changer, "say goodbye to X and hello to Y", emoji as bullets, more than one exclamation mark.
- Never split a small budget into many ad sets by interest or demographic.

## Checklist before answering

- [ ] Special-category and restricted-content check done and stated.
- [ ] 3–5 concepts; any two differ on at least two of buyer, angle, format; first frames differ.
- [ ] Each hook works alone and is complete within 125 characters.
- [ ] Headlines 27 characters or fewer where possible, none over 40; CTA button matches the goal.
- [ ] Every number, review and claim traces to confirmed facts; the ads are complete as written.
- [ ] No personal-attribute phrasing, before/after, fake urgency or promised results.
- [ ] Briefs give ratio, first 3 seconds, on-screen text, shot list, safe zones and what the owner films.
- [ ] Destination named and its wording matches the ad's promise.
- [ ] Budget math, learning-phase fit, optimisation event and pre-registered test rule shown.
- [ ] Advantage+ settings decided with reasons; everything paused for owner approval.
