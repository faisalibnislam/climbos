Fictional business: **Kestrel Studio**, a four-person Shopify agency in Leeds that fixes product pages and checkouts for independent shops. Confirmed facts: in Kestrel's free audits of 60 Shopify stores (January–September 2026), 41 showed the delivery cost for the first time at checkout rather than on the product page. Audited stores must not be named. Brand kit: navy #14213D, off-white #F5F1E8, orange #E76F24; headlines in Fraunces SemiBold, body in Inter; wordmark logo in off-white or navy. The post (LinkedIn and Instagram) argues for showing delivery cost on the product page. Task: brief the single image for the post.

### Good

> **Message (under 10 words):** 41 of 60 stores hid delivery cost until checkout.
> **Job:** prove the post's claim at a glance. **Type:** typographic card with a dot chart of confirmed data. No store screenshots: the audited stores are confidential and a mocked-up checkout would be fake.
> **Placement:** one portrait image for the Instagram feed and LinkedIn (confirm current pixel specs; e.g. 1080 × 1350). Keep every element inside the central 3:4 area so the Instagram grid crop doesn't cut it.
> **Hierarchy:**
> 1. "41 of 60" in Fraunces SemiBold, off-white, the largest element, upper third.
> 2. Headline directly below, about half that size: "stores showed delivery cost only at checkout".
> 3. Dot grid, 10 across × 6 down, centred: 41 dots filled orange, 19 dots as off-white outlines (filled versus outlined, so the meaning doesn't rely on colour).
> 4. Source line in Inter, small, off-white: "Kestrel Studio audits of 60 Shopify stores, Jan–Sep 2026".
> 5. Kestrel wordmark in off-white, small, bottom-left inside the 3:4 area.
> **Colour and contrast:** navy background. Off-white on navy is about 14:1 and orange on navy about 5:1, both above WCAG AA; don't put orange text on off-white (under 3:1).
> **Don'ts:** no store names, logos or screenshots; no shopping-cart clip art; no percentage (keep the raw counts the post uses); nothing in the corners outside the 3:4 crop.
> **Alt text:** "Chart of 60 dots, 41 filled in orange. Text: 41 of 60 stores showed delivery cost only at checkout. Source: Kestrel Studio audits of 60 Shopify stores, January to September 2026."

**Why it's good:** one message drawn from a confirmed number, an honest image type (no fake screenshots of real or invented stores), a hierarchy a designer can build without questions, brand colours applied by hex with contrast checked, the meaning carried by shape as well as colour, crop-safe placement, and alt text that includes the numbers.

### Bad

> Create an eye-catching, modern graphic for our CRO post. Use a stock photo of a happy woman shopping on her phone, plus a mockup of a Shopify checkout showing "+300% conversions". Put our logo big at the top. Text: "Unlock your store's potential with Kestrel Studio, the UK's leading Shopify conversion experts! Book a free call today!" Use bright, fun colours.

**Why it's bad:** no single message or hierarchy, a stock model implied to be a customer, a fake checkout screenshot with an invented "+300%" result, an unsourced "leading" claim and banned "unlock", the logo as the focal point, a paragraph of on-image text, the brand kit ignored, and no ratio, safe zone or alt text.
