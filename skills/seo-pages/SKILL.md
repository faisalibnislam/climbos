## Purpose

A great SEO page wins a high-intent query for a small business and is the source AI answers cite. It is the format the results page rewards, executed better. It answers each question in its first sentence, contains something no competitor page has (real prices, photos, data, process, experience), shows clearly who wrote it and why they know, links into a tight cluster, and leads the reader to one sensible next step. It would be worth publishing even if search engines didn't exist. Mass-produced pages are the opposite and can sink the whole site.

## Inputs you will get

- **Target query or topic**, or a service list plus areas served.
- **Confirmed facts**: services, prices, areas, process, credentials, proof, Business Profile details.
- **First-hand material**: the owner's own prices, job photos, numbers, case notes, opinions, and an author bio. This is what makes the page worth ranking.
- **Research notes**: the SERP read (what ranks, whether an AI Overview appears and which sources it cites, People Also Ask, map pack, video, Reddit threads), competitor pages, and sub-questions from sales calls or forums.
- **Existing site pages**, for internal links and to avoid two pages competing for one query.

When something is missing:
- No first-hand material → write the brief, not the page, and list exactly what to gather: photos, prices, a worked example, the owner's answers to 3–5 questions.
- No sourced market data → state only the business's own numbers. Any market figure needs a cited source, or leave it out.
- No author → byline "The <Business> team". Never invent a person.

## Procedure

1. **Read the intent from the SERP.** Classify the query as informational, commercial investigation, transactional or local. Note which page types rank (service pages, lists, comparisons, tools, videos), whether an AI Overview appears and whom it cites, whether a map pack shows, and what every ranking page fails to answer.

2. **Decide whether the page should exist.** It needs real demand, a fit with what the business sells, something unique to say, and no existing page already targeting the query (if one exists, improve it instead). ClimbOS flags an exact target-phrase match in an observed title or heading as a review warning; that lexical match is a clue, not proof of search cannibalization. Prefer high-intent pages over informational blog posts. AI summaries now absorb informational clicks: Pew found users clicked a traditional result on about 8% of visits with an AI summary versus 15% without. Cited pages still earn more clicks than uncited ones, and commercial and local pages hold up better.

3. **Choose the page type and follow its rules.**

   | Type | Use when | Must contain |
   |---|---|---|
   | Service page | "service" or "service + city" queries | What you do, who for, real prices or price drivers, process, photos of real work, credentials, reviews quoted with permission, FAQ from real customer questions, one CTA |
   | Service-area page | You serve several towns from one base | One page listing real areas served, with any travel fees or limits |
   | Location page | Only with real local substance | Jobs done there, local specifics (housing stock, rules, access), local reviews, staff or premises there, local prices or availability |
   | Pricing / cost page | "how much does X cost" | The answer in sentence one, your own prices, what changes them, worked examples, sourced market ranges only |
   | Comparison (X vs Y) | Buyers compare two options | Criteria-based table, who each option suits (including when the other is better), dated and sourced competitor facts, disclosure that you're one of the options |
   | Alternatives | "[competitor] alternatives" | Honest reasons people switch, fair summaries of several options, where you fit and where you don't |
   | How-to | Only when written from first-hand practice | Real steps, photos, tools, time, common mistakes, when to call a professional |

   City-swap test: if you replace the town name and the page still works, don't publish it; fold it into the service-area page.

4. **Build the brief** (always produce it, even when also drafting):
   - Target query and intent; primary reader and their situation.
   - SERP read: what ranks, AI Overview present or not, cited sources, what's missing.
   - 5–10 fan-out sub-questions the page must answer, taken from People Also Ask, forums, sales calls and AI Mode follow-ups.
   - Unique value: the first-hand data, photos, prices, process or opinion only this business has.
   - Experience signals: author name and role, photos, customer quotes, dates.
   - Outline: H1, H2s as sub-questions, answer-first paragraphs.
   - Internal links: up, across and down (step 8).
   - Schema (step 9); title, meta description and slug; the conversion path; update cadence and "last reviewed" date.

5. **Write answer-first.** The intro answers the query in 1–2 sentences, then says what the page covers. Under every H2, the first sentence answers that heading's question with explicit numbers, dates and conditions. Use tables for comparisons and prices and numbered lists for steps. Keep important content in HTML text, never only in images. Length is whatever it takes to answer the fan-out fully; there is no word-count target.

6. **Add E-E-A-T signals.** Trust matters most.
   - A named author with role and relevant experience, plus a short bio.
   - A visible "Last reviewed" date; sources linked for any external figure.
   - Original photos with descriptive alt text.
   - Contact details and business information consistent with the Google Business Profile.
   - If AI drafted substantial parts, the human who verified it is the byline. Disclose automation where readers would reasonably expect it, in line with Google's who/how/why guidance.

7. **Write title, meta and slug.**
   - Title: about 50–60 characters (display truncates around 600 px). Query words near the front in natural order, brand at the end if room.
   - One H1 matching the query naturally; it can differ from the title.
   - Meta description: about 150–160 characters that answer and give a reason to click. Google often rewrites it, so make the opening paragraph strong too.
   - Slug: short, lowercase, hyphenated, the core topic only, no dates.

8. **Plan internal links.** Link up to the cluster hub (the main service page), across to 2–3 sibling pages, and down to the money page or booking page, with descriptive anchor text. List only exact same-site URLs directly observed during this run; never guess a path or use an external page as an internal link. ClimbOS validates each destination against the current run's pages and omits unverified links. List 2–3 existing pages that should link to the new page only when their exact observed URLs are available. Keep the cluster tight around what the business sells and where.

9. **Use honest schema only.** Mark up only what is visible on the page: Organization or LocalBusiness, Service, Product and Offer, BreadcrumbList, Article with author. FAQPage only for real visible FAQs; it rarely earns rich results for most sites now, so expect no SERP gain. Never add review markup for the business's own reviews about itself; Google ignores self-serving review markup for local businesses and organisations.

10. **Cover AI answer visibility.**
    - Google says no special AI optimisation is needed: the page must be indexed and eligible for snippets (no nosnippet).
    - AI Mode runs related sub-queries ("fan-out"), so clear answers to adjacent questions create more chances to be cited.
    - Keep facts identical across the site, Business Profile and directories (prices, areas, hours), and state them in quotable sentences.
    - Recommend a monthly prompt check ("best [category] for [segment] in [city]") and tracking AI referrals (chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini) as a separate source. AI feature clicks are reported inside Search Console's web totals.

11. **Set the conversion path.** One CTA appropriate to intent: transactional → book, buy or call; commercial → quote, trial or comparison download; informational → the related service or a lead magnet.

12. **Set maintenance.** Name what to re-check and when: prices and competitor facts quarterly. Refresh the "last reviewed" date only when content actually changed.

### Limits

| Element | Target |
|---|---|
| Title tag | ~50–60 chars (~600 px) |
| Meta description | ~150–160 chars |
| H1 | One per page, natural query wording |
| Intro answer | First 1–2 sentences |
| Each H2 | Answered in its first sentence |
| Internal links | 1 up, 2–3 across, 1 down + 2–3 inbound |

## Quality bar

- Page type matches what the SERP rewards for the query.
- At least one element of information gain no competitor page has, and it appears early.
- Every H2 is a real sub-question answered in its first sentence; the fan-out list is fully covered.
- Named author with relevant experience, dated review, sourced external figures, original photos.
- Every price, number and claim traces to confirmed facts or a cited source.
- Title, meta and slug are within targets and written for humans.
- Internal links and schema are specified and honest.
- The page passes the city-swap test and would be worth publishing without search traffic.

## Never do

- Never produce scaled template pages: city × service grids, near-identical location pages, or bulk pages made mainly to rank. Scaled content abuse is a spam policy violation however the pages are made, and Google has reportedly extended its spam policies to AI Overviews and AI Mode.
- Never invent statistics, market prices, case studies, author credentials, reviews or "studies show".
- Never write throat-clearing intros ("In today's ever-evolving landscape", "In this comprehensive guide, we'll delve into…") or close with "In conclusion".
- Never keyword-stuff, hide text, or repeat the query unnaturally in headings.
- Never make false or unsourced claims about competitors on comparison pages, or hide that you are one of the options.
- Never use FAQ schema for content that isn't visible, or self-serving review markup.
- Never recommend informational blog volume as the main engine for a small business without strong justification.
- Never publish a how-to the business has no first-hand experience of.

## Checklist before answering

- [ ] Intent classified and SERP read summarised, including whether an AI Overview appears and whom it cites.
- [ ] Page type matches the SERP; the page passes the city-swap test.
- [ ] Brief complete: reader, 5–10 fan-out questions, unique value, experience signals, outline, links, schema, title/meta/slug, CTA, update cadence.
- [ ] Intro answers the query in 1–2 sentences; every H2 answered in its first sentence.
- [ ] Information gain is present and early.
- [ ] Every number and claim traces to confirmed facts or a cited source; the page is complete as written.
- [ ] Named author, "last reviewed" date, original photos specified.
- [ ] Title ~50–60 chars, meta ~150–160 chars, short slug.
- [ ] Internal links up, across, down and inbound listed with anchors; schema limited to visible content.
- [ ] No banned phrases, no stuffing, no scaled or doorway pattern.
