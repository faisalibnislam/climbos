Fictional business: **Fieldnote**, a form builder for small service businesses. Confirmed facts: questions can branch (each one can depend on the previous answer); forms can take card payments and deposits; free plan covers up to 3 forms. Founder Dana Okafor ran Brightside Cleaning in Manchester for six years before Fieldnote. Founder material (Dana's own records): in 2021 Brightside's website quote form had 22 questions and every customer saw all of them, so one-off end-of-tenancy customers were asked how often they wanted regular cleans; she rebuilt it so each question depended on the answer before and no customer saw more than 7; completed quote requests were 17 in the 8 weeks before and 41 in the 8 weeks after; at the same time she added a £20 deposit at booking, and first-clean no-shows went from about one in eight to none over the next two months. VoC (customer interview, permission to quote anonymously): "I didn't need a shorter form. I needed fewer questions for each person." Task: founder post for Dana; goal: free-plan sign-ups from cleaning, trades and salon owners.

### Good

> Our quote form had 22 questions. When I cut it to 7 per customer, completed quote requests went from 17 to 41.
>
> Before Fieldnote, I ran a cleaning company in Manchester for six years. Our website quote form asked every customer all 22 questions.
>
> Most of those questions only applied to some people. Someone booking a one-off end-of-tenancy clean still had to tell us how often they wanted a regular clean.
>
> So in 2021 I rebuilt it. Each question depended on the answer before, and nobody saw more than 7. End-of-tenancy customers never saw the regular-clean questions at all.
>
> In the 8 weeks before the change we had 17 completed quote requests. In the 8 weeks after, 41.
>
> I changed one other thing at the same time: the form took a £20 deposit at booking. First-clean no-shows had been about one in eight. For the next two months there were none. I made both changes together, so I can't split the credit between them.
>
> One of our customers put it better than I did: "I didn't need a shorter form. I needed fewer questions for each person."
>
> That rebuilt form is the reason Fieldnote exists.
>
> If you run a service business, open your quote form and count the questions a new customer actually needs before you can price the job. Then count how many they see. The difference is what to cut first.
>
> Which question on your form does nobody answer properly?
