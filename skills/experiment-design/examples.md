Fictional business: **Quillfern Studio**, a two-person brand-design agency selling a £2,400 "brand sprint" to new cafés and bakeries. Confirmed facts: about 600 site visitors a month, 6 enquiries a month, 2 sprints sold a month. Numbers are invented for illustration.

### Good

> **Decision:** whether to replace the £2,400 sprint with a £450 "menu and signage refresh" entry offer.
>
> **Why not a page A/B test:** 6 enquiries a month. Detecting even a doubling of a 1% enquiry rate (d = 1 pt) needs about 16 × 0.01 × 0.99 ÷ 0.01² ≈ 1,580 visitors per variant: over 5 months. Not feasible.
>
> **Test 1, outreach reply test:** two emails to 400 cafés opened in the last 12 months that trade as limited companies (checked on Companies House; sole traders excluded under PECR), published business addresses, opt-out in every message, randomly split. A: sprint offer. B: refresh offer. Baseline reply 4% (assumption; no history). Detect a doubling: ≈385 per variant, so 200 per variant is **directional only**, stated up front.
> Rule: B positive replies ≥2× A → build the refresh page; B ≤ A → drop; between → rewrite once. Guardrails: bounce <2%, negative replies <1%.
>
> **Test 2, conversations:** 6 calls with past enquirers who didn't buy, fixed script. Rule: if ≥3 of 6 name price unprompted, price is the blocker.
>
> **Readout (example):** A 5/200 positive (2.5%), B 13/200 (6.5%). Rule says build the refresh page. Confidence: directional (under the computed volume). Calls: 4 of 6 named price ("we'd spend that on the oven", call notes 3 Nov). Next: build the refresh page by 15 Nov and run a 3-angle ad lab.

**Why it's good:** it starts from the decision, shows why a page test can't finish, picks tests the volume supports, registers thresholds and guardrails in advance, labels confidence honestly and ends with one action.

### Bad

> Let's A/B test the homepage: blue vs green CTA button and "Get started" vs "Book a call". Run it for a week and pick the winner once it hits 95% significance. Industry average conversion is 2.35%, so we should aim for 4%.

**Why it's bad:** micro-tweaks that can't be detected at 600 visitors a month, a peek-and-stop significance rule, an invented benchmark, no decision named, no guardrails and no volume maths.
