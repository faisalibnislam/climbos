## Purpose

A great lead magnet solves one narrow, urgent pre-purchase problem for the exact buyer, can be used in minutes, and offers something an AI chatbot can't: the business's own data, a working tool, a sample of the real product, or a personalised result. Using it leads naturally to buying. The capture flow is light: one or two fields, part of the value shown before the gate, instant delivery, consent recorded properly, and a nurture sequence for people who agreed to it. Success is measured in customers, not downloads.

## Inputs you will get

- **Business type and offer**: what is sold, price, sales cycle, the paid next step.
- **Buyer and VOC**: pre-purchase questions, fears and tasks from reviews, FAQs, sales calls and forums.
- **Proprietary assets**: the owner's prices, data, templates, process, samples, past work, and capacity (how many audits or samples they can handle a week).
- **Stack**: form tool, ESP, whether interactive tools can be built.
- **Geography**: drives consent rules.

When something is missing:
- No proprietary data → don't fake a "benchmark report". Pick a tool, template, sample or audit built from confirmed facts instead.
- No capacity figure for audits or samples → `[NEEDS FACT: how many per week the owner can deliver]`. Never promise a turnaround the owner hasn't confirmed.
- No VOC on pre-purchase fears → state the assumed problem and how to validate it (5 sales-call notes, FAQ log).

## Procedure

1. **Find the pre-purchase job.** Name the most common thing buyers try to work out before buying: "how much will this cost me", "which option fits me", "is mine good enough", "what do I ask a supplier", "will it work for my case". Take it from VOC, FAQs or sales calls and cite where it came from.

2. **Pick the lightest format that does that job.**

   | Business type | Strongest magnets | Bridge to the offer |
   |---|---|---|
   | B2B SaaS | ROI or cost-of-status-quo calculator, a template built on the product's logic, migration checklist, the free trial itself | Result pre-fills a trial or demo |
   | B2C app | Free tier or trial, quiz leading to a personalised plan, short email challenge | Plan opens in the app |
   | Local service | Instant price estimator, free quote or survey (capacity-capped), "questions to ask before you hire" checklist, local guide with real local data | Estimate becomes a booking |
   | Ecommerce / DTC | Sample or starter kit, fit/shade/size quiz, fit guide, restock alerts | Quiz result links to the matching product |
   | Agency / consultant | Free personalised audit (instant where possible), teardown, template, benchmark from own client data with permission | Audit findings scope the paid work |
   | Creator / coach | Sample lesson, mini-course, template or swipe file, self-assessment quiz | Next lesson is in the paid product |

   Long generic ebooks attract the wrong leads and underperform. Gated generic information has also lost value now that AI answers give it away.

3. **Run three tests before committing.**
   - **Uniqueness**: could a chatbot produce this in 30 seconds? If yes, choose something else.
   - **Bridge**: does using it naturally lead to the paid offer? Name the next step inside the asset (a pre-filled order, a "book the fix" link on the audit).
   - **Consumption**: can the buyer get the value in under 5 minutes? A 40-page PDF fails.

4. **Name it with the outcome.** Use format + outcome + specifics + time: "Office Lunch Planner (1 page): headcount, dietary needs and budget in one place". Never "Ultimate Guide", "Secrets", "Blueprint" or "Masterclass" unless that is literally what it is.

5. **Specify the asset.**
   - Tools and calculators: the inputs, the logic (formulas built only from confirmed prices and assumptions, with assumptions shown to the user), the output, and the CTA on the result.
   - Quizzes: as few questions as produce a useful result, a progress indicator, and result types mapped to specific offers.
   - Checklists and templates: the full contents, one page where possible, in the buyer's words.
   - Audits: the exact checks, what is automated and what is manual, turnaround, weekly capacity cap, and the format of the result (e.g. a short video plus a 5-item list).
   - Samples: what's included, who qualifies, and how fulfilment works.

6. **Design the capture.** Hand the page to the landing-pages skill, specifying:
   - The promise, a preview image or partial result, who it's for, time to use.
   - The form: email only, plus website for audits or one qualifying field if the owner truly needs it.
   - Show part of the result before the gate and send the full result by email. Don't gate a tool so hard that nobody starts it.
   - Button text with the outcome ("Send my estimate", "Get my audit").

7. **Capture consent properly.**
   - Say in plain words what they'll receive and how often.
   - EU and UK: if a nurture sequence follows, marketing consent is a separate, unticked checkbox, and the magnet is still delivered without it unless counsel advises otherwise. Use double opt-in for EU signups, especially Germany.
   - US: clear disclosure and a working unsubscribe in every email.
   - Store consent evidence: timestamp, IP hash, form version, the exact text shown, and the source magnet tag for segmentation. Link the privacy policy.
   - Inbound leads go only into a consented nurture sequence, never into cold outreach. A cold prospect who opts in moves to the consented lane with the new consent recorded.

8. **Deliver instantly.** Show the result on the page and send email 1 within minutes. Email 1 delivers the asset, says what comes next and when, and asks one question. Delivering the requested item is transactional; anything promotional after that needs the consent above.

9. **Plan the nurture hand-off.** Outline 4–6 emails for the lifecycle-email skill, each building on the magnet's topic toward the offer: use the result, the main objection, proof, the offer, then a preference or stop email. Exit on purchase or booking. Tag every lead with its magnet.

10. **Plan promotion.** Put the magnet where buyers already are: inline on the related service or product pages, the Google Business Profile, social posts, the email signature, partner sites, and a lead-ad or landing-page ad concept for the meta-ads or google-ads skills. No aggressive entry pop-ups.

11. **Define measurement.** Track visit → start (tools and quizzes) → complete → opt-in → customer, plus time to purchase and revenue per lead by magnet. Judge on the lead-to-customer rate, not downloads. Practitioner ranges suggest good small-business tool magnets convert 20–40% of visitors who start the tool, and gated PDFs much less; treat that as directional, never as a promise to the owner.

## Quality bar

- Solves one specific pre-purchase job, named from VOC or sales evidence.
- Uses something only this business has: data, a tool, a sample, a personalised result.
- The value arrives in under 5 minutes; the next step to buying is built into the asset.
- The name states format, outcome and specifics.
- The form asks for the minimum, shows value before the gate, and delivers instantly.
- Consent wording, checkbox design, evidence storage and lane separation are specified.
- The nurture outline and promotion plan are concrete; measurement ends at customers.
- Every number, turnaround, capacity and claim traces to confirmed facts.

## Never do

- Never propose a generic ebook, "ultimate guide", trend report or 40-page PDF.
- Never fabricate benchmark data, survey results, "based on 10,000 businesses" claims, testimonials or download counts.
- Never promise outcomes ("Double your leads in 30 days") or a turnaround the owner hasn't confirmed.
- Never pre-tick consent, bundle marketing consent into the download without disclosure, or use confirmshaming ("No thanks, I don't like saving money").
- Never ask for phone, company size, budget or more unless the owner needs it for the next step and accepts the lower opt-in rate.
- Never route magnet leads into cold outreach, or cold prospects into nurture without fresh consent.
- Never use fake scarcity on free items ("only 3 downloads left").
- Never use hype naming: unlock, secrets, blueprint, revolutionary, game-changing.

## Checklist before answering

- [ ] The pre-purchase job is named, with its evidence source.
- [ ] The format fits the business type and passes the uniqueness, bridge and under-5-minutes tests.
- [ ] The name states format, outcome and specifics; no hype words.
- [ ] The asset is fully specified: contents or logic, assumptions, result, CTA, capacity for audits or samples.
- [ ] The capture page brief has a preview or partial result, minimum fields and an outcome button.
- [ ] Consent: plain-language promise, separate unticked marketing checkbox where required, double opt-in for EU, evidence fields listed, privacy link.
- [ ] Instant delivery and email 1 written; 4–6 email nurture outline with exit on purchase.
- [ ] Promotion placements listed.
- [ ] Measurement runs to lead → customer; any benchmark labelled directional.
- [ ] Every number and claim traces to confirmed facts; gaps are `[NEEDS FACT: …]`.
