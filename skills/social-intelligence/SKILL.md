## Purpose

Make a social post feel native to this business, its audience, and the platform. Humor and trends are creative choices, not universal defaults. A current format can help a good idea travel; it cannot make a weak or mismatched idea useful.

## Before choosing a style

Use the business type, offer, audience, country and language, approved brand voice, examples and exclusions, selected platform, and goal already in the run. Treat owner-provided identity and audience details as reported unless verified. Do not infer a brand's humor tolerance from its industry alone.

Choose the voice deliberately: plain, warm, expert, playful, cheeky, dry, absurd, or another evidence-backed fit. Match the intensity to approved examples and the platform. If the owner has not established a playful voice, use light wit only when it helps explain the customer's real situation; do not force a joke into every post. A SaaS company may use a clever operator observation without turning a professional post into a meme. A sexual-health brand may use non-graphic, non-shaming cheekiness when its approved voice, audience, market, and platform support it. Do not make the same joke travel unchanged across channels.

For health, finance, legal, alcohol, gambling, children's products, and other sensitive or regulated categories, keep claims accurate and use a restrained tone unless the owner has explicitly approved a specific style. Never use humor to obscure a safety limitation, product term, health claim, or customer risk. For sexual-health or adult products, keep content non-graphic and for adults; do not target minors or infer that a product is available, effective, or permitted in a market without supporting facts. Avoid sexual pressure, stigma, body-shaming, coercion, or misinformation. LinkedIn is a professional context: do not use overt sexual innuendo or sexually suggestive meme jokes there; recast the idea as a factual, professional observation or choose another angle. Recheck current platform and market rules for sensitive material; this playbook is not a compliance determination.

## Trend and meme decision

For each social brief or weekly calendar, consider whether a timely angle would help. Use a trend or meme-inspired format only when all four checks pass:

1. **Current signal:** the research supplied in this run shows recent activity on the target platform or in the target market. Use an exact source URL. Prefer a platform's own trend resource when it fits; distinguish paid-ad examples from evidence of organic popularity. A search result, model memory, old example, or trending label without a recent date is not proof that a trend is current.
2. **Audience fit:** the people this business serves are likely to understand the reference, in the stated geography and language. Do not transfer a trend from another audience just because it is popular somewhere.
3. **Brand and platform fit:** the reference suits the approved voice, category, platform norms, current rules, and the business's reputation risk. A trend that depends on tragedy, a sensitive news event, politics, harassment, or a competitor's protected material is not a reach shortcut.
4. **Useful point:** the post still teaches, demonstrates, connects, or makes a relevant offer. The reference should frame the business's own useful idea, not replace it.

If a source is used in a post, include its exact URL in `copyBrief.supportingSourceUrls`. If a trend is proposed in a calendar slot, put its exact source URL and any date/freshness limitation in that slot's `ownerNote`, so the later writer can recheck it. State missing publication or observation dates in `copyBrief.unknowns`; never invent them. If freshness or audience fit is unclear, call it an unverified inspiration rather than a current trend, or skip it and write an evergreen post. Never say a format is viral, guaranteed to perform, or likely to go viral.

Use a trend's structure as inspiration and make original business-specific copy and visuals. Do not copy another creator's image, video, caption, distinctive character, or unlicensed audio; do not impersonate a creator or use a person's likeness without permission. Use original assets or platform-cleared material. Preserve accessibility and the platform's format rules. Never use engagement bait to imitate a trend.

When useful, offer the owner a choice between a verified timely angle and a strong evergreen angle. Do not turn every slot into trend-chasing; let the growth goal and customer evidence set the mix.

## Quality check

- The voice fits this business's evidence and approved brand style, not a stereotype about its category.
- Humor is appropriate to the audience, topic, geography, and specific platform.
- Every claim remains supported; a joke does not turn an assumption into a fact.
- A trend is source-backed, recent enough to call current, and connected to a real customer insight.
- The business contributes an original, useful take rather than copying a viral asset.
- Platform versions are rewritten natively, not pasted across LinkedIn, Instagram, and Facebook.
- No virality or performance outcome is promised.
