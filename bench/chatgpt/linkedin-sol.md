## 1. A better project brief

**Angle + audience:** Practical intake workflow for agency owners and freelancers.

### Ready-to-publish post

Your website client and your branding client shouldn’t have to answer the same project questionnaire.

Here’s how I’d structure an agency intake form:

Start with what they need: a website, branding, or both.

Then branch.

Website work? Ask about the existing site, the pages they need, and what visitors should do.

Branding work? Ask about the audience, positioning, and where the identity will appear.

Ask everyone about the deadline, budget range, and who approves the work.

My rule: every question should help you scope the project or decide what happens next.

## 2. Before you buy more traffic

**Angle + audience:** A diagnostic process for founders and marketers collecting inbound enquiries.

### Ready-to-publish post

Before I’d spend more money sending people to an enquiry form, I’d fill it out on my phone.

Then I’d check:

• Is it clear what happens after I submit?
• Can I explain why every required question is necessary?
• Am I asking for details that belong in a later conversation?
• Does the form ask me questions that don’t apply to my enquiry?

That gives me a list of things to investigate.

Next, I’d look at where people actually leave.

Formkit includes question-level drop-off analytics on every plan. You can see views, starts, completions, completion time, and the question people quit on.

That won’t tell you why someone left. It gives you somewhere specific to investigate.

My approach: choose one question, make one change, and compare what happens next.

## 3. AI can draft it. You still publish it.

**Angle + audience:** Answer the control objection for business owners considering an AI form builder.

### Ready-to-publish post

I want AI to write the first draft of a form. I still want to decide what my business asks people.

Those fit together.

In Formkit, you can describe what you need and let AI draft the questions, pages, and logic.

Nothing goes live until you publish.

Here’s a brief I’d start with:

“Create a project enquiry form for a web design agency. Ask what the client needs, their budget range, target launch date, and who approves the project. Show website questions only to people who select website work.”

Then I’d review it:

Does each question belong?
Is the wording clear?
Does each branch lead to the right place?
Are we collecting anything we don’t need?

Formkit’s logic map and tester let you check the paths before publishing.

That’s where I’d put the human attention: deciding whether the form makes sense for the business and the person filling it out.
