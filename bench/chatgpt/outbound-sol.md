Research date: October 18, 2026  
Business: [Formkit](https://formkit.app/) · B2B SaaS  
Goal: More paying customers in the next 90 days  
Monthly ad budget: USD 600 ceiling, paid directly to ad platforms

## Recommendation and research limits

**Start with HUEMOR, Amply and Bop Design.** Their public offers provide specific outreach angles around lead qualification, form conversion and branded intake.

**Research limit:** These are 10 real companies with relevant workflows, but no public evidence establishes that any is actively shopping for Formkit. Treat these as qualified prospects—not confirmed buyers. Most timing signals are ongoing business activity; Orbit has a seasonal application signal.

Formkit's [website](https://formkit.app/) confirms conditional logic, question-level drop-off reporting, branding, and Pro features including custom domains and connections. Those are the capabilities used in the pitches below.

## Ideal customer profile

A US-based B2B web or marketing agency that collects project inquiries, offers several services, and could use branded forms with conditional questions and completion reporting.

- **Must-haves:** Public project-intake workflow, multiple service offerings, and a business owner responsible for lead handling or website performance.
- **Disqualifiers:** Form-builder competitors; sensitive medical or financial intake requiring unverified safeguards; no identifiable business use; recipients who have opted out.
- **Buying triggers:** Intake redesign, new service launch, active application cycle, form-conversion initiative, or a stated need to improve qualification.
- **Decision-maker:** Founder/business principal at a smaller agency; operations, business development or marketing leader at a larger agency.
- **Paid-plan hypothesis:** Custom domains and connections could justify Pro. A basic contact form alone may fit Formkit's free plan.

**Scores are analyst judgments, not purchase probabilities.** Fit considers workflow relevance and a plausible paid use case. Timing considers public evidence of a current reason to evaluate. An undated contact page earns a low timing score. Unknown size means no reliable headcount was established from the reviewed pages.

## Contact exclusions and sending rules

- No guessed email addresses, private addresses, purchased contact data or inferred email patterns are included.
- Do not pitch through client-proposal forms, job applications, support, press or privacy inboxes. These pages establish fit; they are not invitations for vendor submissions.
- For Clay and other international teams, establish the recipient's actual jurisdiction before sending; a US headquarters does not establish where an individual works.
- Suppress anyone who has opted out. A public address alone does not establish permission under every applicable law.
- US commercial email rules cover B2B outreach too. The sending system must supply accurate sender details, advertising identification, a valid postal address and an opt-out mechanism, and honor opt-outs. Those elements are deliberately outside the copy below, as requested. Source: [FTC CAN-SPAM guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).

## Cold email sequence

Use the company-specific first line below in place of `{{firstLine}}`. Send follow-ups only while there is no reply or opt-out. Days are **business days elapsed from the first email**. Greetings, footers and opt-out lines are omitted as requested.

### Day 0

**Subject: Project intake idea**

{{firstLine}}

I'm reaching out from Formkit with an idea for your project intake: ask which service someone needs, then show only the questions relevant to that work.

Formkit supports conditional questions, branded forms and reporting on where people stop answering. Custom domains and connections are available on Pro.

Would a short intake outline based on your public services be useful?

### Day 3

**Subject: Questions before the call**

One practical starting point: ask for the service needed, the main business goal and the target launch date. Then branch into service-specific questions.

That gives you a focused brief to review before deciding what to discuss on a call. Would you use that structure?

### Day 7

**Subject: Find the sticking point**

Another angle is testing the brief itself. Formkit reports the question where someone stopped, so you can investigate whether a budget, timeline or scope question needs changing.

You could evaluate one intake form before considering any wider change.

### Day 14

**Subject: A smaller starting point**

If replacing your main inquiry form is too much, a smaller test would be a discovery brief sent after someone books a call.

That leaves the initial contact step in place while you assess the questions and answers. Is that a more useful starting point?
