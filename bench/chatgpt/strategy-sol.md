# Formkit: 90-Day Growth Plan
Prepared October 18, 2026. Owner review required before publishing, sending, or spending.

- **Prioritize paid workflow adoption, agency distribution, and reusable templates.** Run only a small search advertising experiment.
- **90-day target: eight additional active paying companies**, after cancellations. This is a planning target, not a prediction.
- **Owner commitment: five hours a week**, with seven hours in week one.
- **Proposed advertising cap: $400 over 90 days.** Keep the rest unspent unless a channel proves its economics.

Formkit’s free plan includes unlimited forms and responses. Pro costs **$6 per seat monthly or $60 annually** and adds custom domains, branding removal, integrations, payments, and other capabilities. Business costs **$19 per seat monthly or $190 annually**. Attract businesses that need those paid capabilities—not simply more free form builders. [Source: Formkit pricing](https://formkit.app/pricing).

## Forecast assumptions

Formkit’s public pages were verified, but actual traffic, signup, conversion, and retention data were not available. **All volumes and scenario rates below are planning assumptions.** Replace them with actuals after week one.

The benchmark anchor is ChartMogul’s survey of 200 software products: **3–5% free-to-paid is “good” for conventional freemium; 8–12% is “great.”** Those figures measure conversion within **six months**, so they cannot be treated as 90-day predictions. This plan uses **2% / 4% / 8%** as low / likely / high assumptions for eligible cohorts recruited early enough to convert. Late signups need longer follow-up. [Source: ChartMogul Conversion Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/).

Fractional customer counts are expected values, not promises or literal outcomes. Low / likely / high cases are scenarios, not statistical confidence intervals.

## The growth portfolio

### 1. Bankable play: turn working forms into paid workflows

**Start here.** Help people already using Formkit complete a business task that requires Pro.

- **Audience:** free companies with a published form, real responses, and an expressed need for a custom domain, integration, payment collection, or another paid capability.
- **Offer:** a short setup guide for their specific task, with a shared weekly office hour for anyone who gets stuck.
- **Execution:** show the relevant upgrade at the moment of need. Someone trying to connect Google Sheets should see what Pro enables there—not a general list of features.
- **Product fit:** these upgrade reasons are documented on [Formkit’s pricing page](https://formkit.app/pricing).

#### 90-day forecast

For **100 existing eligible free companies**, the benchmark-based scenario is:

| Scenario | Companies offered relevant help | Assumed paid conversion | New paying companies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 100 | 2% | 2 |
| Likely | 100 | 4% | 4 |
| High | 100 | 8% | 8 |

**100 is a modeling unit, not a claim about Formkit’s user base.** With 25 eligible companies, divide the results by four. These are gross conversions, not measured incremental lift from the intervention.

#### Test, kill, scale

- Test one workflow with the first 25 eligible companies.
- **Revise** if fewer than five complete the setup step within 14 days; investigate friction before adding more messages.
- **Stop offering individual help** if nobody upgrades within 30 days and interviews reveal no paid need.
- **Expand** after two companies pay and successfully use the workflow. Turn repeated explanations into reusable guidance.
- Cap personal assistance at 15 minutes per company.

**Today:** count eligible free companies and identify the most common paid feature they attempted or asked about. If there are none, start this play with new accounts from plays two and three.

### 2. Creative, proven mechanic: the agency client-handoff kit

**Make Formkit part of a website project’s delivery.**

- **Audience:** independent web designers and small agencies delivering client websites.
- **Asset:** one kit containing a client onboarding form, creative brief, quote request, and a short guide to connecting a client’s domain and destination tool.
- **Distribution:** offer useful demonstrations to agency communities, design educators, and template creators. Recruit partners through existing relationships first.
- **Partner incentive:** give them a practical resource for their clients and credit their contribution. Do not build an affiliate system yet.

**Why this fits:** Formkit documents separate companies for clients and supplies onboarding, brief, and quote templates. Each client’s plan is separate; do not imply that one agency subscription covers every client. [Source: Formkit for agencies](https://formkit.app/use-cases/agencies).

**Why the mechanic is proven:** Typeform operates an agency partner program and publishes customer-created workflow templates. This validates the distribution approach—not Formkit’s expected results. [Agency program](https://www.typeform.com/partners/agencies) · [Customer templates](https://www.typeform.com/blog/introducing-new-typeform-customer-templates).

#### 90-day forecast

| Scenario | Assumed new free company accounts | Assumed paid conversion | Expected paying companies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 20 | 2% | 0.4 |
| Likely | 75 | 4% | 3 |
| High | 150 | 8% | 12 |

Paid-conversion assumptions use the ChartMogul anchor above. Account volumes are assumptions. The likely case requires approximately **five distributing partners bringing 15 accounts each**. Recruiting five partners is a test objective, not an established response rate.

#### Test, kill, scale

- Spend no more than four initial hours on the kit.
- Approach 15 carefully selected potential partners over three weeks.
- **Stop that partner segment** if nobody agrees to use or share it.
- **Revise the asset** if the first 20 referred accounts produce fewer than five published forms.
- **Scale** after two independently acquired companies pay and keep their workflow active for 30 days. Recruit similar partners using observed results.

**Today:** assemble the existing onboarding, creative brief, and quote templates into one guided agency resource. List five relevant people you already know.

### 3. Creative, proven mechanic: templates for a business deadline

**Sell the workflow needed for an upcoming event—not a generic form builder.**

- **Initial audience:** small training businesses preparing paid workshops for January.
- **Asset:** a workshop registration kit with registration questions, confirmation guidance, a payment setup walkthrough, and a response-management workflow.
- **Moment:** planning the next workshop creates an immediate reason to finish setup.
- **Distribution:** practical contributions in trainer communities, collaborations with workshop educators, and a searchable walkthrough on Formkit’s website.
- **Paid reason:** payment collection and integrations require Pro. Start with organizers who already use Stripe; Formkit documents payment collection through the customer’s own Stripe account. [Source: pricing](https://formkit.app/pricing).

**Why the mechanic is proven:** Tally documents community-shared templates as part of its growth approach and maintains a public template gallery. The workshop audience and January timing are hypotheses for Formkit. [Tally growth account](https://blog.tally.so/how-our-users-help-tally-grow/) · [Template gallery](https://tally.so/templates).

#### 90-day forecast

| Scenario | Assumed qualified visits | Assumed signup rate | Assumed paid conversion | Expected paying companies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 200 | 5% | 2% | 0.2 |
| Likely | 600 | 9% | 4% | 2.2 |
| High | 1,200 | 12% | 8% | 11.5 |

Formula: qualified visits × signup rate × free-to-paid rate.

ChartMogul reports a **9% visitor-to-signup rate across freemium products**; that anchors the likely signup assumption. It is not a template-page benchmark. Traffic volumes and the other scenario rates are assumptions. [Source: ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/).

#### Test, kill, scale

- Show the kit to five actual workshop organizers before completing it.
- **Stop the audience test** if none has an upcoming event or needs a paid capability.
- **Revise** after 100 qualified visits if fewer than five create accounts.
- **Scale distribution** after two unrelated organizers pay and publish working registration forms.
- Build a second kit only after the first generates paying customers.

**Today:** find five organizers publicly promoting upcoming workshops and inspect their registration process. Record the recurring setup problem before writing the guide.

### 4. Capped moonshot: narrow paid search

**Test whether highly specific paid intent can work at Formkit’s price.**

- One campaign, one landing page, one use case.
- Start with searches around a **form builder with a custom domain**, provided keyword research confirms relevant demand.
- Explain the paid capability and price directly.
- Exclude developer-library intent, jobs, tutorials unrelated to buying, and generic free-only searches.
- First test: **$100 maximum**.

Unbounce reports **5.1% median landing-page conversion for SaaS Google search traffic**. Its conversion actions vary; this is neither a paid-customer rate nor a verified Formkit signup rate. [Source: Unbounce SaaS benchmarks](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/).

#### Initial $100 test forecast

| Scenario | Assumed CPC | Clicks | Assumed signup rate | Assumed paid conversion | Expected paying companies |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | $5 | 20 | 3% | 2% | 0.01 |
| Likely | $2.50 | 40 | 5.1% | 4% | 0.08 |
| High | $1 | 100 | 8% | 8% | 0.64 |

Formula: budget ÷ CPC × signup rate × free-to-paid rate. CPC and scenario adjustments are assumptions; paid-conversion assumptions use the ChartMogul anchor.

**These economics are weak.** Even the high scenario implies approximately $156 in ad spend per expected customer. That is why this is a capped experiment.

#### Test, kill, scale

- Stop at $100 and let the cohort mature for 30 days.
- Stop earlier if irrelevant queries consume $25.
- **Do not renew** if there are no paying companies.
- Release further budget only if actual acquisition cost fits the payback rule below and acquired companies use the product.
- A single purchase is a signal to investigate, not proof of repeatability.

**Today:** inspect keyword demand and estimated CPC. Do not launch until signup-to-payment tracking works.

## Target and economics

**North Star:** active paying companies—companies with a current paid subscription and at least one genuine external form response in the trailing 30 days.

- **Day-90 target:** baseline + eight active paying companies, after cancellations.
- Count a company once, regardless of seats.
- Track new subscription MRR alongside company count.
- Exclude staff, test purchases, refunds, and credit-pack-only purchases.

The likely model produces approximately **nine gross paying companies** if play one has 100 eligible existing companies. Without that pool, the likely acquisition model is approximately **five**. Rebase the target after week one’s count; do not invent an installed base to preserve the target. Gross conversions do not guarantee the active-paying target because cancellations and inactivity reduce the result.

At one monthly Pro seat, eight companies represent only **$48 in added MRR** before cancellations, fees, and costs. That makes disciplined spending essential. The price comes from [Formkit](https://formkit.app/pricing); the revenue illustration assumes one seat per company.

**Proposed spending rule:** acquisition cost should fit within six months of observed monthly gross profit per acquired company. This is an owner-selected policy assumption. Calculate gross profit after payment fees, AI usage, and service costs; track owner time separately.

## Channels in priority order

| Priority | Channel | Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In-product guidance and existing users | Convert demonstrated paid needs |
| 2 | Agency and educator partnerships | Reach buyers through trusted implementers |
| 3 | Workflow templates, targeted communities, search content | Capture a specific business task |
| 4 | Narrow paid search | Test economics under a strict cap |

**Not doing during these 90 days:**

- **Broad paid social or display:** no validated acquisition economics.
- **High-volume cold email:** too much research, infrastructure, and follow-up for this price point and time budget.
- **Daily social publishing:** reuse demonstrations selectively; measure qualified visits.
- **A large SEO library:** finish two useful workflow assets first.
- **Lifetime deals:** subscription customers are the goal.
- **A major launch campaign:** defer until activation and paid conversion work.

## Phases and owner time

| Phase | Work | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–14 | Establish baseline, verify workflows, build agency kit, interview organizers | Confirm a real paid need |
| Days 15–30 | Distribute both assets, run office hours, launch the $100 search test if ready | Identify setup friction and early buyers |
| Days 31–60 | Follow cohorts, improve one bottleneck, repeat successful partner distribution | Stop weak audiences; release budget conditionally |
| Days 61–90 | Expand the strongest play, document repeatable setup, review retention | Choose the next quarter’s channel |

**Weekly five-hour allocation:** 90 minutes partnerships, 60 minutes workflow assets, 60 minutes user help, 60 minutes targeted distribution, and 30 minutes measurement.

## Measurement

Use company-level attribution and weekly cohorts.

- Record acquisition source, partner or asset ID, signup date, first publication, first external response, paid-feature intent, first payment, seats, plan, and cancellation.
- Preserve first-touch source; record later assistance separately.
- Attribute referred companies to their acquisition play. **Do not count them again under activation.**
- Review signup → published form → external response → paid → active paid.
- Compare cohorts after the same number of days. Report late cohorts as immature.
- Track advertising CAC, recurring revenue, gross profit, refunds, and owner minutes per acquired company.
- Add one signup question asking where the person heard about Formkit to supplement attribution.

**At day 30, decide what to stop. At day 60, decide what deserves repetition. At day 90, judge the plan by active paying companies and their economics.**
