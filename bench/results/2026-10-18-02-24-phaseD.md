## Losses to read (first judge, reasons)

- **plausible/facebook v22>v0f**: B turns each fact into a reader benefit. It names the pain (clicking 'Accept all'), explains why a small script matters, and says what you still get. A states the fact and stops, so its posts are too thin to persuade in a Facebook feed. / B's third post fits the transparent brand voice very well. It openly says Plausible isn't for deep custom reports, which is supported by the site's own 'not right for every use case' line. That builds trust and qualifies buyers. / B's call to action is a free trial, which matches the goal of more paying customers. A always sends people to the live demo, a softer step that is one click further from conversion.
- **tartine/facebook v22>v0f**: The goal is more holiday pastry pre-orders and catering. B leads with a holiday pre-order post and invites larger gatherings to get in touch. A never mentions holiday pastry pre-orders or catering. It covers only special cakes and shipping. / A repeats '600 Guerrero St. pickup only' across all three posts and both first comments. That wastes slots in a 60-day push. B's three posts each do a separate job: holiday pre-orders, cake lead time, and shipping as gifts. / B's hooks are more native to Facebook ('Someone on your list misses this bakery.'). A's hooks read like policy notices.
- **finch/facebook v22>v0f**: B is more native to Facebook. It has a warm, supportive voice, a clear 'start small' angle aimed at the high-confidence overwhelmed audience, real engagement questions, and useful first comments that add value. / A is cleaner on truth, but its body copy is so thin and clipped ('Grey morning, kettle still boiling.') that it reads like ad fragments rather than page posts. It is unlikely to spark comments or carry the brand voice. / A's production briefs are excellent: real screen recordings, test accounts, a reminder to verify figures, safe zones. That is valuable to a non-expert owner.
