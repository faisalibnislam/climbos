## Stop writing the same form twice

**Channel:** linkedin

**Title:** Stop writing the same form twice

**Body:** Drafting a form in ChatGPT, then rebuilding it field by field in a builder, is the same job done twice.

The draft in the chat is usually the better thinking. It's the conversation where you worked out who fills the form in, what you need from them and what you'll do with each answer. Retyping it into a separate tool adds nothing, except a chance to drop or change a question on the way.

So Formkit works inside Claude and ChatGPT: you build and publish the form in the same conversation where you planned it. It works on every plan, including the free one, which has unlimited forms, responses and members.

The catch is that the form is only as good as the description you give the assistant. Say who will fill it in, which answers are required, and what decision each answer feeds. A question that doesn't change what you do next probably doesn't belong on the form.

The document below shows how to write that description, using a client intake form as the example.

## Find the question where people quit

**Channel:** linkedin

**Title:** Find the question where people quit

**Body:** Your form only shows you the people who finished it. The ones who quit halfway leave no trace.

That hides the most useful thing a form can tell you: which question made people stop. When an enquiry form gets fewer replies than you expected, the usual instinct is to cut questions at random or rewrite the whole thing. Both are guesses.

The better move is to measure, question by question, how many people reached each one and how many carried on. The biggest gap between two questions points at the one to change. Candidates worth checking first: a budget question asked before you've explained anything, a required phone number, a file upload that's awkward to do on a phone.

Formkit shows drop-off by question and keeps partial responses on every plan, including the free one. So you change that one question, leave the rest alone, and see whether the drop at that point shrinks.

If you have a form live right now, pick the question you'd bet loses the most people. Then check whether your current tool can prove it.

Which question on your forms do people quit at most?
