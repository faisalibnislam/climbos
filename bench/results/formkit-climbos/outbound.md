**Must Haves**

- Size 1–15 people. Source: the studio's about or team page, or job ads. Why: the owner runs intake, and Pro at $6/seat stays cheap.

- A public inquiry or intake form is the required first step for new clients. Source: the studio's contact or inquiry page. Why: the form is part of how clients see them, which is Formkit's core angle.

- Trades in the US or is a UK limited company. Canada or Australia only with a verbatim published work email. Source: site footer, contact page, Companies House. Why: this decides whether email is allowed at all.

- Nice-to-have: a rival form tool confirmed (Typeform or Google Forms link in the form). Source: the form's link or embed destination. Why: a switch target with a known gap.

**Disqualifiers**

- Germany or Austria. UK sole traders or partnerships. Canada or Australia with no verbatim published email. Source: footer, registry.

- Template sellers, form-tool partners or agencies tied to a platform ecosystem such as HubSpot partners. Source: product and partner pages. These are partnership pitches, not sales.

- Universities, student organisations or teams over 50 people. Source: about page, domain (.edu).

- Franchise units where head office picks the tools. Source: footer or 'locations' page.

- Existing Formkit customers, competitors or anyone on a suppression list. Source: owner's customer list and the suppression list.

**Triggers**

- New service line or new inquiry form added. Source: the studio's dated news or blog page.

- Website relaunch or new studio launch. Source: dated announcement post, or Wayback Machine snapshots of the inquiry page.

- Hiring that sends applicants to a basic form. Source: the careers page or public job boards, with the posting date.

- New paid workshop or event using a basic registration form. Source: the event page or an Eventbrite listing, with the event date.

- Public reviews complaining of slow replies or replies landing in spam. Source: dated Google reviews, or the studio's own page.
