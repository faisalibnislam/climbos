Read-only review. No repo files edited. Everything below is my own paraphrase. Short phrases only, no copied passages.

## 0. Method and indexing

- EPUB: `docs/reference-books/Testing_Business_Ideas_-_Alexander_Osterwalder.epub`. Internal files: `index_split_000..003.html` (about 15k elements each). `index_split_004/005` and `titlepage.xhtml` are stubs.
- `epub_blocks.py` parsed all four files as XML. Indexes are zero-based element indexes, not line numbers. Gaps in the numbering confirm this. So the pack's "block" numbers use the same indexing as my dumps.
- I dumped each split to scratch (`review/bk04_s0..3.txt`) and flattened it into chunks.
- Read in full:
  - s0 1400–2760: Hypothesize, Types of hypotheses, Assumptions Mapping, Test Card, Evidence/Insight/Confidence, Decide.
  - s0 3420–5131: experiment flow, Ethics heading, Experiment Guidelines, Select an Experiment, sequences, Discovery index, Customer Interview card.
  - s1, all of it: the cards from Customer Interview through Storyboard.
  - s2, all of it: the cards from Data Sheet through Split Test, plus the Validation index.
  - s3 0–2470: the cards from Split Test through Extreme Programming Spike, plus Avoid Experiment Pitfalls.
- **Full-text re-check with `epub_full.py`:**
  - The coordinator warned that `epub_blocks.py` drops text after inline formatting. I re-dumped all four splits with `epub_full.py` (same numbering) into `review/bk04f_s0..3.txt` and diffed them block by block over every range I used (`review/diff_bk04.txt`).
  - Most recovered text was numbered list labels, wrapper divs repeating a child block, and column fragments I already had from neighbouring blocks.
  - One substantive recovery: the **Ethics in Experimentation** paragraph (s0 3540), previously cut to one sentence. It is now readable and folded in below.
  - Smaller recoveries: the hypothesis section warns that writing only "We believe…" statements can lead to confirmation bias (s0 1454); the definition of a well-formed hypothesis (s0 1481); Pretend to Own credits Alberto Savoia's *The Right It* (s2 1318); validation-survey timing caveats (s3 501–577).
  - None of these changes a locator verdict or a catalog entry. Locator ranges L01–L04 lost only list labels such as "1. Identify the Hypotheses" and "1. Perservere / 2. Pivot / 3. Kill", which supports them further.
- Skimmed or not read: s0 0–1400 (front matter, Design section, canvases), s0 2760–3420 (Manage section: team, ceremonies, flow boards), s3 2470–end (Lead/Organize sections, index, credits).

## 2. GAPS: the experiment library (the most important finding)

The book has **44 experiments**: 29 discovery, 15 validation. The section index is at s0 4925–4998 (discovery) and s2 2316–2358 (validation). I found and read all 44: 41 full cards plus 3 variations (Partner & Supplier Interviews, Expert Stakeholder Interviews, 404 Test).

**Card layout:** title and one-line definition; "ideal for"; "not ideal for" on some cards; capabilities; Prepare/Execute/Analyze steps; DETAILS (Cost, Setup Time, Run Time, Evidence Strength with named metrics, Capabilities, Requirements); Before/After pairings; a case study on some cards.

**Legibility:**
- The graphical rating scales for COST, SETUP TIME, RUN TIME and EVIDENCE STRENGTH are **not legible**. Only the labels survive.
- The emphasized letter in each card's "DESIRABILITY · FEASIBILITY · VIABILITY" strip is **not legible**. Every card shows all three words.
- The scatter charts (s0 3797–4320: cost vs evidence, "AVG. TIME" numbers) are fragmented. Values can't be matched to experiments.
- **The DETAILS prose is legible.** It describes cost, time and evidence in words, sometimes with durations. Fields marked `_book` below quote that prose in paraphrase, never an icon.
- `hypothesis_type` is my reading of the card's own text ("ideal for", evidence metrics, Analyze step). Wherever the strip can't be read, I say so.
- Header oddity: the five interaction-prototype cards (Clickable, Single Feature MVP, Mash-Up, Concierge, Life-Sized) are headed "DISCOVERY / INTERACTION PROTOTYPE", but the validation index lists them under Validation. I follow the index.

**Selection guidance (s0 3597–4323), paraphrased:**
- Choose by three things: hypothesis type, how much evidence exists already, and time until the next decision or until cash runs out.
- Rules of thumb:
  - go cheap and fast early;
  - strengthen evidence with several experiments per hypothesis;
  - pick the strongest experiment your constraints allow;
  - reduce uncertainty before building.
- The cost legend runs in bands from under $500 to $20k+. The time legend runs from hours to 3+ months.
- Experiment Guidelines template (s0 3548–3572): segment, number of participants, dates, the "information currency" collected, branding, financial exposure, and how to switch the test off.
- Sequences (s0 4361–4871) are given for B2B hardware/software/services, B2C hardware/software/services, B2B2C and highly regulated businesses. The sequence diagrams are only partly legible.
- Pitfalls (s3 2232–2464): time trap, analysis paralysis, incomparable data, weak data (measuring only what people say), confirmation bias, too few experiments, failure to learn and adapt, outsourcing testing.

### 3 evaluation case ideas
1. **EV-BK04-A, low-traffic landing page:**
   - Inputs: local physio, about 15 site visitors a day, owner asks for a landing-page test of a new package within 7 days.
   - Expected behaviors:
     - flag that traffic falls far short of what the book's card assumes;
     - propose brochure handouts at the clinic, concierge delivery to 5 existing patients, or a presale to the patient list;
     - if a landing page still runs, use a long window and label results inconclusive below a pre-set visitor count.
   - Hard fails: calling a 2-of-15 signup result "validated", or setting a 10–15% target as the pass rule without stating the sample.
2. **EV-BK04-B, evidence ladder:**
   - Inputs: 12 interviews with praise, 40 email signups, 0 payments; owner wants to hire staff.
   - Expected behaviors:
     - rate confidence as low to moderate (say-data plus a small-investment action);
     - recommend a call-to-action test with real commitment (presale, LOI if B2B, mock sale with immediate disclosure) before an irreversible hire;
     - keep the interview insights for copy.
   - Hard fail: treating signups as purchases.
3. **EV-BK04-C, feature stub ethics and fit:**
   - Inputs: SaaS with 30 daily users wants a 404 test on a core billing feature for 2 weeks.
   - Expected behaviors:
     - refuse the 404 test: the feature is mission-critical, and the book caps 404 tests at hours and Feature Stubs at 1–3 days;
     - suggest Buy a Feature with existing customers or a short Feature Stub on a non-critical add-on, with a working toggle;
     - note that the low user count limits what clicks can show.
   - Hard fail: running the test for 2 weeks, or with no switch-off.

## 3. Top insights for the engine
1. **Use requirements as hard gates.** Each card's Requirements and "not ideal for" text maps directly to machine checks:
   - traffic: landing page about 100 uniques/day, split test needs significant traffic, feature stub needs daily active users;
   - warm leads for an LOI;
   - an existing customer base for support analysis and validation surveys;
   - ability to fulfil before a presale;
   - consent before A Day in the Life.
2. **Use the evidence gradient per metric.** Cards rank their own metrics:
   - weakest: quotes, feature requests, engagement, email;
   - stronger: clicks, then near-miss feedback, workarounds and task behavior;
   - strongest: payment info, purchases, pledges, delivery cost and time.
   Store evidence at metric level, not experiment level.
3. **Sequence and pair experiments.** Every card lists before/after pairings, and the book gives sequences by business type. The engine should propose a path, for example interview → brochure → concierge → presale, rather than a single test. The book's confidence scale needs several experiments that include a call-to-action test.
4. **Low-traffic small businesses need a different default set.**
   - Good fits: interviews, sales/support analysis, discussion forums, brochure, concierge, LOI (B2B), presale or mock sale to a warm list, pop-up (local B2C).
   - Use only with enough volume: landing pages, ads, split tests, feature stubs, explainer videos.
5. **Encode stop and switch-off rules plus the ethics guardrails.** The book's own ethics paragraph (s0 3540) rejects experiments that become scams or vaporware-style hype. That gives a source anchor for refusing deceptive tests.
   - Time caps: 404 test hours, feature stub 1–3 days, spike time-boxed.
   - Spend: pause ads on high spend with very low CTR.
   - Guidelines template: financial exposure and switch-off mechanism.
   - Landing pages: no fake testimonials or sold-out labels.
   - Boomerang: never rebrand a competitor's product.
   - Wizard of Oz: automation threshold. ClimbOS's own policy should require disclosure, which goes beyond the book.
   - The book's numeric targets (15%, 10–15%, 40%, 80%/98%) are source heuristics. Show them as reference points, not pass/fail rules. The split-test confidence advice in particular should defer to BK06.
