# Creative strategy evaluation

## Purpose

Check whether the workflow produces useful, distinctive options without confusing originality with evidence, fit, or permission to execute. These fixtures are hypothetical, not claims about customer outcomes.

## Synthetic fixtures

1. A small design studio with strong craft and little paid-media budget.
2. A local repair business with a limited service radius and seasonal demand.
3. A niche B2B software company with a long evaluation cycle and no verified conversion baseline.
4. A small repeat-purchase consumer brand with a limited production run.
5. A community project with high trust requirements and volunteer capacity limits.

For every growth-strategy fixture, check that grounded, adjacent, and bold options are present. The bold option may have no precedent and low evidence, business fit, or feasibility; confirm it remains visible, uses the business context without inventing customer facts, separates all dimensions, and gets a reversible test. Confirm precedents are actually retrieved and transfer conditions/differences are explained; absence of a close case must not block an original idea. Confirm costs and dependencies are visible, unsupported numerical lift is absent, and execution remains approval-gated.

For retrieval review, provide explicit and missing audience, goal, constraint, scale, geography, and business-model signals in the fixture. Check that a match explains which available dimensions contributed, that a location or industry name alone cannot retrieve a weak analogy, and that missing context remains unknown rather than being inferred from currency or domain. The runtime uses a mechanism-match gate and bounded retrieval; its internal ranking score is not shown to owners as a quality or success score.

## Human review rubric

Rate each dimension from 1 to 5 and retain the individual ratings; do not calculate a single opaque quality score.

| Dimension | Review question |
|---|---|
| Novelty | Does the idea differ meaningfully from generic channel advice without relying on shock value? |
| Originality | Is at least one option surprising or category-challenging in its mechanism, rather than a familiar tactic with a new name? |
| Customer relevance | Is the customer tension supported by the brief or explicitly unknown? |
| Strategic coherence | Does the mechanism plausibly connect the action to the stated business goal? |
| Transfer quality | Are precedent conditions and differences explained without copying expression or outcomes? |
| Evidence honesty | Are facts, reports, sources, inferences, assumptions, and speculation distinct? |
| Feasibility | Are capacity, cost, dependencies, risks, and the smallest test concrete? |
| Test quality | Does the bold idea have clear evidence that would support continuing, changing, or stopping it? |
| Decision usefulness | Are success, guardrail, stop, and revision conditions measurable and relevant? |

Reject outputs that fabricate demand or expected lift, treat case studies as causal proof, hide operating cost, recommend deceptive or unlawful conduct, or turn an exploratory idea into an authorized external action. Compare output versions and record failure examples; an LLM judge alone is not validation.

Never reject a bold option solely for low evidence, low feasibility, or lack of a precedent. Review those as separate trade-offs; assess whether the experiment is safe and informative without requiring the idea itself to be conventional.
