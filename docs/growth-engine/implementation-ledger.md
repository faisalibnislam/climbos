## Resumption note (2026-10-24, engine and learning work)

The first 2026-10-24 strategy improvement now passes the computed business diagnosis into channel-fit research and final channel ranking. Only a `supported` diagnosis changes the shortlist: the existing channel journey-stage taxonomy brings channels relevant to demand, activation, conversion, retention, delivery or economics ahead of channels that only score well on generic priors. The score itself remains the base channel score; the scorecard labels diagnosis fit, and uncertain diagnoses preserve the old ordering. Deterministic unit coverage verifies the retention case and unchanged behavior without focus. This is still a stage-taxonomy proxy, not live-model or business-outcome validation.

Issue 07 now has a structured owner-reported lifecycle stage (idea validation, pre-launch, early traction, growth, maturity, or possible decline). A missing stage stays unknown; website copy cannot set or infer it. Strategy context gives stage-specific planning guidance while labeling the stage as owner-reported, and the versioned plan snapshot captures it. A changed stage now marks the saved plan stale and blocks approval or starting a play; saving the same stage again does not. Tenant-scoped business editing and the onboarding read model expose the field for a later UI. This is a usable engine input, not an independent classification; frontend controls and stage-change outcome validation remain.

Issue 05 now asks research for structured competitor evidence across buyer, positioning, offer, pricing, proof, distribution, and experience. Each finding carries the exact page read and a separate cautious business implication; evidence URLs are rejected unless present in the current run's supplied sources. The optional records persist with business insights so later plans can use them. This makes comparisons more inspectable, but citations do not prove the page's claim, and the implication remains an inference rather than observed customer preference or competitive advantage.

Downstream social, ad, email, SEO and specialist content tasks now receive the saved competitor comparisons alongside customer language and positioning. Business research is passed as a bounded untrusted context block. Prompts distinguish competitor-published claims from independent evidence and prohibit copying, naming competitors, asserting superiority, or presenting their prices/features as current unless the owner specifically requests a sourced comparison. This strengthens differentiation context without treating competitor marketing as truth.

REQ-COMP-07 now has a scheduled refresh path: business name/site/type changes and owner edits to product, audience, pricing, offer, or competitor facts move research due time forward; otherwise the configurable default is 90 days (owner/admin may choose 0, 30, 60, 90, or 180 days). A daily bounded due queue starts the existing metered research job, respects credit availability and workspace approval thresholds, retries deferred work weekly, and skips unchanged businesses until due. A progressive cursor backfill initializes schedules for legacy businesses without scanning the full table in one run. This does not run against production here; scheduled live behavior and refresh quality have not been evaluated.

Channel recommendations now also receive the existing billing-based UTM source signal as a bounded source-by-source summary: payment-event count, identified customer count, first recorded payer count, net billing amount, and recorded refunds. Matching refunds follow their order; customer IDs are not exposed. The first-payer count uses the earliest positive payment in the loaded source-scoped ClimbOS billing history, so it distinguishes observed new payers from renewals while remaining incomplete if earlier history was never imported or a read cap is reached. An owner-facing import mutation accepts daily Google Ads and Meta Ads results in whole currency units, validates the period and amounts, replaces the same platform/date/currency row, and stores owner-reported provenance. When a recognized paid UTM source and its platform spend are available in the same currency and date window, ClimbOS computes tracked media ROAS and media spend per first recorded payer. If the owner provides a valid gross margin, a deterministic helper computes the media-only break-even ROAS used for a cautious screen; it excludes labor, fulfillment, tools, and other costs. The agent prompt and strategy snapshot preserve the window, partial-read state, and limits: UTM paths are descriptive last-touch associations, ROAS is not incremental lift or full ROI, the payer-cost proxy is not CAC, missing tags do not prove a channel had no impact, and none of these values alone establish profitability. Spend for other platforms, qualification-to-margin joins, variable delivery cost, and causal validation remain unavailable.

Issue 27 / Issue 127 now gets a deterministic, source-deduplicated trailing-365-day billing summary of gross payments, recorded refunds, net payments, and a same-window refund-amount share when that ratio is meaningful. Strategy agents receive it with explicit limits; it is also hashed into plan snapshot v2. If refunds exceed same-window gross (for example, because they relate to earlier purchases), or there is no gross denominator, no percentage is produced. The summary is not a customer refund probability, does not infer causes or refund reasons, and is marked incomplete when the shared 10,000-event read cap is reached. This makes refund exposure visible to recommendations but does not use it as a cohort forecast adjustment or separately itemize fulfillment costs.

Issue 04 / Issue 27 qualification now has an optional owner-entered count and required short operational definition in the funnel. Counts must nest (`leads ≥ qualified leads ≥ first value ≥ paid customers`); when qualification is present, activation is measured from that qualified group. Diagnosis reports its Beta range and owner definition without assigning a benchmark verdict, includes the step in downstream leverage arithmetic, and passes its rate to experiment sizing. The play-writing instructions forbid calling an owner-defined rate weak without an applicable business target or customer/contribution comparison. This is a measurement signal, not proof of audience fit; qualification joined to channel and eventual contribution remains to be implemented.

Since the original S4 note, owner economics now recalculate forecasts, portfolio selection and goal gap on plan reads; approval and play launch are blocked if the inputs differ from the plan snapshot. Outreach learning now separates any replies from owner-reported reply disposition, and sparse first-party rates remain visible with a conservative planning bound. Generic all-business rate pools have been removed. These changes are pushed on `growth-engine-v2`; they are not deployed or live-model validated.

The attached improvement brief's Issue 21 now has canonical display grouping for same-category, same-review-state facts with equivalent normalized meaning; source records remain attached to the display row and reviewing the canonical fact updates the whole exact group. Matching is deliberately high precision (normalized terms, currency/value, entity words and negation), not embedding-based semantic deduplication. Issue 22's price/trial/capability conflict detector compares pending researched facts with confirmed/edited facts and returns each fact's review state and source evidence. It preserves explicit billing units, recognizes direct and passive feature-availability wording, and places plans with detected material conflicts in `needs_input`; approval is blocked until the owner resolves the facts and refreshes the plan. Detection remains lexical and limited to clearly structured pricing, trial and capability statements.

Issues 26–29: analytics no longer counts every subscription renewal as a new customer. “First recorded paying customers” counts each identified customer once, on their earliest positive non-refund payment found in the imported history; rows without customer IDs are excluded, and bounded-history calculations are marked partial. This measures first recorded payment, not causal campaign attribution or proof of retained profitability. The S3 forecast now accepts optional reach-ramp weeks and paid-conversion lag days. It integrates a linear ramp, excludes conversions that would close after the forecast horizon, applies the same timing to baseline and intervention, and still charges the full planned-period costs. Each forecasted play also carries structured uncertainty metadata distinguishing a single scenario from input-range scenarios, listing varied drivers, marking statistical intervals as unestimated, naming timing defaults and listing material omissions. These are owner/model scenario inputs, not empirically calibrated timing estimates; seasonality and time-varying rates remain unmodeled.

Issues 361/371/372 now have a tenant-scoped owner-observation foundation. A user can record an answer sample with provider, query, optional location, check time, mention/sentiment labels, citation URLs, competitor mentions, and a reviewable excerpt. The report groups comparable provider/query/location samples and separately reports a panel summary for observed first-party citations and business/competitor mention answers. Failed and no-answer checks stay visible but outside answer denominators; unknown business-mention labels exclude the whole answer from the relative-mention denominator. Outputs label the evidence `owner_observed` and explain equal weighting, incomplete competitor labels, repeated-sample dependence, and the lack of causal business-impact evidence. Wilson intervals are explicitly labeled as relying on independent-observation assumptions; repeated prompts and platform changes can violate them, so they are not calibrated uncertainty for a complete provider or market distribution. This is not direct provider monitoring, a fixed query panel, an AI market-share estimate, a visibility-gap diagnosis, or proof that optimization changed business outcomes. Fixture coverage: 8 focused tests; full suite/typecheck/lint were run for the preceding commit, with focused tests/typecheck/lint run for this panel-summary change.

The observation system now also supports tenant-owned immutable query panels (up to 100 provider/question/location combinations). Panel queries are normalized and duplicate tuples are rejected; observations must match the selected saved panel query, provider and location exactly. Panel reports show per-question observation counts and retain questions with no observations, making coverage gaps explicit. Owners create a new panel when they need a changed query set. This establishes repeatable query membership; controlled experiments and business-outcome linkage remain unbuilt. Coverage includes tenant isolation, exact-match validation, duplicate rejection, empty-query visibility and adjacent-window comparison; 11 focused tests pass.

Panel and ungrouped reports can now compare equal-length adjacent periods on the same provider/query/location dimensions. Per-query output shows both samples and rates plus descriptive absolute rate changes; absent or unknown denominators remain null. The API labels the comparison as non-causal and includes window boundaries and per-period truncation. This is a trend comparison, not a controlled AEO experiment: content intervention/version, hypothesis, outcome metric, query demand weighting, and business-impact linkage are not captured. Regression coverage uses a pre-period and post-period fixture to confirm comparable query matching and no fabricated delta for an unsampled query.

Issue 23 now separates facts past their category review interval from the current verified fact index used by agents. The previous statements remain available in a bounded, untrusted recheck block; they cannot be cited as confirmed through structured fact references. Age alone remains a review signal, not proof a statement is false.

Issue 24 now has shared research and critic instructions to assess source role, publication/measurement date, method, population, fit, and independent origins; planning prompts carry those limits into recommendations. The engine does not assign a numeric reputation score or independently validate a publisher's credibility, and this assessment remains model-reviewed rather than a deterministic source registry.

Business-insight evidence links are now checked against the exact site pages and research results read for that run. A competitor, alternative, customer quote, objection, or gap citing an unseen URL fails the citation gate. This verifies source presence only; it does not prove that a page supports the associated claim.

The attached improvement brief's Issue 57 now has a tenant-scoped, trailing-365-day customer-concentration signal in agent context, computed from deduplicated non-demo billing events in the business currency. It reports top-one/top-three shares, identified-revenue coverage, and a bounded-read warning without exposing customer identifiers. Customer and order IDs are scoped to each billing source to avoid false cross-provider matches, so the same real person may count more than once. This is descriptive billing concentration, not contractual exposure, a forecast, causal attribution, or evidence that diversification is feasible; materiality still needs business context and owner judgment.

The same source-scoped identity rule now applies to Issue 26/29's first-recorded-paying-customer metric. Its history lookup uses a new `(org, source, customer, occurredAt)` index so an equal ID from a different billing provider cannot make a paying customer disappear from the count. Refund-only activity no longer makes the metric claim it has paying-customer data. Cross-provider identity resolution remains unavailable and is disclosed in the metric definition.

Monthly cohort size, repeat activity, and cohort revenue now use the same source-scoped customer identity. Equal provider IDs no longer merge unrelated people into one retention history; cross-provider person resolution remains unverified.

Issue 127's cohort revenue now subtracts separate refund events from the matching source-scoped customer/order cohort and month, while refunds do not count as retained payment activity. Unmatched refunds remain outside customer cohorts rather than being guessed onto a customer.

Billing attribution also scopes customer touches and refund/order matching by source. New revenue imports and demo touches store their source; a legacy touch without source is used only if the customer reference appears in one source in the current revenue set. When that legacy identity is ambiguous, the event remains unattributed rather than receiving another provider's channel credit.

Issue 58 now receives a bounded 365-day tagged-source concentration summary in agent context when non-demo billing events include recognized UTM platforms. It reports top-one/top-three share among positive net revenue assigned to recognized tags and the number of tagged conversions, with explicit association and completeness limits. This cannot detect untagged or offline dependency, policy exposure, or the full platform mix; it must not be treated as proof of causation or as a standalone diversification recommendation.

Issue 25 now retains all missing funnel inputs in the computed diagnosis while selecting one next measurement for the agent brief: first the customer outcome, then the specific count or clarification that separates the remaining explanations. Supported diagnoses do not generate follow-up questions. This reduces owner burden without pretending the other unknowns are resolved; the saved diagnosis still carries the full missing list. The shared diagnostic now rejects fractional or unsafe funnel counts, churn outside 0–100%, and invalid sales-cycle lengths before computing any posterior readings. Contradictions return no derived readings, so malformed data cannot become rounded evidence in the agent brief.

Issue 44 now runs ClimbOS's deterministic risky-claim screen across title, headline, body, call to action, hashtags, and alt text both when an artifact is created and when it is edited. The screen catches guarantees, unsupported scale/superlative language, numeric performance promises, selected absolute safety claims, and medical wording. A user correction can clear a flag by removing the risky wording; making an edit by itself no longer clears it. The screen is lexical and bounded to known English risk patterns, not semantic substantiation or legal review.

Issue 05's competitor watch now retains the exact homepage/pricing page URLs used for each observed change, bounded removed/added text excerpts, and observation time in competitor overview and weekly report data. Internal watch reads and writes verify the organization ID before returning or changing competitor data. This makes the model's summary traceable to the observed competitor pages and changed text; it still does not prove a competitor's public claims or identify the reason customers choose them.

REQ-COMP-05 now strips common analytics query parameters and standalone navigation boilerplate before competitor page hashing and line diffs. Regression coverage confirms changing UTM tags or menu labels does not create a competitor-change event. This remains a conservative lexical filter; it does not identify every template, personalization, or dynamically rendered page change.

S7 now filters sourced competitor watch candidates before returning or seeding them: it excludes the owner's own domain and known directory/app-store hosts, collapses duplicate vendor hosts, requires a source URL, and supplies a canonical HTTPS host. After business insights are saved, a bounded background job checks direct-candidate homepages through the existing SSRF-safe reader and seeds only identity-matched sites up to the active free or paid watch allowance. Removed sites are retained as exclusions so a refresh cannot silently add them again; the owner can explicitly re-add one. Each auto-seeded watch records its discovery source. This remains limited to insight-run candidates in research order; there is no broad independent discovery or measured relevance ranking.

The competitor watcher now also checks the fetched homepage's final domain against the owner's watched domain. HTTPS/HTTP, www and subdomain changes remain one site; a redirect to a different domain is saved as a visible watch error and does not update the prior baseline or trigger a summary. This protects against silently changing the identity being monitored; legitimate company domain migrations require the owner to update the watch.

Issue 08's engine-side capacity guard now blocks net-new acquisition channels when the owner has confirmed zero monthly delivery capacity, while retaining existing/returning-customer work. Paid search and social are also blocked before ranking when the owner explicitly confirms conversion tracking is absent; inferred or unknown tracking does not trigger a hard block. The gate is applied in code to the plan scorecard and Acquisition Intelligence read, with focused tests. This does not yet model staffing, inventory, support load, or remaining capacity after current commitments, so it is not a complete fulfillment-capacity model.

Issue 08's initial 80% diagnosis watch signal has been superseded. Review found that the owner capacity field already means additional monthly capacity, so comparing it with customers already acquired mixed different quantities. The planner and acquisition gate use the field as a limit on incremental customers; diagnosis now reports the stated remaining capacity as context and supports a delivery constraint only when the owner reports zero additional capacity. It does not infer utilization from funnel volume.

Issue 10/25's funnel diagnosis rejects an explicitly supplied measurement period that is zero, negative, or non-finite instead of silently interpreting it as 30 days. It now applies the same fail-closed rule to fractional/unsafe counts, invalid churn, and invalid sales-cycle lengths, returning a conflicting-input state without derived readings. Omitted periods still use the documented 30-day default.

REQ-EVID-04 now computes a separate financial evidence status for each play's fully loaded cost/payback comparison and persists it as code-generated metadata. Assumptions, planned values, unchecked claims and unlabeled inputs keep the arithmetic visible but downgrade a money-loss lint from error to warning; the plan labels the comparison conditional or unlabeled, and portfolio exclusions use scenario language. A hard loss error is reserved for inputs with owner-confirmation or source-review stamps. This is an input-check status, not proof of source relevance or business fit; owner confirmation staleness is not modeled, and no result is calibrated to observed outcomes.

REQ-STRAT-02's measurable option comparison now uses the same seeded joint-draw mean as portfolio ranking for expected contribution. Delivery-capacity feasibility uses P90 customers over the shared horizon, so a high-upside range cannot pass capacity solely because its middle scenario fits. The result remains a reviewer warning because qualitative trade-offs can outweigh simplified scenario arithmetic.

REQ-OUT-04 now checks structured decision constraints against the selected plays. When the owner's funnel diagnosis is measured and supported, a conflicting model-selected diagnosis fails lint; when the constraint is a strategic hypothesis, a missing matching play is surfaced as a warning. The check uses deterministic play-stage keywords, so semantic fit still needs reviewer evaluation.

The shared agent and critic contracts now preserve the distinction between published company claims, owner reports, observed behaviour, commitments and measurements. They no longer treat website copy or an owner summary as independent proof of customer demand, product performance, willingness to pay or business viability, and they prohibit a pivot recommendation based only on a weak webpage. Numbers must be supplied, calculated from supplied inputs or sourced. This advances Issues 01 and 10 as a prompt-level guardrail; semantic enforcement and live-model behavior remain unverified.
