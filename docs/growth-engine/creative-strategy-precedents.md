# Creative strategy precedents

Last reviewed: 25 October 2026. This is a curated retrieval set for strategy exploration, not a forecast database or universal playbook.

## Verified coverage

The current runtime library contains **4 editorially reviewed cases** (`CP-001`–`CP-004`). The requested target is 100 useful cases, leaving **96 cases unverified and not yet included**. The general growth case collection is separate and is not counted as creative-strategy coverage.

Each case records its reported choice and mechanism, evidence class, causal limits, transfer conditions and risks, a small-business adaptation, and a reversible test. Retrieval requires lexical overlap with both a mechanism and a transfer condition; it returns at most three cases and never treats a precedent as evidence about the target business.

The sources cover Airbnb's election cereal boxes, Patagonia's "Don't Buy This Jacket" campaign, Nike's waffle-sole experiment, and Mario's pixel-constrained character design. Outcomes retain the source's status: retrospective before/after reports are not causal estimates. See `shared/knowledge/creativePrecedents.ts` for claim-level source records.

## Runtime use and editorial additions

Creative strategy is attached to the existing growth-plan task. A structured review records the convention being challenged and its basis, transfer logic and differences, assumptions, distinct ratings, a reversible test, success signal, guardrail, stop condition, and revision evidence. Proposals remain ideas; existing tenant, approval, destination, budget, and action-scope checks govern execution.

Low-fit retrieval returns no precedent. Do not force an analogy based only on industry, invent cases to fill the 100-case target, reuse protected campaign expression, or copy reported outcome rates into a customer forecast. Add a case only after checking material claims against primary sources and, where useful, credible independent reporting. Record context, dates, evidence type, counterfactual limitations, transfer conditions, risks, and a reversible test; require editorial review before runtime use.
