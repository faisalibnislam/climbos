# Creative strategy precedents

Last reviewed: 25 October 2026. This is a curated retrieval set for strategy exploration, not a forecast database or universal playbook.

## Verified coverage

The current runtime library contains **60 editorially reviewed cases** (`CP-001`–`CP-060`). The requested target is 100 useful cases, leaving **40 cases unverified and not yet included**. The general growth case collection is separate and is not counted as creative-strategy coverage.

Each case records its reported choice and mechanism, evidence class, causal limits, transfer conditions and risks, a small-business adaptation, editorial audience/goal/constraint/scale/geography/business-model tags, and a reversible test. Runtime retrieval requires a mechanism match, then ranks with available audience, goal, constraint, scale, geography, and business-model context. The model receives short match explanations with each case rather than an opaque relevance score. Retrieval returns at most two cases to the production growth-plan task and never treats a precedent as evidence about the target business; when those context dimensions are absent, they do not contribute a match.

CP-054 adds a negative pricing-positioning case: the Tata Nano's price and "people's car" framing attracted substantial initial attention, but affordability, aspiration, category comparison, product expectations, safety reports, and delivery constraints interacted. Its academic source presents possible explanations rather than causal proof; it is not a universal argument against low prices.

CP-055 adds a product-value validation case: Juicero's visible device function had a simple manual workaround, while the company and some food-service customers valued cleanliness, convenience, freshness checks, and logistics. The public hand-squeeze demonstration was not representative research and does not alone explain the company's closure; its own shutdown letter cited nationwide supply-chain infrastructure limits.

CP-056 adds a customer-insight-to-creative-platform case: Snickers linked a repeatable humorous premise to its hunger-satisfaction role, then adapted it across markets and into social formats. The results come from retrospective award-case reporting without a controlled counterfactual; a peer-reviewed cultural analysis also flags stereotyping risks. Do not copy its expression, assume the insight fits another audience, or treat reach as customers.

CP-057 adds a participatory social-fundraising case: the Ice Bucket Challenge combined participant-made videos, peer nominations, and a donation request. ALS Association totals are organization-reported; a peer-reviewed UK study estimates short-lived effects of exposure, not transferable business lift. The entry separates participation from the intended outcome and rejects coercive nomination, unsafe imitation, and review-request use.

CP-058 adds a real-time social response case: Oreo's blackout post depended on prior cultural programming, a prepared cross-functional team, event-specific relevance, context checks, and human client approval. The engagement counts were reported at the time; sales and customer acquisition effects remain unestablished. Its large-brand staffing model is not a small-business benchmark, and trend response must never be automated or used to exploit emergencies.

CP-059 adds a co-designed product adaptation case: IKEA Israel partnered with disability organizations to adapt familiar furniture and share printable modifications. Sources differ on whether there were 12 or 13 designs; uptake, sales, cost-effectiveness, and measured accessibility outcomes are not established. Transfer requires affected-user participation, specialist safety review, and accurate limits on compatibility and access claims.

CP-060 examines Metro Trains Melbourne's *Dumb Ways to Die*: entertainment and a distinctive character helped a disregarded safety message earn attention, but attention did not establish comprehension or behavior change. WARC and the current official site report reach and incident reductions without a causal estimate; a peer-reviewed youth study and UTS doctoral thesis document comprehension and evaluation limits. Transfer requires testing what the audience understood, safety and cultural review, and separate measurement of attention and useful action. The campaign's name, characters, music, visual identity, and dark humor are not transferable assets.

## Runtime use and editorial additions

Creative strategy is attached to the existing growth-plan task and its retrieved precedent context is injected into the growth-plays agent and critic, bounded to two cases per request. The owner can request a balanced, more conventional, more surprising, or more ambitious range; every request retains a bold option. The saved plan records the chosen range, exact task/input hashes, prompt contract version, and exact retrieved IDs, and keeps one grounded, one adjacent, and one bold option outside the ranked/costed execution plays. Each option records why a convention persists, what could be lost by breaking it, the specific action and customer behavior, transfer logic/differences or an explicit absence of precedent, separate ratings, and a reversible test with continue/change/stop evidence. Workspace editors can append decisions, owner-edited versions, test status, and evidence-labeled outcome notes; the next strategy run may use only that same workspace's records as untrusted preference/learning context. These notes do not establish causality. Proposals remain ideas; existing tenant, approval, destination, budget, and action-scope checks govern execution. The existing large-growth library remains a hypothesis source, not a verified case corpus.

Low-fit retrieval returns no precedent, and original bold ideas remain required even when retrieval is empty. Do not force an analogy based only on industry, invent cases to fill the 100-case target, reuse protected campaign expression, or copy reported outcome rates into a customer forecast. Add a case only after checking material claims against primary sources and, where useful, credible independent reporting. Record context, dates, evidence type, counterfactual limitations, transfer conditions, risks, and a reversible test; require editorial review before runtime use. Current gap: 45 additional distinct cases still need reliable source research and editorial review, with added emphasis on small/resource-constrained organizations and geographic, business-model, and mechanism diversity.
