## 2. Product outcome and measurable standard

A useful ClimbOS plan must answer:

1. What is keeping this business from reaching its goal, and what evidence supports that diagnosis?
2. What is already working and should be protected?
3. Which customer segment, offer, and route to demand should receive scarce attention?
4. Which plausible alternatives were rejected, and why?
5. What specific work will happen, for which actual targets, in what order?
6. Can the owner afford and deliver it, including calls, fulfilment, approvals, production, AI credits, tools, and cash?
7. What is known, inferred, planned, assumed, missing, or contradicted?
8. What result is feasible within the actual time horizon?
9. What observation would cause ClimbOS to stop, revise, or scale the activity?
10. What has ClimbOS actually completed, what is waiting, and what changed in the business?

**REQ-OUT-01:** Every recommended action must connect diagnosis → intended business effect → evidence → target or asset → resources → execution capability → measurement.

**REQ-OUT-02:** Channel names are categories, not deliverables. “Use influencers,” “do SEO,” “post consistently,” and “run email campaigns” are incomplete recommendations.

**REQ-OUT-03:** Permit a focused plan with one strong move, a measurement task, an offer/positioning improvement, or a conditional recommendation. Do not force every business into the same portfolio shape.

**REQ-OUT-04:** A sensible plan may say the requested target is infeasible under current constraints. Explain the binding constraint and give resource, timing, offer, or target alternatives.

**REQ-OUT-05:** A plan must be usable before every unknown is resolved. Distinguish actions safe to start now from actions whose viability depends on missing information.

### 5.1 Provenance rules

**REQ-EVID-01:** Owner confirmation must be metric-specific. A fact saying “20 customers” does not confirm 20% margin. A margin number in one business, currency, period, or product does not confirm another.

**REQ-EVID-02:** Research verification must bind the exact canonical page and supporting passage to the metric, value, unit, denominator, population, and period. A matching domain or nearby number in model-written notes is insufficient. Search snippets are search evidence, not a fetched-page receipt.

**REQ-EVID-03:** Preserve distinct statuses: successfully fetched, extracted, semantically compatible, owner confirmed, and empirically calibrated. A code step checking that a passage contains a number cannot honestly certify the whole claim.

**REQ-EVID-04:** When machine checks cannot establish support, retain the source claim as reported/unverified and explain the limitation. If the claim is required for a firm financial decision, downgrade the decision or request input.

**REQ-EVID-05:** The model cannot grant itself support states, provider capabilities, approval, or observed business outcomes. Reject or overwrite such fields in model output.

**REQ-EVID-06:** Apply owner overrides and derived metrics without losing evidence lineage. Persist the calculation's input IDs and snapshot. An owner edit invalidates dependent forecasts and any approval bound to changed content or spend.

**REQ-EVID-07:** Planned allocations are choices. Budget and time caps are constraints. Neither is a measured conversion rate. Distinguish them throughout the UI and calculations.

**REQ-EVID-08:** Real missing inputs stay null. Explicit assumptions can support an exploratory scenario, with source, rationale, sensitivity, and validation task. Never replace an unknown business fact with a default while presenting it as known.

## 7. Strategic judgment and planning

### 7.1 Decision process

Use the following logical process. These are reasoning and software responsibilities, **not a requirement for ten separate model calls**:

~~~text
Observe business and results
  → identify the binding constraint
  → consider credible alternatives
  → calculate and apply hard constraints
  → choose an action agenda
  → resolve specific targets
  → prepare assets
  → authorize through workspace policy
  → execute through supported integrations
  → observe outcomes
  → retain, revise, stop, or scale
~~~

**REQ-STRAT-01:** Save a concise diagnosis with supporting observations, competing explanations, confidence limitations, and a falsifier. Distinguish lack of traffic from weak activation, retention, positioning, offer, delivery, or economics.

**REQ-STRAT-02:** Compare 2–4 credible strategic options when the decision merits it. One option may be improving the current offer/conversion/retention before acquisition. Save why an option loses. Do not force multiple model-generated candidates for a trivial decision.

**REQ-STRAT-03:** Apply deterministic constraints before ranking: cash, time, sales cycle, fulfilment capacity, connected capability, minimum evidence, and financial viability.

**REQ-STRAT-04:** Prefer coherent actions that reinforce one guiding policy. A primary acquisition motion, a supporting move, and necessary maintenance are often enough. The appropriate number depends on the business.

**REQ-STRAT-05:** Replace mandatory “bankable + creative” portfolio gates with a decision contract that permits evidence-based exceptions. Creativity is useful when the mechanism fits the business; novelty and channel count are not quality scores.

**REQ-STRAT-06:** Remove incentives to repair economics by inventing more reach, higher rates, or convenient ranges. Lint feedback should identify the invalid assumption and legitimate remedies: verify the input, lower spend, change offer/segment, run a capped test, defer, or reject.

**REQ-STRAT-07:** Address the economics of the whole customer path: acquisition, qualification, sales conversion, fulfilment, retention, refunds, and contribution. “More leads” is not automatically useful.

**REQ-STRAT-08:** Allow expert override of a channel-library ranking only with stated evidence and a recorded rationale. Scores are decision aids, not calibrated probabilities.

### 7.2 Principles to encode in executable playbooks

|---|---|

| Trustworthy online experimentation research | Require valid assignment, compatible denominators, adequate information, and guarded interpretation. Low sample results are a signal to investigate, not a certain winner. |

### 7.3 Ranking and tradeoffs

Use comparable quantities where data supports them:

- Expected incremental contribution over a defined horizon, with sensitivity.
- Cash exposure and time to recover cash.
- Owner/team effort, including sales and fulfilment.
- Time to first useful signal and time to revenue.
- Evidence quality and transferability.
- Reversibility and downside.
- Strategic fit and ability to compound.

When probabilities are unsupported, present cases and break-even thresholds rather than a fabricated expected value. If you use a weighted score, publish the dimensions and explain it is a heuristic. Avoid multiplying several subjective 0–100 scores into false precision.

For a learning experiment, compare expected information benefit with the cost and consequence of choosing wrongly. Implement sensitivity-based question ranking first; do not manufacture a precise “value of information” when inputs are unavailable.

## 8. Mathematics, forecasts, and targets

**REQ-MATH-01:** Audit both shared/growth/calc.ts and shared/agents/economics.ts. Reuse correct formulas. Fix the wrapper behavior and semantic assumptions, not just the displayed text.

### 8.1 Goal, scenario, and commitment

Persist separately:

- Owner goal: metric, baseline, desired value, period, and whether the desired value is total or incremental.
- Forecast/scenario: what the selected actions support, its inputs, horizon, limitations, and low/base/high cases.
- Operational commitment: the work ClimbOS and the owner will actually perform, with resources and prerequisites.
- Goal gap: desired outcome minus the supported scenario, with options to close it.

Example: “Reach 50 customers by day 90” with 20 existing customers means 30 additional net customers, adjusted for expected losses. It must not silently become “50 new customers” or a different simulated total.

Do not replace a revenue or retention goal with an acquisition count just because the current portfolio calculator produces customers. Use a typed metric graph and explain the connection.

### 8.2 Formula catalog

Use explicit units, currencies, periods, and input IDs. Code computes all outputs.

- One-cohort customer funnel: reach × product of conditional stage rates.
- Paid clicks: spend / CPC, only when CPC is known or explicitly assumed.
- Media CAC: cash acquisition spend / new paying customers.
- Fully loaded CAC: (cash + labor + tools + incentives + creator fees + other acquisition costs) / new paying customers.
- Monthly contribution: revenue per customer per month × contribution margin.
- Cohort contribution over n months with constant churn c: CM × [1 − (1 − c)^n] / c; if c=0, CM × n.
- Simple payback: CAC / CM. State its timing assumption.
- Churn-aware payback: first period cumulative contribution covers CAC, under a documented discrete or interpolated cash-timing convention.
- Allowable CAC: contribution within the owner's payback horizon, optionally constrained by a stated contribution reserve/profit requirement.
- Break-even CPC: allowable CAC × downstream click-to-customer rate.
- Next-period recurring revenue: retained existing recurring revenue + new recurring revenue, with expansion/contraction modeled separately when known.
- One-off/service/ecommerce contribution: order value minus cost of goods/delivery/fees/refunds and acquisition costs; do not apply subscription churn/LTV by default.
- Partner funnel: partners approached → accepted → activated → qualified referrals → customers. Do not multiply an assumed partner audience by a general cold-email rate.
- Creator funnel: actual campaign exposure estimate → visits → qualified conversions; distinguish observed views from followers and proposal reach.
- Portfolio total: account for audience overlap or deduplicate actual people/events. A fixed 20% haircut is an explicit scenario choice, not a fact.
- Resource schedule: action-level cash and time rolled up by day/week/month and role. Include one-time costs and delayed conversion.
- Cash/runway: distinguish revenue recognition, contribution, cash collection, refunds, and cash spend where data permits.

**REQ-MATH-02:** Unknown is distinct from zero. Zero churn is a valid known value. Inspect churnOf and applyOwnerEconomics, which currently accept only positive churn. Unknown churn must not quietly become zero retention loss.

**REQ-MATH-03:** Stop silently coercing ratios above 1 into percentages. Inputs must declare ratio versus percent; validate at the boundary. Reject negative, nonfinite, or unit-incompatible values.

**REQ-MATH-04:** Preserve the actual goal horizon. Avoid rounding a few days into a full week of reach, or forcing every plan to 90 days. Model launch/ramp time, sales lag, seasonal windows, and fulfilment capacity.

**REQ-MATH-05:** Monte Carlo percentiles over elicited triangular inputs describe that assumed model. They are not empirically calibrated confidence bounds. Distinguish observed/statistical uncertainty, operational variability, and subjective scenarios. Do not call P50 “most likely” as a general statistical fact; a median is not necessarily a mode.

**REQ-MATH-06:** Distinguish a point funnel estimate from the median of a simulated product. If CAC uses one while the headline uses the other, explain the basis or use consistent cases. For real expected CAC under uncertainty, do not substitute the ratio of means without considering its meaning.

**REQ-MATH-07:** Do not call a scenario profitable as an unconditional claim. Show cash and fully loaded economics, break-even requirements, sensitivity, and the decision state.

**REQ-MATH-08:** Do not sum unlike outcome units, time periods, or currencies. Keep attributed revenue separate from causal incremental revenue. Do not add Google and Meta claimed conversions into total business revenue.

### 8.3 Deterministic reference fixture

For spend $600, CPC $2, click→signup 0.08, signup→paid 0.25, revenue $30/month, margin 0.8, churn 0.05:

- Clicks: 300.
- Customers: 6.
- Media CAC: $100.
- Contribution per customer per month: $24.
- Simple payback: 4.1667 months, ignoring churn.
- Three-month cohort contribution: $68.46.
- Twelve-month cohort contribution: approximately $220.6272.
- Three-month break-even CPC: $1.3692.

The simple payback number must not be presented as churn-adjusted payback. Add known-zero churn, unknown churn, one-off order, mixed currency, fractional horizon, unprofitable action, finite fulfilment capacity, no customers, and missing-input fixtures.

### 18.3 Expert-quality rubric

Use a proposed weighted rubric, reconciled with the existing scoring document:

| Dimension | Proposed weight |
|---|---:|
| Diagnosis and decision usefulness | 25 |
| Evidence, provenance, and honesty | 20 |
| Economic and measurement validity | 20 |
| Executability, specific targets, and feasibility | 20 |

### 18.5 Conversion and experiment validity

**REQ-EVAL-02:** Reuse shared/abtest and existing tracking before adding a second experiment system.

Each experiment declares unit of assignment, population, primary metric, denominator, baseline, minimum meaningful effect, duration, planned sample, guardrails, and decision rule. Record exposure and outcome separately. Detect sample ratio mismatch and tracking failures.

As a planning approximation for a two-arm binary-outcome test with equal allocation:

n per arm ≈ 2 × (z_alpha/2 + z_power)^2 × p × (1-p) / delta^2

For p=2%, a 0.5 percentage-point absolute change, alpha=0.05, and power=0.8, this is roughly 12,300 observations per arm. Verify the chosen design in code. Small businesses with a few hundred visitors cannot be promised decisive A/B results in a week.

When traffic is insufficient, use evidence appropriate to the question: customer interviews, usability checks, direct offer tests, or cautious sequential observation. Label before/after comparisons as confounded when relevant. Do not turn a Bayesian output into a claim of causal certainty.

Avoid optimizing vanity outcomes: email opens can be affected by privacy/bots; clicks may be bot traffic; social engagement is not revenue. Choose qualified business outcomes and include deliverability, unsubscribe, complaint, and fulfilment guardrails.

## 23. Required acceptance tests and adversarial cases

Tests should assert meaningful behavior, not mirror implementation line-by-line. Use deterministic fixtures and mocks for offline coverage. Add real provider sandbox/live checks only with actual setup and authorization.

### Evidence and inputs

- A fact with “20 customers” does not verify 20% margin.
- A price of 29.99 is not verified merely because unrelated text contains 30.
- A conversion rate is not verified because only a different funnel rate or one range endpoint appears near a source URL.
- A cited pricing page cannot verify a conversion rate absent from the passage.
- A different path on the same domain is not treated as the cited page being read.
- A search snippet is labeled search-only.
- A model-supplied check/approval/support state cannot upgrade itself.
- Owner-confirmed zero churn remains zero.
- Unknown churn remains unknown unless an explicit scenario assumption is selected.
- Required null fields survive OpenAI normalization.
- Expired/conflicting evidence downgrades dependent decisions appropriately.
- Owner overrides preserve lineage after save/read/reconciliation.

### Strategy and calculation

- A business with adequate demand but insufficient delivery capacity does not get a generic acquisition-first plan.
- A low-price product does not receive high-cost direct sales without a defensible economic path.
- A legitimate one-play plan can pass; unsupported forced creativity is not required.
- An impossible target remains visible with a feasible alternative and quantified gap.
- A revenue goal is not silently converted into an unrelated customer target.
- Three days of activity do not become a full week of reach.
- Different currencies/units cannot be summed.
- Known positive, zero, negative, null, and nonfinite inputs behave explicitly.
- One-off service/ecommerce economics do not require invented monthly churn.
- Monthly/weekly cash totals and owner effort respect actual constraints.
- A scenario with subjective ranges is not labeled empirically calibrated.
- Exhausted review with a hard failure is not saved as ready for autonomous execution.
- A valid provisional plan remains inspectable without requiring endless model repair.

### Targets and content

- A selected creator channel resolves named public profiles or truthfully reports the limitation.
- Multiple creators on one platform remain distinct.
- No public email is invented.
- Missing contact information does not erase an otherwise useful verified profile.
- Historical competitor collaboration is not automatically a current exclusivity claim.
- Duplicate discovery does not produce duplicate outreach.
- No verified demographics or price is inferred from follower count.
- Competitor discovery works without user-entered names and handles irrelevant same-name entities.
- Copy facts and offers match current approved context.
- A hook's promise is delivered by the body.
- Campaign variants differ meaningfully.
- The writing language and market match the brief.
- SEO drafting chooses improve-existing when appropriate.
- Internal links point to real eligible pages.
- An unavailable CMS stays needs-connection/export instead of fake published.
- A public review reply does not disclose private customer details or promise an unsupported refund.

### Execution and automation

- Duplicate webhook/cron events produce one intended external operation.
- A revoked policy or provider connection blocks a pending operation.
- A changed artifact invalidates its old approval.
- Copilot does not perform external writes.
- Review mode respects action-level approval.
- Bounded Autopilot executes eligible operations without repeated approval while respecting caps and escalation.
- Suspended/deleted workspaces and emergency stop block new actions.
- An uncertain provider write is not blindly repeated.
- A read-after-write receipt is saved.
- Template-eligible actions make zero model calls.
- No-change monitoring makes zero summary calls.
- Tenant A cannot retrieve Tenant B's evidence, cache entry, targets, or credentials.
- Source prompt injection cannot change authorization or expose secrets.
- Token, tool, deadline, and monetary ceilings stop further work while preserving checkpoints.
- Retried completed steps do not double-charge.

### Measurement and freshness

- Compatible numerators and denominators share the same defined window/population.
- “Reply” and “qualified positive reply” are distinct metrics.
- Lifetime page views are not divided into only recent conversions without labeling/normalization.
- A play rename preserves stable ID history.
- A low-sample result stays inconclusive when appropriate.
- An experiment with assignment/tracking failure is not declared a conversion winner.
- A fresh fetch of an old benchmark does not reset the benchmark's publication date.
- A proposed knowledge rule must pass promotion checks before becoming active.
- A rule can be rolled back with dependent version references intact.
