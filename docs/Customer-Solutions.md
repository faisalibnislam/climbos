# What ClimbOS currently provides

Inventory checked against the local application on 10 November 2026. ClimbOS helps an owner decide how to grow, prepares the work, and helps them learn from results. This describes the local build, not a launched service or a claim that every connected-provider operation has been verified live.

1. **A starting point for a business.** Begin with a website or a plain-language description, including a business without a website. An anonymous website first look provides a limited homepage snapshot and preliminary ideas.
2. **Business research.** Read public pages, extract statements with sources, identify customers and offers, and separate facts, published promises and assumptions. Owners can check, correct or reject extracted details.
3. **A growth direction.** Explain who to reach, why they might choose the business, what to focus on and which alternatives should wait. Longer-term direction stays connected to a small practical test.
4. **Short, manageable plans.** Plan for 14, 30 or 90 days, with small milestones, a next action, useful learning signals and later reviews. Compare saved plans and carry learning into future plans.
5. **AI preparation and help.** Ask for research, explanations or drafts through chat. Turn a recommendation into bounded work and review what AI has prepared. Specialist profiles cover different business tasks; they are AI workflows, not human advisers.
6. **Places to find customers.** Compare business-specific search, social, partnership, community, referral, sales, directory and marketplace routes. Show evidence, prerequisites, costs and uncertainty instead of a universal channel checklist.
7. **Creator and partner research.** Find potential niche educators, reviewers, YouTube creators and complementary businesses; explain a demonstration angle, inspect sources and contact rules, and prepare unsent pitches. Topic fit does not establish interest or availability.
8. **Business prospect research.** Research possible business customers from bounded public sources, explain fit, prepare outreach campaigns and track replies. Real sending requires the appropriate mailbox, recipient checks and approvals.
9. **Competitor comparisons.** Compare offers, positioning, published pricing, strengths and gaps. Watch individually confirmed public competitor sites for changes and suggest a test based on the retained evidence.
10. **Social posts.** Prepare native text, carousels and video scripts for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, TikTok, Pinterest and YouTube. Include captions, visual directions and accessible descriptions where the format needs them.
11. **A content calendar and regular posting.** Plan recurring content, schedule drafting, review posts and manage approval rules. Connected publishing paths exist for supported Facebook Page, professional Instagram, LinkedIn and X posts. TikTok, Pinterest and YouTube currently use export/manual publishing.
12. **Images and short videos.** Prepare visual briefs, generate supported images, edit graphics and layouts, and assemble supported short-video workflows. Generation and rendering depend on configured providers/workers and their usage limits; a synthetic visual is not real customer or product evidence.
13. **SEO pages and content.** Choose between improving an observed existing page and proposing a new one; draft titles, descriptions, page copy, FAQs, source-backed claims, internal-link suggestions and proposed publishing details. Keep demand and rankings unknown when no data exists.
14. **Search and AI visibility checks.** Sample technical search readiness, work with connected Search Console observations, prepare page-review requests, and record manual checks of answers from search/AI tools. This is not continuous tracking of every AI engine or a guarantee of citations.
15. **Website improvement advice.** Review a bounded sample of page content, screenshots and supplied performance evidence; identify confusing or weak elements and suggest changes. Design reviews can connect a finding to a specific draft, test and recorded result.
16. **Offer and landing pages.** Build and preview ClimbOS-hosted pages, lead forms and offer variants. Capture inquiries and compare designs. A hosted page does not modify the customer's existing website; WordPress supports a separately approved private draft, not automatic live publication.
17. **Advertising preparation and controls.** Draft Google Search and Meta ads, prepare paused campaigns, show supported connected reports and apply approved activation/pause actions with limits. Real use needs advertiser access, provider credentials and exact budget/account approval.
18. **Marketing emails and follow-ups.** Draft campaigns and onboarding, follow-up, retention or re-engagement sequences. Supported marketing sends need the customer's configured Resend account, eligible recipients, suppression/unsubscribe handling and approval.
19. **Customer records and sales progress.** Import supported contacts, maintain notes and a sales pipeline, track opportunities from first contact toward a sale, and use recorded customer feedback in later work.
20. **Messages, reviews and feedback.** Manage supported connected messages and manually imported reviews, draft replies, identify tentative recurring themes and prepare neutral review invitations. Automatic public review sync and native public review-reply publishing are not implemented.
21. **Chat on a business website.** Configure a visitor chat widget that answers from supplied business information and collects inquiries. Live use requires installation and an available backend.
22. **Results and business numbers.** Use supported GA4 and Search Console connections, read-only Stripe revenue or validated CSV imports; review funnels, attribution views and cohorts. Record actual spend, time, repeat customers and retained revenue/contribution for growth activities.
23. **Tests and learning.** Prepare experiments, record versions, budgets, responsibility and decision rules, compare observed results, and carry learning forward. A difference in results does not automatically prove that a change caused it.
24. **Pricing, offers and business-model decisions.** Review how a business makes money, compare possible offers and future horizons, and calculate scenarios when the owner supplies the required numbers. Missing revenue, margins, retention and capacity stay unknown.
25. **Launch, listing and review-platform preparation.** Prepare versioned Product Hunt, G2 and Google Business Profile material and evidence/checklists for manual submission. These preparation flows do not automatically publish listings, generate genuine customer reviews or guarantee platform acceptance.
26. **Team and operating controls.** Manage businesses, roles, brand guidance, approved examples, connections, privacy, budgets, credits, activity, approvals and pause controls. AI work is metered; external actions stay tied to the relevant business, content version, destination and allowed scope.

The strongest current offering is **research → a clear recommendation → prepared drafts → review → measured learning**. External sending, publishing and advertising are conditional on real setup and permissions. AI quality varies and some drafts remain provisional or blocked by factual checks; implemented code is not proof of expert-quality advice for every industry.

The normal free allowance remains 50 credits. The bounded first website read plus first-plan path fits that allowance with the default route. One correction can reuse unused funds inside the original quoted ceiling; it stops when there is insufficient room. The code does not invent credits, subscriptions or customer results to make onboarding pass.
