## Implemented

- Haiku gathers bounded public evidence and handles routine extraction/routing. Sonnet writes substantive strategy and reviews it. A matched Haiku strategy request failed to produce a complete accepted draft, so the cheaper writer was not promoted.
- The focused workflow uses one writer and one reviewer, with at most one separately reserved revision and review. It removes channel-scoring, forecast-portfolio and synthesis calls from the default first-step experience. It does not invent the inputs those forecasts require.
- Tenant- and website-bound fresh research is reused, including explicit source restrictions. Provider prompt caching covers stable prefixes. Actual read/write usage is metered; a requested cache is not counted as a hit. Completed phases are checkpointed; ambiguous failures retain their spending holds and do not replay automatically.
- The recommendation already includes useful starter drafts. Approval reuses those drafts; it does not regenerate another set of posts and emails. Direct draft review is available before plan approval and makes no AI request.
- The selected focused/detailed contract reaches the quote, initial reservation, worker and model selector. Quotes include the bounded revision allowance and cost cap. The initial reservation and maximum displayed quote are deliberately different. No automatic repair loop starts when a page opens.
- A compact expert method compares mechanisms behind the simple screen. It preserves business-specific positioning, alternatives, useful search/social assets, uncertainty, contact rules and longer-term learning, rather than reducing strategy to a short outreach checklist.
- The worker retains the actual 14/30/90-day horizon, industry restrictions, geography context and creativity preference. Milestones use exact day windows and keep future ambitions separate from forecasts.
