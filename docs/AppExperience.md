# ClimbOS app: understandable without business training

The application uses task-based navigation and plain-language explanations. Existing specialist routes remain available.

| Main destination | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Home | Describe what you need and pick up one useful item waiting for you. |
| Your goals | Switch between saved goals, see one recommended move, and open detail when needed. |
| Your work | Find prepared drafts, save private edits, follow AI activity, and open specialist editors. |

Results, customer tools, and the full tool directory remain under More. Business & settings contains the existing setup and policy controls. Specialist URLs stay valid.

The 10 November goal-first update supersedes the older navigation and fixed-length planning examples below. New focused recommendations judge preparation and first-review timing from the goal, approach, dependencies, capacity and actual deadline. There is no 14/30/90-day picker in Home, Goals, onboarding, or Business settings. Historical milestones remain labelled as an older planning window; they are not rewritten into fresh AI estimates.

Each goal has its own plan and drafts. Revising a goal keeps its ancestor history; adding an unrelated goal creates a separate thread. Switching focus starts no AI work and does not change budgets. Jobs capture their goal so changing tabs cannot reattach a result to another goal.

Make it happen opens the saved prepared drafts when they already exist, without another AI charge or plan-approval prerequisite for reading them. Private edits are stored separately from the immutable plan and its approval, with optimistic revision checks to protect a teammate's changes. They are retrievable in Your work. Saving never publishes, sends, changes a website, or grants execution permission. Publishing remains in the existing destination-specific workflow.

Home shows a single relevant item before the shared request composer for returning owners. Earlier conversations, additional decisions and recorded results are available through disclosures. Sending a request opens its conversation; choosing a suggestion only fills editable text. More detail on local verification is in [Goal-first UX implementation](Goal-First-UX-2026-11-10.md).

The content calendar has day, week, and month views in the workspace timezone. It includes scheduled posts, Autopilot slots, and items waiting for review; selecting a date opens that day's agenda on both touch and desktop layouts. Opening an item leads to its post or Autopilot workspace. A calendar recommendation never schedules or publishes by itself.

Plan version comparison starts with a plain-language list of changes to the saved diagnosis, plays, selected set, channels, and customer groups. It uses the original values saved with each version, not forecasts recalculated from today's inputs. Older plans without enough saved detail are labeled unavailable; modeled customer ranges remain scenarios, not results.

When a saved growth decision includes its structured focus, the plan names the first move in the summary and keeps the supporting move and recurring check in the existing details section. Older decisions without that saved focus continue to show the rest of their available explanation without blank placeholders.

Saved plans also become due for refresh when a confirmed business fact crosses its category review interval. The age is a reminder to recheck, not evidence that the fact is false; approval and starting work wait until the plan is refreshed. Older snapshots without a recorded freshness state remain comparable by their saved fact IDs and edit times, and conservatively require refresh when any current fact is already due for review.

The plan's Pages read list labels same-domain pages as published business claims. Other domains are shown as references whose independence is unverified; being on another website alone does not prove a claim.

The Sources section can also show the saved research notes for each plan version. The optional disclosure includes the notes, source links, retrieval date when recorded, and publication/update dates explicitly reported in metadata on pages ClimbOS fetched. Page-reported dates are not independently verified and remain distinct from retrieval time. It identifies the notes as AI-written and source reliability/relevance as model assessments, not independent verification. Business facts show whether they came from the owner, research, or a demo, their review state and last-check date, and expandable links to the source records attached to that fact. A saved page excerpt is shown only during its retention period; after it expires, the owner sees that the link opens the current page, which may have changed. Source records are checked against the workspace and business before display. Only the tenant's selected plan evidence with a matching stored hash is shown; missing or invalid evidence stays unavailable.

Home and request results label action-card progress in plain language: Suggested, In progress, Completed, Ready for review, Needs setup, Couldn’t finish, or Skipped. Request summaries distinguish work that still needs a next step, drafts ready for owner review, completed work, and actions the owner skipped. A completed card that would affect something outside ClimbOS is labeled Ready for review and says that nothing has gone live; opening its result does not grant approval or publish it. External-effect actions say “Prepare it” in the request feed, Today list, and swipe deck.

Account-connection and Autopilot suggestions open their matching setup pages from the request feed, Today list, and swipe deck. Their labels say what page opens; navigation alone does not connect an account, enable Autopilot, complete the suggestion, or change its permissions.

`src/components/app/navigation.ts` owns the navigation labels, tool explanations, aliases, and legacy-route grouping. Tool directories link to the real existing editors and records. Connections and review remain directly accessible; technical settings are grouped under Advanced tools without changing permission checks.

Home and first/additional business onboarding share `RequestComposer`. Home's four starter requests match the saved business type when it is known; an unknown type gets the generic set. Suggestions are exposed to assistive technology as a named group. A suggestion only adds editable text; it never starts work. Existing input is preserved when another suggestion is selected. Home request drafts survive navigation and page reloads in the current browser tab and are scoped to both the signed-in person and business. New-business setup drafts also survive in the current tab and are separated by signed-in person and first-versus-next business setup. Drafts are not synced to another device. The contextual Climb panel follows the same suggestion behavior and labels its separate page-help conversation.

Content review shows the draft's intended reader, objective, core idea, and exact growth-plan item when the copy is tied to one. Planned Autopilot slots show the same plan item before drafting, so owners can see which approved offer the post will use.

Owner-started prospect emails and review invitations check for an unresolved email to the same workspace address before queuing and again before mailbox handoff. The review preview and sending panel explain addresses left out; no arbitrary cross-workflow cooldown is applied. Sending still requires the existing recipient confirmation, workspace access, and mailbox controls.

Growth Home opportunity questions open an editable field instead of sending a fixed prompt. The Home request composer shows the deterministic credit estimate for interpreting a request and the separate maximum budget for safe draft or analysis work that may start automatically; demo work is labeled as using no paid AI credits. The owner sees that sending does not alter the plan or publish anything. The backend supplies the saved, tenant-checked opportunity context to the existing Today conversation. Home, Your plan, specialist run history, and detailed work receipts use the same plain-language labels for queued, working, approval, scheduled, paused, finished, failed, canceled, and uncertain runs; an unknown future state shows a neutral “Status update” label instead of an internal code.

When plan-target research is enabled and an approved plan has a linked search, Home shows its current status and up to three suggested business or creator names. Creator suggestions are labeled as possible partners, while business suggestions are labeled as possible customers. Both are identified as public-research suggestions, show no contact details, and link to the plan for the full evidence and review. This is a bounded, read-only preview; it does not start a search or send outreach.

Home reads a bounded set of drafts waiting for review. When more than 20 are waiting, counts say “20+” instead of implying the returned sample is the exact total; the assistant receives the same lower-bound label. The review list remains limited to six recent drafts, with the link to the full review queue available.

Date-sensitive growth recommendations lead with a plain-language timing choice, suggested dates when supported, the named event or season, and why that moment may matter to the business. Missing information stays visible; preparation, delivery, capacity, source claims, and evidence dates are available in an expandable detail section.

Offer recommendations explain whether to consider a change, check details first, improve another business issue, or keep the offer. The first view shows who the idea is for, its evidence, money left after listed costs, capacity, key risks, unknowns, the next step, and the approval boundary. The test plan, alternatives, promotion history, and full risk list open only when requested; the cost-detail disclosure labels every price and cost as measured, reported, sourced, inferred, or assumed and shows its supporting claim or source where available. Missing facts remain unknown and any live change still needs the existing approval.

When a draft is made from an approved plan, its exact offer terms travel with each saved content version. Owner edits and translations keep that context, and approval rechecks the price, discount, eligibility, mechanism, urgency, and selected plan item. A “check details first” promotion remains a test proposal and cannot be treated as a customer offer. Older drafts without a saved offer snapshot retain their earlier review behavior.

Command search uses local filtering. A natural-language request is handed to Home as an editable draft rather than dispatched from search. Handoff keys are scoped to the signed-in person and workspace. The assistant provider resets transient state when the workspace or authenticated identity changes.

Onboarding is one genuine AI conversation titled “What's your wish?” The old six-step flow, fact-review form, analysis-depth chooser and “Finish later” header action are retired from this entry. The owner sends a wish; the AI asks for the website when missing, reads public pages and follows up only on useful gaps. A business without a website is supported. Goals, stage and available help are discussed in the same composer. Suggested answers remain editable drafts and never submit themselves. An explicit unauthorized setup URL shows an access explanation rather than falling back to another business.

Discovery messages and a source-labeled business memory are saved in Convex per person and business. Direct website answers are retained before AI extraction. Public claims need matching source quotes; private goals, stage, current efforts and resources need owner evidence. AI answer repair preserves already validated details. The owner can correct information in chat; explicit uncertainty is accepted for private inputs. The app does not ask for customer records, passwords or API keys.

“Make my plan” appears once the offer, audience, business model, goal and website status have adequate context. Stage, time, budget and past marketing are optional; absent private inputs stay unknown. A source-supported inferred audience remains labeled as a working hypothesis. The model cannot bypass that server check by declaring readiness. Planning captures an immutable discovery brief and public-page excerpts in the job input so subsequent chat changes cannot silently alter that job. Numeric owner time and ad limits come only from explicit wording, with no invented revenue or currency conversion. The chat uses the budgeted standard model route; strategic planning retains its existing route, quote, reservation and approval controls. Website reads use bounded public fetching without a paid scraper, and recent reads are reused. Nothing is published or sent by onboarding.

The 11 November local update applies the ordered timeline to the real plan page. Next steps opens by default, with an ordered step rail and one active workspace. Selecting another step replaces the workspace rather than expanding another long section. Prepared work, Why this plan and Research have separate focused tabs. The timeline preserves actual saved phase order and timing. New plans can provide 1–8 tasks per step; older plans use their existing first actions and first-step checklist without inventing new work. Team-reported completion is saved per exact plan and task text, survives reloads and can be undone. A revised plan starts with its own progress. Viewing or editing a draft does not mark a task complete, approve the plan or publish anything. Discuss this step fills the existing editable assistant composer; it does not submit or charge automatically. Make it happen is an explicit, quoted AI preparation action. Completion records are included in workspace export and deletion.

Pages explain their purpose before technical details. Optional “What do these words mean?” help defines business terms without replacing precise stored data. Results distinguish Recorded, Linked to a source, Estimated, Reported by the platform, and Based on your inputs. These presentation labels preserve their original underlying metric kinds and calculations. Ad campaign results can be refreshed from linked Google or Meta campaigns; Meta action types remain separate and are not treated as conversions without a confirmed goal match. The view labels the platform source and sync time, explains that platform results do not establish incremental customers or profit, and never pauses or edits a campaign from reporting. Growth-play results show how many elapsed weeks have an owner entry; a missing week stays unknown, not zero, and prevents the play from being labeled ahead, on track, or behind for the full period.

Specialist settings show each profile's ClimbOS credit budget, recent run costs, and team-reported usefulness ratings when available. For completed runs, workspace editors can rate usefulness and optionally report their own time saved; leaving time blank preserves it as unknown. A 90-day summary averages multiple time reports on the same run before showing the median per run and identifies the result as member-reported, not a measured business outcome. The monthly total includes credits used or set aside; runs still in progress or waiting for review may include estimates. Older profiles without a stored monthly limit still show known usage and say that no limit is recorded. Credit usage is not customer revenue, business profit, or measured time saved. If the bounded usage query cannot return a complete total, the screen says usage is unavailable rather than presenting the limit as recorded spend. Existing run, edit, and archive controls remain the way to change or stop a specialist.

Completed built-in specialist run receipts let workspace editors optionally record a business result they observed, its group and time period, and an optional comparison. The page labels it as the member's report and says it does not prove the specialist caused the result. Unknown or unmeasured outcomes can remain blank; reports are scoped to the reporting member and workspace.

“Pause all activity” sets the workspace stop first, cancels unstarted jobs, removes schedules from all waiting posts, and requests a pause for every recorded active ad campaign. Cleanup drains indexed batches so workspaces with more than one page of queued items are covered. Resuming does not reschedule posts. A provider action already handed off may still finish, and an external ad pause can remain unverified if the provider is unavailable.

Business insights describe what helps the business, what gets in the way, and what to watch. Each finding stays compact until the owner opens “Evidence and why it matters.” That detail shows its recorded basis, confidence, strategic implication, and source links. Missing support is called out as an idea to check, not a confirmed fact. Optional next steps connect the findings and show a first test and measure; these are suggestions to review, not established advantages. Commercial evidence uses a plain-language status while retaining whether the evidence supports a small test, is mixed, or remains insufficient.

In a website audit, an owner or editor can save a chosen Design Critic direction against its exact finding and audit run. The editable brief is available after that direction is saved, so it always reflects the recorded owner choice. Creating a ClimbOS landing-page draft rechecks that exact saved choice on the server and stores the audit, finding, direction, region, reviewed page, and any suggested measurement plan alongside the draft. This source remains visible on the page record while its content revisions and publish state are tracked separately. For these audit-linked drafts, an owner or editor must save the result to measure, how to measure it, and either a sourced current result or an explicit reason and plan for collecting it before publishing. The plan is stored in recoverable version history and applies only to the exact saved page version; editing or restoring content requires the owner to review and save it again. An unknown current result remains labeled unknown. When the render service is available, the review shows the synthetic desktop/mobile capture, its source page, viewport dimensions, and capture time; these private captures are scheduled for cleanup after 30 days. A capture is not evidence of customer behavior. The saved direction is separate from whether the finding is accepted or resolved, is visible to the workspace, and creates an audit record. “Prepare editable brief” opens a separate ClimbOS landing-page draft; it does not alter the audited external website. The draft preview shows the current editor content using the same shared page layout as the visitor page, with desktop or mobile width constraints that activate its responsive layout. The example form is inert; analytics and lead submission are disabled. This preview remains limited to ClimbOS-hosted pages and does not render the customer's audited website. Saved versions and exact-version publishing checks remain in control of any later ClimbOS-hosted page update.

Competitor-change test ideas show the exact retained page-diff line that prompted the idea. If a proposed test cannot point to one of those lines, ClimbOS keeps the observed change but omits the test idea. The line is source-reported page text; it does not prove customer demand or predict results.

Suggested competitor sites show the saved research summary and a separate possible-relevance explanation when those details were recorded, with a link to the cited source. When comparison details are missing, the owner sees that limitation and can review the research source before adding the site. A “direct competitor” label comes from business research and does not confirm customer-segment or geography fit.

Owners can mark an unadded competitor suggestion “Not relevant.” ClimbOS retains that choice for the workspace and leaves the site out of later suggestions. This does not start monitoring or use research credits. The owner can still add the site manually later if they change their mind.

Researching a possible competitor only creates a sourced suggestion. It does not start recurring website monitoring. An owner must add the specific site in the competitor tool; scheduled summaries also stay within the workspace credit approval limit. Watches created automatically by an older version are held until an owner or admin confirms each site. Existing manually added watches and their controls remain intact.

The Automations settings page can pause or resume weekly competitor checks without deleting saved sites. A check requested by the owner remains available while the weekly schedule is paused; a scheduled check already queued rechecks the pause before reading a site.

The same settings page can pause all scheduled specialist profiles in a workspace. Pausing prevents due profiles from being claimed and rechecks the setting immediately before a queued run starts. Owners can still start profiles manually; after resuming, profiles already due may run on the next scheduler check.

When a social draft uses a current trend or meme format, its review details retain the exact source URL, date, and source-reported observation. ClimbOS requires the fields to match a dated record from the current research run and rejects future or over-30-day-old signals as current. The 30-day limit is a product validation rule; it does not describe how long trends universally last. The source statement remains source-reported, not proof that the idea fits the audience or will perform.

Growth-run experience timing is recorded from the existing workflow: the first finding timestamp is set only when a quick-take opportunity links to confirmed business facts, and the first-action timestamp is set when an owner starts a linked plan action. Hypotheses and generic progress previews do not count as findings. These timestamps measure workflow response and owner follow-through; they do not measure business growth. Run completion and actual AI/research usage remain available from the existing run and usage records. Starting an action is not evidence that an external change was published, sent, or completed.

The operator console summarizes first-confirmed-finding and first-owner-action time only when at least five non-fixture strategy runs have the relevant timestamp. It also shows current task statuses for runs started in the last 30 days and separates recorded from estimated research spend. These are operational measurements, not proof of customer or business impact; capped windows and small samples stay visible.

The anonymous homepage preview shows its business summary, extracted page claims, and—when an exact source quote is verified—one preliminary opportunity with a small next step while the remaining ideas are still being drafted. If no clear observation is supported by a quote from the fetched homepage, it is omitted. A matching quote verifies only that the page said those words; the recommendation remains an AI interpretation of one page, not a confirmed business problem or evidence of customer behavior.

`shared/agents/ownerLanguage.ts` supplies the same everyday-language guidance to the contextual assistant and the agent runtime. The owner-brevity skill and generated registry have matching guidance. The change applies to owner-facing explanations, preserves technical field names, source labels, uncertainty, and permissions, and keeps requested publishable copy subject to its channel requirements. Backend instruction changes require the normal backend release process to take effect outside local tests.

Verification lives in `tests/unit/app-navigation.test.ts`, `tests/unit/onboarding-language.test.ts`, the parser regressions, and `e2e/simple-app.spec.ts`. Browser checks use synthetic demo data and the design-system specimen; they do not perform live customer sends, publishing, ad activation, or paid AI generation. Authenticated owner onboarding is covered by rendering and permission regressions, rather than creating real additional customer companies during UI checks.

## AI preparation and workspace refresh — 11 November 2026

The workspace retains the existing sage, lime, ink and surface palette. The customer app uses clearer sans-serif headings, roomier navigation targets, a quieter top bar and one active plan step. Home leads with saved work when available, followed by the shared composer. Activity, Needs you, Results and Ideas use focused tabs. Onboarding remains one conversation; its first view centers the wish and composer. Suggested messages remain editable and never submit themselves.

Routine AI replies target 10–25 words with a 30-word maximum. This applies to conversational replies and work summaries, not substantive deliverables. The server stops intake once core business gaps are resolved even when the model asks an optional strategy question. It repairs channel/customer-problem questions instead of asking owners to do the analysis. Missing private facts remain unknown; public page statements remain source claims.

Make it happen starts one scoped preparation job for the exact saved plan step. Haiku researches with at most two web searches; the bounded public reader checks source pages and observed contact/resource links without a paid scraper. Sonnet prepares usable private text: named research, page copy, channel-specific posts, pitches, scripts or review analysis as appropriate. One repair is allowed. The credit quote covers the three possible model calls without reserving an additional unused repair. Opening saved work costs nothing and duplicate submissions reuse the same job.

The job stores its business context, exact plan step, sources, owner answers and previous drafts. Source quotes, URLs and contact addresses are checked against supplied evidence. Explicit matching product-outreach refusals block that pitch. These checks do not establish commercial truth, complete policy interpretation, demand or contact consent. Unknown permission stays unknown. AI performs bounded public checks and prepares independent work rather than handing those checks back to the owner. It asks at most one essential private question when useful preparation cannot otherwise continue.

A failed source check triggers one repair. If only part of the repaired result is valid, independently usable drafts remain visible with a limitation notice; rejected drafts and unsupported citations are held back. If no usable draft survives, the job fails rather than inventing completion. Private validation diagnostics are retained. Earlier saved results and owner edits remain accessible even after a later failure; retries reuse the most recent available preparation. Draft edits use revision checks and can be copied. The immutable snapshot survives a later owner edit and can reuse preparation from an earlier saved version of the same goal.
