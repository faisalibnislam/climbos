# ClimbOS app: understandable without business training

The application uses task-based navigation and plain-language explanations. Existing specialist routes remain available.

| Main destination | What it is for |
|---|---|
| Home | Describe what you need, follow requests, and choose a next step. |
| Your plan | Understand what to focus on and why. |
| Your work | Open the existing post/email/ad editor and calendar, regular posting, pages, images, video tools, or design editor. |

The content calendar has day, week, and month views in the workspace timezone. It includes scheduled posts, Autopilot slots, and items waiting for review; selecting a date opens that day's agenda on both touch and desktop layouts. Opening an item leads to its post or Autopilot workspace. A calendar recommendation never schedules or publishes by itself.
| Customers | Open customer research, customer progress, messages, reviews, and visitor-chat setup. |
| Results | Read available business measurements with source and uncertainty labels. |
| All tools | Find existing tools through everyday or specialist search terms. |

`src/components/app/navigation.ts` owns the navigation labels, tool explanations, aliases, and legacy-route grouping. Tool directories link to the real existing editors and records. Connections and review remain directly accessible; technical settings are grouped under Advanced tools without changing permission checks.

Home and first/additional business onboarding share `RequestComposer`. Home's four starter requests match the saved business type when it is known; an unknown type gets the generic set. A suggestion only adds editable text; it never starts work. Existing input is preserved when another suggestion is selected. Home request drafts survive navigation and page reloads in the current browser tab and are scoped to both the signed-in person and business. New-business setup drafts also survive in the current tab and are separated by signed-in person and first-versus-next business setup. Drafts are not synced to another device. The contextual Climb panel follows the same suggestion behavior and labels its separate page-help conversation.

Content review shows the draft's intended reader, objective, core idea, and exact growth-plan item when the copy is tied to one. Planned Autopilot slots show the same plan item before drafting, so owners can see which approved offer the post will use.

Owner-started prospect emails and review invitations check for an unresolved email to the same workspace address before queuing and again before mailbox handoff. The review preview and sending panel explain addresses left out; no arbitrary cross-workflow cooldown is applied. Sending still requires the existing recipient confirmation, workspace access, and mailbox controls.

Growth Home opportunity questions open an editable field instead of sending a fixed prompt. The Home request composer shows the deterministic credit estimate for interpreting a request and the separate maximum budget for safe draft or analysis work that may start automatically; demo work is labeled as using no paid AI credits. The owner sees that sending does not alter the plan or publish anything. The backend supplies the saved, tenant-checked opportunity context to the existing Today conversation. Home, Your plan, specialist run history, and detailed work receipts use the same plain-language labels for queued, working, approval, scheduled, paused, finished, failed, canceled, and uncertain runs; an unknown future state shows a neutral “Status update” label instead of an internal code.

When plan-target research is enabled and an approved plan has a linked search, Home shows its current status and up to three suggested business or creator names. It labels these as public-research suggestions, shows no contact details, and links to the plan for the full evidence and review. This is a bounded, read-only preview; it does not start a search or send outreach.

Home reads a bounded set of drafts waiting for review. When more than 20 are waiting, counts say “20+” instead of implying the returned sample is the exact total; the assistant receives the same lower-bound label. The review list remains limited to six recent drafts, with the link to the full review queue available.

Date-sensitive growth recommendations lead with a plain-language timing choice, suggested dates when supported, the named event or season, and why that moment may matter to the business. Missing information stays visible; preparation, delivery, capacity, source claims, and evidence dates are available in an expandable detail section.

Offer recommendations explain whether to consider a change, check details first, improve another business issue, or keep the offer. The first view shows who the idea is for, its evidence, money left after listed costs, capacity, key risks, unknowns, the next step, and the approval boundary. The test plan, alternatives, promotion history, and full risk list open only when requested; the cost-detail disclosure labels every price and cost as measured, reported, sourced, inferred, or assumed and shows its supporting claim or source where available. Missing facts remain unknown and any live change still needs the existing approval.

When a draft is made from an approved plan, its exact offer terms travel with each saved content version. Owner edits and translations keep that context, and approval rechecks the price, discount, eligibility, mechanism, urgency, and selected plan item. A “check details first” promotion remains a test proposal and cannot be treated as a customer offer. Older drafts without a saved offer snapshot retain their earlier review behavior.

Command search uses local filtering. A natural-language request is handed to Home as an editable draft rather than dispatched from search. Handoff keys are scoped to the signed-in person and workspace. The assistant provider resets transient state when the workspace or authenticated identity changes.

Onboarding starts with a business description, accepts a business without a website, shows an editable name, and offers a conventional form using the supplied information. The first take starts alongside the deeper research and remains available on the fact-review step after research finishes, so it is not lost before the owner can read it. This early direction remains explicitly preliminary. It distinguishes confirmed inputs from claims extracted from a website, links the latter to a page read in that run and labels them for the owner to check; unsupported ideas remain hypotheses. It does not replace the later full plan. Additional-business setup explains its separate data context and retains the existing workspace creation and research functions. An explicit unauthorized setup URL shows an access explanation rather than silently falling back to another company's setup.

Analysis-depth choices are available under More options. First-time setup shows the selected research mode's maximum credit estimate before the owner continues; the estimate follows the same engine reservation calculation, and unused reserved credits are returned. Goal and approval wording uses ordinary language. Continuing through fact review still confirms all remaining facts; this consequence stays visible next to the button. Approval, entitlement, spending, and tenant enforcement continue in the existing backend functions.

Pages explain their purpose before technical details. Optional “What do these words mean?” help defines business terms without replacing precise stored data. Results distinguish Recorded, Linked to a source, Estimated, Reported by the platform, and Based on your inputs. These presentation labels preserve their original underlying metric kinds and calculations. Ad campaign results can be refreshed from linked Google or Meta campaigns; Meta action types remain separate and are not treated as conversions without a confirmed goal match. The view labels the platform source and sync time, explains that platform results do not establish incremental customers or profit, and never pauses or edits a campaign from reporting. Growth-play results show how many elapsed weeks have an owner entry; a missing week stays unknown, not zero, and prevents the play from being labeled ahead, on track, or behind for the full period.

Specialist settings show each profile's ClimbOS credit budget, recent run costs, and team-reported usefulness ratings when available. For completed runs, workspace editors can rate usefulness and optionally report their own time saved; leaving time blank preserves it as unknown. A 90-day summary averages multiple time reports on the same run before showing the median per run and identifies the result as member-reported, not a measured business outcome. The monthly total includes credits used or set aside; runs still in progress or waiting for review may include estimates. Older profiles without a stored monthly limit still show known usage and say that no limit is recorded. Credit usage is not customer revenue, business profit, or measured time saved. If the bounded usage query cannot return a complete total, the screen says usage is unavailable rather than presenting the limit as recorded spend. Existing run, edit, and archive controls remain the way to change or stop a specialist.

Completed built-in specialist run receipts let workspace editors optionally record a business result they observed, its group and time period, and an optional comparison. The page labels it as the member's report and says it does not prove the specialist caused the result. Unknown or unmeasured outcomes can remain blank; reports are scoped to the reporting member and workspace.

“Pause all activity” sets the workspace stop first, cancels unstarted jobs, removes schedules from all waiting posts, and requests a pause for every recorded active ad campaign. Cleanup drains indexed batches so workspaces with more than one page of queued items are covered. Resuming does not reschedule posts. A provider action already handed off may still finish, and an external ad pause can remain unverified if the provider is unavailable.

Business insights describe what helps the business, what gets in the way, and what to watch. Each finding stays compact until the owner opens “Evidence and why it matters.” That detail shows its recorded basis, confidence, strategic implication, and source links. Missing support is called out as an idea to check, not a confirmed fact. Optional next steps connect the findings and show a first test and measure; these are suggestions to review, not established advantages. Commercial evidence uses a plain-language status while retaining whether the evidence supports a small test, is mixed, or remains insufficient.

In a website audit, an owner or editor can save a chosen Design Critic direction against its exact finding and audit run. When the render service is available, the review shows the synthetic desktop/mobile capture, its source page, viewport dimensions, and capture time; these private captures are scheduled for cleanup after 30 days. A capture is not evidence of customer behavior. The saved direction is separate from whether the finding is accepted or resolved, is visible to the workspace, and creates an audit record. “Prepare editable brief” opens a separate ClimbOS landing-page draft; it does not alter the audited external website. The draft's own desktop/mobile preview, saved versions, and exact-version publishing checks remain in control of any later ClimbOS-hosted page update.

Competitor-change test ideas show the exact retained page-diff line that prompted the idea. If a proposed test cannot point to one of those lines, ClimbOS keeps the observed change but omits the test idea. The line is source-reported page text; it does not prove customer demand or predict results.

Suggested competitor sites show the saved research summary and a separate possible-relevance explanation when those details were recorded, with a link to the cited source. When comparison details are missing, the owner sees that limitation and can review the research source before adding the site. A “direct competitor” label comes from business research and does not confirm customer-segment or geography fit.

Owners can mark an unadded competitor suggestion “Not relevant.” ClimbOS retains that choice for the workspace and leaves the site out of later suggestions. This does not start monitoring or use research credits. The owner can still add the site manually later if they change their mind.

Researching a possible competitor only creates a sourced suggestion. It does not start recurring website monitoring. An owner must add the specific site in the competitor tool; scheduled summaries also stay within the workspace credit approval limit. Watches created automatically by an older version are held until an owner or admin confirms each site. Existing manually added watches and their controls remain intact.

The Automations settings page can pause or resume weekly competitor checks without deleting saved sites. A check requested by the owner remains available while the weekly schedule is paused; a scheduled check already queued rechecks the pause before reading a site.

The same settings page can pause all scheduled specialist profiles in a workspace. Pausing prevents due profiles from being claimed and rechecks the setting immediately before a queued run starts. Owners can still start profiles manually; after resuming, profiles already due may run on the next scheduler check.

When a social draft uses a current trend or meme format, its review details retain the exact source URL, date, and source-reported observation. ClimbOS requires the fields to match a dated record from the current research run and rejects future or over-30-day-old signals as current. The 30-day limit is a product validation rule; it does not describe how long trends universally last. The source statement remains source-reported, not proof that the idea fits the audience or will perform.

Growth-run experience timing is recorded from the existing workflow: the first finding timestamp is set only when a quick-take opportunity links to confirmed business facts, and the first-action timestamp is set when an owner starts a linked plan action. Hypotheses and generic progress previews do not count as findings. These timestamps measure workflow response and owner follow-through; they do not measure business growth. Run completion and actual AI/research usage remain available from the existing run and usage records. Starting an action is not evidence that an external change was published, sent, or completed.

The operator console summarizes first-confirmed-finding and first-owner-action time only when at least five non-fixture strategy runs have the relevant timestamp. It also shows current task statuses for runs started in the last 30 days and separates recorded from estimated research spend. These are operational measurements, not proof of customer or business impact; capped windows and small samples stay visible.

`shared/agents/ownerLanguage.ts` supplies the same everyday-language guidance to the contextual assistant and the agent runtime. The owner-brevity skill and generated registry have matching guidance. The change applies to owner-facing explanations, preserves technical field names, source labels, uncertainty, and permissions, and keeps requested publishable copy subject to its channel requirements. Backend instruction changes require the normal backend release process to take effect outside local tests.

Verification lives in `tests/unit/app-navigation.test.ts`, `tests/unit/onboarding-language.test.ts`, the parser regressions, and `e2e/simple-app.spec.ts`. Browser checks use synthetic demo data and the design-system specimen; they do not perform live customer sends, publishing, ad activation, or paid AI generation. Authenticated owner onboarding is covered by rendering and permission regressions, rather than creating real additional customer companies during UI checks.


## Verification on 25 October 2026

- Full suite: `pnpm exec vitest run --maxWorkers=2` — 201 files, 1,503 tests passed. The first unrestricted run hit 5-second timeouts under parallel load; reducing workers resolved them without weakening assertions or changing timeouts.
- Focused onboarding, navigation, parser, and permission checks: 33 passed after the later wording changes.
- Browser: `E2E_BASE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:3000 pnpm exec playwright test e2e/simple-app.spec.ts --project=desktop --project=mobile` — all 4 passed, including accessibility scans, draft preservation in both chat paths, local tool search, existing editor links, and the loaded review-section scroll/focus handoff.
- Type checks and the app lint checks passed. No live customer publishing, outreach, ad activation, or paid AI generation was used for verification.
- `pnpm build` was blocked by a Turbopack CSS worker's port-binding restriction (`EPERM`) in this environment. The supported Webpack fallback compiled and completed TypeScript checks, but does not produce the existing Tailwind utility styling with the current Turbopack-only loader setup. It is not a verified production artifact. The existing build configuration was retained, and visual/browser verification used the styled development app.
- The local frontend is available with `pnpm dev --hostname 127.0.0.1`. Backend instruction changes remain source changes until the normal backend release process is performed. No deployment was performed here.
