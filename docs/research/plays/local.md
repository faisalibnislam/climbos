# Local business growth plays

Segment: owner-operated local businesses. That covers home services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, cleaning, landscaping), local food and beverage (bakeries, cafes, restaurants, caterers), fitness studios and gyms, salons and spas, clinics (dental, physio, vet), local retail, and professional local services (accountants, lawyers, real estate).

Researched 2026-10-17. 40 plays, ranked by predictability x leverage.

## How to read this library

- **Predictability (P):** how reliably the play produces a result when executed competently. H = mechanism is well documented and results repeat across businesses; M = works for most fits but varies by market/offer; L = high variance, hit-driven.
- **Leverage (L):** size of the upside relative to owner effort and cash. H = compounding asset or recurring revenue; M = solid one-off lift; L = small.
- **Evidence grade (E):** A = primary source (regulator, platform policy, peer-reviewed study); B = large vendor dataset with stated sample (BrightLocal, Klaviyo, ezCater, ANA); C = practitioner/vendor claims, small samples, anecdotes. Plays whose core result rests only on grade C are tagged **[WEAK EVIDENCE]**.
- Every number carries a source URL or the label "practitioner estimate". Vendor numbers are labelled as vendor numbers. Most published local-marketing statistics come from companies selling the channel, so treat ranges as planning priors and replace them with the owner's own data after the first 4–8 weeks.
- Research limit: the shared web-search budget ran out before every figure could be traced to its primary report (for example the CallRail and Invoca missed-call benchmarks and the SearchLight LSA benchmark, all cited here through secondary write-ups). Those are flagged "secondary".

## Cross-cutting compliance rules (apply to every play)

1. **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465; final August 2024, effective October 2024).** It bans fake reviews (including AI-written ones), incentives conditioned on a particular sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression by threats or intimidation, and buying fake followers or views. Civil penalties are available against knowing violators; one law-firm alert put the 2024 figure at $51,744 per violation, adjusted for inflation each year. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials , https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/10/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-fake-reviews-testimonials
2. **Google review policy is stricter than the FTC.** Google bans *any* incentive for a review (discounts, free goods), even a sentiment-neutral one. It also bans selectively soliciting positive reviews ("review gating"), discouraging negative reviews, requiring or pressuring customers to review while on the premises, and setting staff quotas for reviews. Source: https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114
3. **TCPA and SMS.** Marketing texts sent with an autodialer or platform need prior express written consent. The FCC "one-to-one consent" rule was vacated by the 11th Circuit in January 2025, but carriers and texting platforms may still require it contractually. Federal quiet hours are 8am–9pm recipient-local. Florida's FTSA is stricter: 8am–8pm, a cap of 3 messages per 24h (scope debated), and $500–$1,500 per message in damages. Other state mini-TCPAs exist (OK, MD, WA and more). Sources: https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule , https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2025/1/delayed-one-to-one-consent-rule-gives-companies-reprieve-plus-other-tcpa-updates/ , https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/florida-expands-telemarketing-laws , https://docs.bird.com/applications/help-and-reference/deliverability-and-compliance/united-states-sms-mms-compliance/us-state-level-laws-and-regulations
4. **CAN-SPAM** applies to every commercial email: accurate headers and subject lines, a physical postal address, and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days. Penalties are per email; secondary sources cite $53,088 after the January 2025 inflation adjustment. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business , https://www.compliancepoint.com/marketing-compliance/inflation-hits-ftc-penalty-amounts/
5. **FTC Endorsement Guides.** Any creator who receives a free meal, service or payment must disclose it clearly in the post itself, not behind "more". The FTC treats tags like #hosted or #comped as unclear. The business should never ask a creator to hide the arrangement. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers , https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/influencing-the-influencers-ftc-updates-its-endor
6. **Healthcare (dental, physio, vet, medical spa):** HIPAA limits the use of patient data in marketing and in replies to reviews; never confirm someone is a patient in a public reply. State dental and medical boards restrict testimonials and patient-referral inducements, and anti-kickback rules apply wherever federal payers are involved. Practitioner caution: have counsel review any referral incentive.

### 1. Compliant review-velocity engine
- id: review-velocity-engine
- fits: every local business with at least ~20 completed jobs, visits or transactions a month and a Google Business Profile. Prerequisites: a customer list with phone or email, and a short direct review link (GBP > "Ask for reviews").
- why it works: Reviews can affect discovery and buying decisions. Consumers now filter hard on recency and rating: 74% look for reviews written in the last 3 months, and the share who will only use 4.5+ star businesses rose from 17% to 31% (BrightLocal 2026). Many surveyed consumers said they are open to reviewing a business, while fewer reported actually doing so in the prior year; invite customers neutrally rather than selecting by satisfaction.
- steps:
  1. Generate the GBP short review link and a QR code; put them on invoices, receipts, the job-completion text and the counter card.
  2. Send **every** customer the same ask, positive or not, by SMS or email within 2–24 hours of service. No "how did we do?" pre-screen that routes unhappy people elsewhere: that is review gating and breaks Google policy.
  3. Use two touches maximum: the ask at T+2h, one reminder at T+3 days, then stop.
  4. Train staff to make a verbal ask at handoff ("we'd really value an honest Google review; I'll text you the link"). Don't hand over a tablet or wait while they write: Google bans pressuring a customer to review on premises.
  5. Choose one eligible public destination for each campaign and use the same neutral invitation and destination for every eligible recipient, regardless of feedback, sentiment, or predicted rating. Consider a different platform only in a separate campaign when the business and customer evidence support it (Google first; Yelp forbids solicitation, so only add "find us on Yelp" signage; Facebook; Healthgrades/Zocdoc for clinics; Houzz for remodelers).
  6. Track weekly: requests sent, reviews received, average stars, days since the last review.
  7. Ask the customer to mention the specific service and neighborhood in their own words ("tell others what we did for you"), without scripting the content.
  8. Feed new reviews into the website as an embedded widget that shows *all* reviews, not a hand-picked set (FTC suppression rule).
- cost & effort: $0–$60/month for review-request software, or $0 if ClimbOS sends it. Setup takes 2–3 hours; then about 15 min/week.
- time to first result: 1–7 days for the first reviews; 4–8 weeks for a visible change in rating and recency.
- expected results: Automated email asks get about 8–15% response for service businesses (vendor data: https://fera.ai/blog/posts/average-response-rate-for-review-requests , https://focus-digital.co/average-review-response-rate/ ). SMS plus a verbal ask is typically 15–30% (practitioner estimate). In-person requests are far more effective than emailed ones in compliance experiments (Bohns & Roganizad, reported at https://blog.grade.us/want-amazing-reviews-make-face-to-face-request ; the study measured general requests, not reviews). A one-star rise on Yelp was associated with 5–9% revenue for independent restaurants, with no effect on chains (Luca, HBS: https://harvardmagazine.com/node/33472 ). Example: 100 customers/month at an 18% response gives about 18 new reviews/month (practitioner estimate).
- leading indicators: request-to-review conversion %, days since the last review (target under 7), share of reviews mentioning a service keyword, average star rating of new reviews.
- risks & compliance: No incentives of any kind for Google reviews (Google policy); the FTC allows sentiment-neutral incentives on some platforms, but Google does not. No gating, no staff quotas, no family or employee reviews without disclosure, no AI-written reviews. In healthcare, never prompt patients to share treatment details. Yelp prohibits asking for reviews outright. Sending review asks by text needs at least transactional consent; keep them non-promotional, or hold marketing consent.
- ClimbOS can automate: generate the review link and QR code; send the T+2h and T+3d asks via SMS/email from the job or POS feed; build a policy-compliant template library; run the dashboard (velocity, recency, rating); alert the owner when there has been no review for 7 days; draft reply suggestions (see play 7). Owner tasks: the verbal ask, staff training, connecting the POS or CRM.
- sources: https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-consumer-review-survey/ , https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/ , https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/ , https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114 , https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials , https://harvardmagazine.com/node/33472 , https://whitespark.ca/blog/the-most-underrated-local-ranking-factor-in-2025/

### 2. Speed-to-lead + missed-call text-back
- id: speed-to-lead-missed-call
- fits: home services, clinics, salons, law and accounting firms, gyms, caterers: any business where the first inbound contact is a call, form or LSA lead. Prerequisites: a business line on a VoIP/forwarding system that can trigger texts.
- why it works: Leads decay in minutes. Unanswered callers go straight to the next business on the map pack. An instant text ("Sorry we missed you, what do you need? Here's a booking link") keeps the lead warm until a human calls back.
- steps:
  1. Measure your baseline for one week: calls received, calls answered by a human, average callback time.
  2. Turn on missed-call text-back on the business line (most VoIP/CRM tools have it; ClimbOS can do it via an SMS provider).
  3. Write a 2-line auto-text with the business name, an acknowledgement, a question, and a booking or quote link.
  4. Route web-form, LSA and marketplace leads into a single inbox with push alerts.
  5. Set a service level: human callback within 5 minutes during hours, first thing next morning after hours, with an after-hours auto-reply giving the time of callback.
  6. Add a voicemail script that pushes text as the faster channel.
  7. Report weekly on response time and booked rate by channel.
- cost & effort: $20–$100/month for the tooling; 2–4 hours of setup.
- time to first result: same day.
- expected results: Missed-call rates vary widely: CallRail's January 2025 small-business benchmark put home services at 14% missed, while Invoca's 2026 home-services benchmark found only 52% of inbound calls answered by a person (both secondary, via https://getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics and https://www.usecarly.com/blog/missed-call-statistics/ ). In CallRail's 2025 consumer survey, 78% had abandoned a business after an unanswered call and 21% immediately called another business (same secondary sources). The 2007 InsideSales/Oldroyd study found responding within 5 minutes vs 30 minutes raised the odds of qualifying a lead about 21x (vendor-sponsored study; https://www.retellai.com/blog/speed-to-lead , https://www.electronics-cooling.com/2011/06/increase-your-sales-effectiveness-by-21x-in-25-minutes/ ). Planning prior: recover 20–40% of previously missed calls as conversations (practitioner estimate).
- leading indicators: % calls answered live, median minutes to first response, missed-call texts replied to, booked jobs from recovered calls.
- risks & compliance: Replying by text to someone who just called you about a service is generally treated as a responsive, non-marketing message. Don't add them to promotional lists without written consent (TCPA). Honor STOP. Respect state quiet hours for automated follow-ups. Note that LSA ranking factors in responsiveness: Google says missed calls can hurt your LSA lead likelihood (https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en-uk ).
- ClimbOS can automate: missed-call text-back, unified lead inbox, AI first-reply drafting, response-time tracking, nudges to the owner's phone, weekly "leads you lost" report. Owner tasks: the callback itself and setting hours.
- sources: https://getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics , https://www.usecarly.com/blog/missed-call-statistics/ , https://www.retellai.com/blog/speed-to-lead , https://callingly.com/blog/state-of-speed-to-lead , https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en-uk

### 3. Dormant-customer reactivation sprint
- id: dormant-reactivation
- fits: any business with 12+ months of customer history: dental and vet recall, salons, gyms (lapsed members), HVAC (no tune-up in 18 months), cleaners, restaurants with an email/loyalty list, accountants (last year's filers who haven't booked).
- why it works: Past customers already trust you and need no persuasion that you're good. The lapse is usually inertia, not dissatisfaction. A personal, multi-touch nudge with a concrete reason to return (a recall, a season, a new service) converts at far higher rates than cold acquisition.
- steps:
  1. Export customers with their last-visit date; bucket them at 6–12, 12–18, 18–24 and 24+ months.
  2. Check consent per channel: email under CAN-SPAM is fine for existing customers with an opt-out; SMS needs prior written consent for marketing; phone calls are fine for existing relationships, but check DNC rules for non-customers.
  3. Write a "reason to come back" for each bucket (overdue cleaning, filter change before summer, "your stylist has openings Thursday").
  4. Run a 3–5 touch sequence over 14 days: personal email, then text if consented, then a phone call from a staff member, then a final email.
  5. Use a booking link with pre-filled details; offer two specific time slots rather than "call us".
  6. Remove responders and bookers automatically; flag anyone who is unhappy for the owner to call personally.
  7. Repeat quarterly for newly lapsed customers.
- cost & effort: $0–$200 in tooling; 4–6 hours of setup plus 1–2 hours of calling per week during the sprint.
- time to first result: 2–7 days.
- expected results: Dental vendor benchmarks give reactivation of 25–35% for patients dormant 6–12 months, 15–25% at 12–18 months, 10–15% at 18–24 months, and under 8% beyond that. A single text or email reactivates about 3–5%, versus 15–25% for a 3–5 touch multi-channel campaign (vendor data: https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-recall-reactivation-statistics-benchmarks , https://clerri.com/blog/dental-patient-reactivation-statistics/ ). Outside healthcare, plan on 5–15% of a 6–18-month lapsed list (practitioner estimate).
- leading indicators: list size per bucket, contact rate, booked rate per touch, revenue from reactivated customers.
- risks & compliance: SMS needs consent; CAN-SPAM for email; HIPAA for clinics (minimum necessary data, no PHI in subject lines). Don't reactivate customers who previously opted out.
- ClimbOS can automate: list bucketing from CSV/POS/CRM, sequence drafting per bucket, sending email/SMS, call lists with scripts for staff, tracking bookings and revenue. Owner tasks: the phone calls, approving offers.
- sources: https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-recall-reactivation-statistics-benchmarks , https://clerri.com/blog/dental-patient-reactivation-statistics/ , https://www.getviva.ai/increase-dental-recall-rates/ , https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

### 4. Rebook-before-they-leave
- id: rebook-before-leave
- fits: dental, physio, chiro, vet, salons and barbers, spas, massage, lawn/pool/cleaning recurring services, personal training.
- why it works: The cheapest booking is the one made while the customer is still in the chair. Prebooking turns intention into a calendar commitment and reduces reliance on reminder campaigns.
- steps:
  1. Define the default rebook interval per service (6-month hygiene, 6-week color, 4-week cut, quarterly pest).
  2. Add "book your next visit" to the checkout script; offer two specific slots.
  3. Show the prebook rate per staff member on a weekly board (not a quota with penalties: coach it).
  4. Send a confirmation with a calendar file, then reminders at 7 days and 1 day before.
  5. For people who decline, send a "pick your time" link 2 weeks before the ideal interval.
  6. Track no-shows; add a deposit or card-on-file for repeat no-show customers if the market tolerates it.
- cost & effort: $0 beyond the booking software; 1 hour of staff training.
- time to first result: immediate.
- expected results: Dental vendors report that patients who leave with an appointment already booked return at 80–90%, versus 35–45% for those who leave without one (vendor data, via https://www.getviva.ai/increase-dental-recall-rates/ and https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-recall-reactivation-statistics-benchmarks ). Average hygiene recall is about 60–70%, and well-run practices reach 85–95% (same sources). For salons, a 10–20 point lift in prebook rate within 60 days is a typical coaching result (practitioner estimate). **[WEAK EVIDENCE]** for exact magnitudes; the mechanism is robust.
- leading indicators: prebook % at checkout by staff member, no-show %, average days between visits.
- risks & compliance: Reminder texts for booked appointments are transactional; keep promotions out of them unless you have marketing consent. Healthcare: appointment reminders are permitted under HIPAA treatment communications; keep the content minimal.
- ClimbOS can automate: reminder and "pick your time" sequences, a prebook-rate dashboard by staff, checkout script cards. Owner tasks: coaching staff, setting the intervals.
- sources: https://www.getviva.ai/increase-dental-recall-rates/ , https://ainora.lt/blog/dental-recall-reactivation-statistics-benchmarks , https://resources.rework.com/vi/libraries/dental-clinic-growth/dental-recall-recare-systems

### 5. Maintenance membership / service plan
- id: maintenance-membership
- fits: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pest control, lawn and landscaping, pool, gutter and window cleaning, house cleaning, auto detailing, vet (wellness plans), dental (in-house membership for uninsured patients), car washes, gyms. Prerequisite: a capacity plan for the shoulder seasons.
- why it works: A membership converts one-off customers into recurring revenue, fills slow-season schedules with tune-ups, gives the technician a reason to be in the home (where repair and replacement opportunities are found), and creates priority-service loyalty when systems fail.
- steps:
  1. Design one plan: 2 visits/year plus priority scheduling, waived diagnostic fee, and a 10–15% repair discount. Price it so the visits alone break even.
  2. Bill monthly (e.g., $19–$29/mo) as well as annually; monthly lowers the friction.
  3. Train technicians to offer the plan at the end of every paid call, showing the customer's own savings ("today's diagnostic would have been free").
  4. Put the plan on the invoice, the website, and the post-job text.
  5. Auto-schedule member visits in the shoulder seasons (spring and fall for HVAC).
  6. Send a renewal touch 30 days before expiry; auto-renew with consent and a clear cancellation path (FTC and state auto-renewal laws).
  7. Track the member census, attach rate, renewal rate and revenue per member.
- cost & effort: $0–$100/month in software; 6–10 hours of design and training.
- time to first result: 1–4 weeks to first sign-ups; 6–12 months for the full flywheel.
- expected results: Offering the plan at the end of a paid diagnostic call converts about 35–55%, versus 1–4% from cold calls or mailers (vendor: https://www.plyrium.com/academy/hvac/hvac-maintenance-agreements-pricing-2026 ). Typical residential pricing is $150–$500/yr, and $175–$350 for two tune-ups (https://pipelineon.com/blog/hvac-maintenance-agreements-recurring-revenue/ ). Maintenance gross margins are cited at 55–65% versus 35–45% for repairs (https://www.applausehq.com/blog/hvac-membership-programs ). One model gives three-year revenue of about $1,640 per member household versus $480 for non-members (Plyrium; vendor). Renewal benchmark is about 86% (secondary: https://www.webtonic.io/blog/hvac-lifecycle-and-retention-marketing-statistics/ ). All figures are vendor-sourced; the ACCA census data is from 2016.
- leading indicators: plans offered per technician per week, attach rate, monthly net member adds, churn.
- risks & compliance: Auto-renewal laws (the FTC ROSCA and state laws such as California ARL) require clear terms, affirmative consent and easy cancellation. Don't oversell repairs at member visits: aggressive upsells drive bad reviews. Dental in-house plans must not be marketed as insurance; check state rules.
- ClimbOS can automate: plan pricing calculator, landing page, checkout/billing link, technician scripts, renewal and visit-scheduling reminders, census dashboard. Owner tasks: plan design sign-off, technician training, delivering visits.
- sources: https://www.plyrium.com/academy/hvac/hvac-maintenance-agreements-pricing-2026 , https://pipelineon.com/blog/hvac-maintenance-agreements-recurring-revenue/ , https://www.applausehq.com/blog/hvac-membership-programs , https://www.webtonic.io/blog/hvac-lifecycle-and-retention-marketing-statistics/

### 6. Google Business Profile foundation (post-Q&A era)
- id: gbp-foundation
- fits: all local businesses. Service-area businesses hide their address but must still verify.
- why it works: The Business Profile is the main storefront for local search: the 2026 Whitespark ranking-factor survey puts the primary GBP category, proximity and keywords in the business name at the top of local-pack factors. Completeness and photos lift engagement, and Gemini-powered "Ask Maps" now answers questions about the business from its reviews and profile content, replacing the Q&A feature Google removed in late 2025.
- steps:
  1. Pick the most specific primary category (e.g., "Furnace repair service" vs "HVAC contractor"; test it against the top 3 competitors). Add every relevant secondary category.
  2. Fill in every field: services with descriptions and prices, products, attributes (women-owned, wheelchair-accessible), hours including holiday hours, booking link, service areas.
  3. Upload 10+ real photos (team, vehicles, before/after, storefront, interior, menu items), then 2–4 new ones per week. No stock photos.
  4. Put a FAQ on the website (with FAQ schema) that answers what the old Q&A used to: parking, pricing range, insurance accepted, service radius. Ask Maps and AI assistants read site and review content.
  5. Post weekly GBP updates (offers, events, recent jobs) with a CTA button. Treat posts as conversion content, not a ranking lever.
  6. Make the business name, address and phone (NAP) identical across your website, Yelp, Apple Business Connect, Bing, Facebook and industry directories.
  7. Check the Performance tab monthly: calls, direction requests, website clicks, and search terms.
- cost & effort: $0. 3–5 hours of setup, then 30 min/week.
- time to first result: 2–8 weeks.
- expected results: Google's own statement, as quoted by agencies, is that businesses with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks than those without (https://www.mediagistic.com/blog/google-photo-insights-more-photos-more-clicks , https://www.dacgroup.com/insights/local-search-news/why-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-matter/ ). Weekly posters are said to see 42% more direction requests than monthly posters (secondary, attributed to SOCi; correlational: https://wisernotify.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/ ). Claims like "520% more calls with 100+ photos" have no primary source; ignore them.
- leading indicators: profile views, calls, direction requests, website clicks, and new photos added per week.
- risks & compliance: Keyword-stuffing the business name violates Google's guidelines and invites suspension, even though it ranks. Virtual offices and fake addresses lead to suspension. No offers that incentivize reviews.
- ClimbOS can automate: a profile audit against a checklist, category recommendations from competitor analysis, weekly post drafting, a photo-upload reminder pipeline (crew shoots, ClimbOS captions and uploads via API), website FAQ page plus schema, NAP consistency checks, monthly Performance report. Owner tasks: verification, taking photos.
- sources: https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/ , https://whitespark.ca/blog/the-most-underrated-local-ranking-factor-in-2025/ , https://ppc.land/google-quietly-kills-q-a-for-an-ai-button-most-wont-use/ , https://www.footbridgemedia.com/marketing-tips/google-shutting-down-gbp-q-a , https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-qa , https://www.mediagistic.com/blog/google-photo-insights-more-photos-more-clicks , https://wisernotify.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics/

### 7. Reply to every review, non-templated
- id: review-reply-system
- fits: all local businesses with reviews on Google, Yelp, Facebook, or industry sites.
- why it works: Replies are read by future customers, not just the reviewer. They show that the owner is present and handles problems well. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, 80% said they are more likely to use a business that replies to all its reviews, but generic or templated replies put off 50%.
- steps:
  1. Set a service level: reply within 48 hours, 7 days a week.
  2. For positive reviews, thank them by name, reference the specific detail they mentioned, and add one line of useful context (a service name or neighborhood, naturally).
  3. For negative reviews, acknowledge, don't argue, take it offline with a direct contact, and follow up publicly once resolved.
  4. Never disclose private details (healthcare: never confirm they are a patient).
  5. Escalate reviews that breach policy (fake, competitor, off-topic) through GBP's review management tool. Don't threaten the reviewer: that is FTC review suppression.
  6. Review monthly themes and fix the operational causes.
- cost & effort: $0; 10–20 min/week.
- time to first result: immediate trust effect; measurable in conversion rate over 1–3 months.
- expected results: 80% more likely to use a business that replies to all reviews; 50% put off by templated replies (BrightLocal 2026: https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/ ). In 2025, 89% expected owners to respond to both positive and negative reviews (https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/ ). Direct revenue lift has not been isolated in public data.
- leading indicators: reply rate %, median reply time, number of negative reviews resolved or updated.
- risks & compliance: FTC suppression rule: no legal threats or intimidation to remove reviews. HIPAA in healthcare. Don't offer refunds in exchange for deleting a review.
- ClimbOS can automate: monitoring reviews across platforms, drafting a unique, specific reply per review for one-tap owner approval, escalation alerts for negatives, a monthly theme report. Owner tasks: approving replies and handling resolutions.

### 10. Double-sided referral program with tracking
- id: referral-program
- fits: all, especially high-ticket or high-trust services (dental, HVAC replacement, roofing, cleaning, salons, accountants, gyms).
- why it works: Referred customers arrive pre-trusted and match the referrer's profile, so they churn less. A Wharton/Goethe study at a German bank found referred customers had 16–25% higher lifetime value, driven by higher margins and retention.
- steps:
  1. Choose a reward that fits the business and the law: service credit for both sides (e.g., $50 off for the friend and $50 credit for the referrer) or a charity donation per referral.
  2. Give every customer a personal link or code (and a printable card) at the moment of delight: job completion, the first great result, or a 5-star review.
  3. Make the ask specific: "Who on your street has an older furnace?" works better than "Know anyone?".
  4. Thank the referrer within 24 hours of the friend booking, with a handwritten note for high-ticket jobs.
  5. Publish a leaderboard or "referral of the month" internally (no public personal data).
  6. Report monthly: referral share of new customers, referral conversion rate, reward cost per acquired customer.
- cost & effort: the reward budget (typically 5–15% of first-job value; practitioner estimate); 3–4 hours of setup.
- time to first result: 2–6 weeks.
- expected results: Referred customers' customer lifetime value was 16–25% higher (Schmitt, Skiera & Van den Bulte; https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/05/Schmitt_Skiera_VandenBulte_2013_Referrral_Programs_2.pdf , https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/VdB-et-al.-JMR-2018.pdf ). For participation, expect 2–8% of active customers to refer at least once a year in an always-on program, and more with a time-boxed push (practitioner estimate). HVAC referral rewards commonly run $50–$75 for service and $100–$150 for equipment installs (consultant: https://christopholivierconsulting.com/?p=7178 ).
- leading indicators: links shared, referral visits, referral bookings, referrer repeat rate.
- risks & compliance: Healthcare: paying for patient referrals can breach state dental and medical board rules and federal anti-kickback law if Medicare or Medicaid is involved. Use thank-you gifts of nominal value or charity donations, and check the state board. Real estate: RESPA restricts referral fees for settlement services. Referrers who post their codes publicly must disclose the reward (FTC Endorsement Guides). Never tie the reward to leaving a review.
- ClimbOS can automate: link and code generation, referral card design, post-job ask messages, attribution tracking, reward fulfilment reminders, thank-you note drafts. Owner tasks: choosing the reward, handwritten notes.
- sources: https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/05/Schmitt_Skiera_VandenBulte_2013_Referrral_Programs_2.pdf , https://www.ama.org/2019/11/20/maximizing-the-benefits-of-customer-referrals/ , https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/VdB-et-al.-JMR-2018.pdf , https://christopholivierconsulting.com/?p=7178

### 11. Pre-stamped loyalty card (endowed progress)
- id: endowed-progress-loyalty
- fits: cafes, bakeries, car washes, barbers and salons, smoothie and juice bars, pet groomers, dry cleaners, quick-service restaurants: anything bought repeatedly.
- why it works: People persist toward goals they feel they have already started. In Nunes and Drèze's car-wash field experiment, a 10-stamp card with 2 free stamps produced 34% completion versus 19% for an equivalent 8-stamp card starting blank, even though both needed 8 purchases. Giving a reason for the bonus strengthens the effect.
- steps:
  1. Convert the existing "buy 9, get the 10th free" card to "buy 12, get 1 free" with 2–3 pre-applied stamps ("Welcome bonus: 2 stamps on us for joining").
  2. Give a reason for the endowment (opening week, your birthday month, neighborhood welcome).
  3. Go digital (POS loyalty or a phone-number lookup) to capture contact data with consent.
  4. Add a "you're 2 away" nudge message when the customer is close to the reward.
  5. Reset the new card with 1 bonus stamp after redemption.
  6. Measure the completion rate and visit frequency against the old card for 90 days.
- cost & effort: $0–$50/month (paper cards or POS add-on); 1–2 hours.
- time to first result: 2–6 weeks.
- expected results: 34% vs 19% completion in the original study, about 79% relative lift (https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-lowdown-on-customer-loyalty-programs-which-are-the-most-effective-and-why/ , https://thinkinsights.net/consulting/endowed-progress-effect ). The original study had a small sample (about 300 cards) and has been widely replicated in lab settings.
- leading indicators: cards issued, stamps per card at 30 days, completion rate, visit frequency of members vs non-members.
- risks & compliance: Loyalty-program SMS needs marketing consent; terms should be clear (expiry, non-transferable); some states regulate expiry of stored value. Don't tie stamps to reviews.
- ClimbOS can automate: card design (print or digital), the "you're close" nudge, cohort analysis vs the baseline, terms drafting. Owner tasks: POS setup and staff habits.
- sources: https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-lowdown-on-customer-loyalty-programs-which-are-the-most-effective-and-why/ , https://thinkinsights.net/consulting/endowed-progress-effect , https://siliconcanals.com/t-car-wash-loyalty-cards-endowed-progress/

### 20. Compliant SMS VIP club
- id: sms-vip-club
- fits: restaurants, cafes, bakeries (daily specials, sell-out alerts), salons (last-minute openings), gyms (class openings), retail (drops), seasonal home services (tune-up reminders).
- why it works: SMS is read fast, and "flash" uses (today's leftovers 30% off at 5pm, a 2pm cancellation slot) turn perishable inventory into revenue. Opt-in at the counter builds an owned audience that doesn't depend on algorithms.
- steps:
  1. Choose an SMS platform with registered 10DLC or toll-free verification (carrier requirement).
  2. Collect consent: a keyword ("text CROISSANT to 12345") or a web form with explicit consent language (what, how often, "msg & data rates may apply", "reply STOP").
  3. Promise a specific benefit: early access, member-only items, last-minute openings.
  4. Send 2–6 messages/month, at most; each must be useful and time-bound.
  5. Use automated flows (birthday, win-back at 60 days, "slot just opened") alongside campaigns.
  6. Respect quiet hours (8am–9pm federal; 8am–8pm in Florida, with its 3/24h cap) and honor STOP instantly.
  7. Track revenue per message and opt-out rate; prune frequency if opt-outs exceed about 2% per send (practitioner estimate).
- cost & effort: $20–$150/month in platform and carrier fees; 1–2 hours/week.
- time to first result: 1–2 weeks after reaching 200+ subscribers.
- expected results: Klaviyo bands a "good" SMS campaign click rate at 8.9–14.5% (https://www.klaviyo.com/uk/marketing-resources/sms-benchmarks-by-industry ). Omnisend's 2025 data (246M sends) reports a 12.39% SMS campaign CTR vs 0.74% for email (https://www.omnisend.com/blog/sms-marketing-benchmarks/ ). Automated flows are about 7.6% of SMS sends but 45.2% of SMS revenue (Klaviyo, via https://www.clickminded.com/sms-marketing-benchmarks/ ). These are ecommerce-heavy benchmarks; local results vary.
- leading indicators: new opt-ins/week, CTR, redemptions per send, opt-out rate.
- risks & compliance: TCPA prior express written consent for marketing texts; one-to-one consent vacated federally, but carriers may enforce it; Florida FTSA (8am–8pm, 3 per 24h, $500–$1,500 per message), Oklahoma and other state mini-TCPAs; carrier 10DLC registration; SHAFT content rules (sex, hate, alcohol, firearms, tobacco) limit alcohol and cannabis promotions, which need age gating. Never import contacts that didn't opt in to SMS.
- ClimbOS can automate: consent-compliant signup forms and counter signage, keyword setup, message drafting, quiet-hour and state-rule enforcement by area code/ZIP, flow automation, revenue attribution. Owner tasks: deciding offers, counter promotion.
- sources: https://www.klaviyo.com/uk/marketing-resources/sms-benchmarks-by-industry , https://www.omnisend.com/blog/sms-marketing-benchmarks/ , https://www.clickminded.com/sms-marketing-benchmarks/ , https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/eleventh-circuit-vacates-tcpa-11-consent-rule , https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/florida-expands-telemarketing-laws , https://docs.bird.com/applications/help-and-reference/deliverability-and-compliance/united-states-sms-mms-compliance/us-state-level-laws-and-regulations

### 38. BNI / chamber / professional networking
- id: networking-groups
- fits: relationship-driven services: accountants, lawyers, insurance, mortgage, realtors, contractors, cleaners, IT support, printers, florists, caterers, photographers.
- why it works: A weekly group of non-competing professionals acts as a referral sales force; chamber membership adds credibility signals, directory links and event access. Results depend heavily on attendance and on giving referrals first.
- steps:
  1. Visit 2–3 chapters or chambers as a guest; pick the one with members who serve your customers (realtors, property managers, financial advisers).
  2. Commit for 6–12 months; attend every meeting.
  3. Prepare a "specific ask" each week ("intro to property managers with 50+ doors").
  4. Run 1-to-1 meetings with the 5 most relevant members in the first 60 days.
  5. Give referrals first; track given vs received.
  6. Use chamber listings, ribbon cuttings and member-to-member deals; ask the chamber to feature you in their newsletter.
  7. Review at 6 months: closed revenue vs dues and hours.
- cost & effort: BNI dues and chamber membership typically run several hundred to a few thousand dollars a year (practitioner estimate); 2–4 hours/week.
- time to first result: 1–3 months.
- expected results: BNI's self-reported data: members received an average of 46 referrals in the prior year; $20.6B in member revenue from 13.5M referrals in 12 months (https://www.bni.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/BNI-SHARES-WHY-REFERRALS-ARE-THE-MOST-POWERFUL-WAY-TO-GROW-A-SUCCESSFUL-BUSINESS_2.20.23_Revised-Final.pdf ). The Schapiro Group chamber study is quoted in chamber materials as showing consumers 63–80% more likely to buy from chamber members (inconsistent secondary citations: https://businessvictorharbor.com.au/blog/the-schapiro-study-the-value-of-chamber-memberships ). **[WEAK EVIDENCE]**: self-reported and inconsistent.
- leading indicators: 1-to-1s per month, referrals given and received, referral-to-close %, revenue attributed vs dues.
- risks & compliance: RESPA if real-estate settlement services exchange referral fees; professional rules for lawyers (no paying for referrals except via approved services) and for healthcare.
- ClimbOS can automate: shortlisting chapters and chambers, member mapping to ideal referral partners, 1-to-1 prep sheets, weekly ask drafts, referral tracking and ROI. Owner tasks: attending.

### 39. Rented demand: lead marketplaces and daily deals (with guardrails)
- id: rented-demand-guardrails
- fits: Marketplaces: home services and pros on Thumbtack, Angi, Bark, Houzz, Yelp Ads. Daily deals: restaurants, spas, salons, fitness, experiences (Groupon, Living Social successors). Use as a capacity-filler, not a foundation.
- why it works: Marketplaces have demand on tap, so you can fill a schedule quickly. But leads are shared, prices are auction-driven, and the platform owns the customer relationship. Deep-discount deals bring trial, but repeat rates are low and many businesses lose money.
- steps:
  1. Set a hard target cost per booked job (e.g., ≤ 15% of the first-job gross margin) before spending.
  2. Choose only categories and job types with high ticket or high repeat potential; exclude low-value jobs.
  3. Respond in under 5 minutes (play 2); shared leads go to the fastest.
  4. On the first job, convert the customer to direct: maintenance plan, review, referral card, your own booking line.
  5. For daily deals: cap the number of vouchers, limit to off-peak days, require a new-customer-only rule, and price so the deal itself breaks even.
  6. Review monthly: cost per booked job, close rate, repeat rate; cut channels above target for 2 months running.
  7. Avoid long auto-renew contracts; read cancellation terms.
- cost & effort: Thumbtack about $25–$75 per lead for most trades; Angi $15–$100+ plus an annual fee (https://superdupr.com/blog/is-thumbtack-worth-it ); Bark about $20–$70 per lead in the US (https://whito.co.uk/trades/tools/bark-cost-uk/ ; credits now expire after 3 months); Yelp Ads from $150/month, billed per click (https://www.webfx.com/blog/ppc/yelp-advertising-costs/ ). Daily deals usually split revenue with the platform; terms vary.
- time to first result: days.
- expected results: HomeAdvisor claims contractors close about 13–18% of shared leads; others use 5–10% (https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/angi-leads-for-contractors ). One vendor reports cost per booked job of $250 for Thumbtack and $542 for Angi vs $168 for LSA (unverified: https://superdupr.com/blog/is-thumbtack-worth-it ). Daily deals: Dholakia's surveys (Rice University) found 55.5–61.5% of businesses profitable from a deal, with only about 20% of deal users becoming repeat customers; restaurants and bars (44%) and cleaning services (27%) had the lowest profitability (https://chiefmarketer.com/daily-deals-sustainable-for-13-of-businesses-study , https://www.technologyreview.com/2010/10/25/199737/social-coupons-good-for-business/ ).
- leading indicators: response time, lead-to-booked %, cost per booked job, % converted to a direct relationship.
- risks & compliance: Angi contracts reportedly auto-renew with early-cancellation penalties (competitor characterization). Don't solicit platform reviews off-platform in breach of terms. For deals, state gift-card law may make the paid value non-expiring. Healthcare discounts follow board rules.
- ClimbOS can automate: a unified inbox for marketplace leads, instant responses, cost-per-booked-job reporting across all channels, "convert to direct" follow-up sequences, deal-capacity calculator. Owner tasks: quoting and doing the work.

### 40. Vehicle wrap and "Best of" awards (low-priority signals)
- id: vehicle-wrap-awards
- fits: Wraps: home services with vehicles that are on the road daily in the service area (HVAC, plumbing, pest, cleaning, landscaping, mobile groomers, food trucks). Awards: businesses in markets with a legitimate newspaper or community "Readers' Choice" contest.
- why it works: A wrap turns daily routes into repeated local impressions and signals legitimacy at the customer's curb. A legitimate local award is a trust badge for the website and GBP and a press hook. Both are brand signals with weak direct attribution, so do them after the higher-ranked plays.
- steps:
  1. Wrap: put three things on it in large type: what you do, one phone number or short URL, and a trust cue (stars, "licensed & insured"). Use a unique phone number on the vehicle to track calls.
  2. Park wrapped vehicles visibly at job sites and in high-traffic spots after hours (where legal).
  3. Awards: find legitimate local contests (published by the newspaper or community outlet itself); note the nomination window.
  4. Tell customers you're nominated, by email, SMS (consented), social and counter signage. Ask for votes, not reviews, and never trade incentives for votes if the contest rules prohibit it.
  5. If you win, add the badge to the website, GBP photos, vehicles, invoices.
  6. Ignore unsolicited "you won" emails asking for a plaque or "media package" fee: the BBB reports more than 1,300 award-scam reports since 2015.
- cost & effort: Wraps $2,500–$5,000 per vehicle (practitioner estimate). Awards $0 (legitimate contests don't charge winners). 2–6 hours.
- time to first result: wraps 1–3 months; awards on the contest cycle.
- expected results: Vendor claims of 30,000–70,000 daily impressions per wrapped vehicle are modeled marketing estimates, not measured (https://www.adquick.com/car-wrap-advertising , https://gilroyblackout.com/blog/vehicle-graphics-roi-cost-per-impression-analysis/ ). No measured revenue effect of local awards found. **[WEAK EVIDENCE]** for both.
- leading indicators: calls to the vehicle-only number; for awards, vote-drive participation and website trust-badge clicks.
- risks & compliance: Some HOAs and cities restrict commercial vehicle parking. Contractors' license numbers are legally required on vehicles in some states. Award scams: verify the publisher before paying anything. Vote incentives may breach contest rules.
- ClimbOS can automate: wrap design concepts, a tracking-number setup, legitimate contest discovery with deadlines, vote-drive messages, scam checks on award emails, badge placement. Owner tasks: wrap purchase.
- sources: https://www.adquick.com/car-wrap-advertising , https://www.plumbermag.com/editorial/2018/12/wrapping-a-vehicle-can-be-an-easy-way-to-advertise , https://gilroyblackout.com/blog/vehicle-graphics-roi-cost-per-impression-analysis/ , https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/cambridge-business-owner-warns-about-award-vanity-scam/ , https://theitem.com/stories/beware-of-best-of-sumter-scammers-asking-for-payment,415307 , https://www.surreynowleader.com/business/are-you-the-best-of-surrey-north-delta-beware-of-online-sales/
