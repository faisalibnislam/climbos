## How do I get a photo or screenshot under an upload limit?
Open Diet Image, drop in the images or a whole folder, and set the size you need: its page says you can fit a limit in kilobytes or megabytes, or convert to another format such as WebP.

You can drop files on the window or on the Dock icon. Diet Image keeps a new version only when it is valid and actually smaller, so a file that can't shrink is not swapped for a worse one. The page lists nine supported formats, including the common photo and web ones, and says animated GIF and WebP files stay animated.

## Will smaller images look worse?
That depends on the preset, and Diet Image has three: Lossless leaves pixels alone, Balanced trims gently, and Smallest compresses hard.

If the picture must not change, choose Lossless. If you need a smaller file, try the gentler Balanced setting first and move to Smallest only if the file is still too big. The Diet Image page says every output is decoded and checked on its own before it is accepted.

## Can I remove location data before I share photos?
Yes, according to its page, Diet Image lets you keep or remove location, camera details, comments and copyright separately.

That means you can strip the location from a photo you are about to post and still keep the copyright line.

## How do I shrink a PDF or a scan?
Use DietPDF: its page describes it as built for getting a PDF under an email or upload limit and for shrinking scans and image-heavy documents.

It says you see exactly what changed before you save, and it can keep a folder of incoming PDFs small without you thinking about it.

## How do I make a video small enough to send?
Use Transcode, which its page says converts video, audio and images on Apple's own engines and lets you trim and clean up a clip first.

The collection page also lists opening files macOS won't play on its own, and pulling the audio out of a video or putting an audiobook together.
