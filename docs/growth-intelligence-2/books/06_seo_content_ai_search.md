# Book 06 — SEO, Content Strategy, and AI Search

## 1. Search as a business system

Search connects an existing question to a useful destination. A sustainable search strategy therefore starts with the question, the person asking it, and the business's legitimate ability to help. Keywords describe some of that demand; they are not the demand itself. A business can rank for a popular phrase while attracting people it cannot serve. Another business can earn profitable customers from a small set of precise questions that mainstream keyword tools barely register.

The objective for ClimbOS is to build a chain from observed demand to relevant content to a credible next step to a measured commercial outcome. Search impressions, rankings, and citations in generated answers are intermediate observations. They are useful when they reveal whether that chain works. They should never replace revenue quality, customer suitability, or sustainable contribution as the final purpose of the program.

Consider a hypothetical specialist software company selling to architects. An article about attractive buildings might generate visits from students and tourists. A documented guide to exchanging a specific drawing format might attract fewer readers but help practicing architects evaluate whether the product solves their immediate problem. Neither topic is automatically right. The choice depends on the product's verified capabilities, buying process, and strategic position.

Throughout this book, examples and numerical scenarios are hypothetical unless attributed. The frameworks are proposed operating methods for ClimbOS, not disclosed search-engine scoring systems. Search providers do not promise indexing or rankings simply because a site implements recommended practices.

## 2. What changed by October 2026

The live Google guide dated July 10, 2026 continues to position generative search within foundational SEO. It rejects special Google visibility benefits from llms.txt, artificial content chunking, or a special AI schema. It also links a dedicated Generative AI performance report and a Search Console inclusion control. These are material updates to older advice. [Google generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).

An older official AI-features page still describes AI traffic within the overall Web performance report. ClimbOS should preserve that difference in documentation detail, inspect the actual connected property's available reports, and label imported fields by their true report and date. Do not silently assume that every account or API exposes newer reporting identically. [Google AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features); [Generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139); [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024).

The report documentation says the worldwide rollout completed August 31, 2026, and describes impression reporting, not a standalone AI-revenue attribution system. A report may be absent when impressions are insufficient. Its downloaded zeros can also represent unavailable values, so ingestion must preserve that ambiguity. The inclusion control is distinct from AI-training controls. [Report details](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139); [Control details](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024).

The practical response is capability discovery rather than a hardcoded marketing promise. During connection, record available reports, export routes, permissions, and regional behavior. Where only manual export is possible, accept that limitation. A dashboard can truthfully distinguish observed AI visibility, overall search performance, third-party monitoring, and unknown coverage.

This book's AI-search recommendations emphasize identifiable expertise, useful information, technical access, and commercial measurement. These are decisions under uncertainty, not a claim that every answer engine uses Google's systems. Research each additional provider separately before applying crawling controls or reporting assumptions across platforms.

## 3. Diagnose the binding constraint

Before commissioning content, identify which part of the search system is constrained. There are six common constraints: insufficient suitable demand, inaccessible pages, weak relevance, inadequate evidence, poor conversion, and unattractive economics. A site may have several, but the next investment should address the one that currently limits results most strongly.

Use a sequential diagnosis. First, establish whether potential customers actually ask related questions. Next, verify that the important destinations can be reached and understood. Then inspect whether their content answers the real task. After that, assess whether readers have a compelling and credible next action. Finally, connect the resulting customers to retention and contribution. Do not spend weeks improving article formatting when the destination promises a feature the product does not provide.

A hypothetical business receives 2,000 relevant search visits monthly but only four qualified inquiries. More impressions may help, yet the immediate opportunity could be clearer scope, a credible example, or a shorter inquiry process. Another business receives ten qualified inquiries from only 100 relevant visits. Its next constraint may be visibility rather than conversion.

ClimbOS should output the diagnosis, evidence, confidence, and cheapest useful verification step. When analytics are absent, the next step is instrumentation plus a small qualitative review, not an invented funnel. A useful recommendation can say that conversion remains unknown and specify how the owner can resolve that uncertainty.

## 4. Build a demand and intent map

Organize demand around customer situations. Capture the triggering problem, desired outcome, current workaround, alternatives considered, purchase constraints, and urgency. Then identify queries that express those situations. Interviews, support requests, sales objections, internal search, and connected Search Console data provide complementary signals. Each source has selection bias: support logs represent existing customers, while search reports omit some demand and may underrepresent a new category.

A practical map separates learning, doing, evaluating, and buying. Learning content helps a person understand a problem. Doing content helps accomplish a task. Evaluating content supports comparison and risk reduction. Buying content explains availability, fit, cost, and next steps. These categories can overlap within one query, so inspect actual results and customer language rather than forcing every phrase into a rigid funnel stage.

For each opportunity, store a concise intent statement: “A project manager needs to collect missing information before a deadline and is comparing a reusable process with manual reminders.” That statement is more useful to an author than a bare keyword. It also reveals where an answer should avoid a product pitch because the reader first needs a procedure.

Group synonymous queries into one task when one useful page can satisfy them. Split only when audiences need materially different workflows, evidence, or destinations. The resulting information architecture should reflect useful distinctions, not every spelling variation returned by a tool.

## 5. Prioritize with uncertainty and opportunity cost

A proposed prioritization formula is: expected incremental contribution equals incremental qualified visits multiplied by activation rate, paid conversion rate, and contribution per new customer, less creation, maintenance, and distribution costs. Every quantity should use an explicit period. If contribution is measured over six months, do not compare it with annual content expense without explaining the mismatch.

Suppose a hypothetical article is expected to attract 300 additional suitable visits over six months. Assume 8% activate, 10% of activations become paying customers, and six-month contribution per customer is $120. The modeled contribution before content cost is $288: 300 × 0.08 × 0.10 × 120. If production costs $500, this scenario does not justify the article on direct acquisition alone. It may still support sales or onboarding, but those benefits need separate evidence.

Use ranges because ranking, click behavior, and conversion are uncertain. The downside case may assume no incremental traffic; the upside case must remain plausible without pretending to be a percentile forecast. A one-point opportunity score can help sort a backlog, but it should not conceal the assumptions that created it.

When evidence is weak, prioritize a low-cost learning asset over a large publication commitment. An expert interview, small comparison update, or useful template can test demand before a whole content hub consumes the team's capacity.

## 6. Technical access before editorial scale

Audit the pages that matter commercially before crawling every imaginable URL. Begin with the homepage, core product pages, pricing or purchase information, top landing pages, and representative article templates. For each, inspect actual status, redirects, robots directives, canonical declarations, rendered content, internal links, and mobile usability. Save observations with timestamp and requested URL so a later deployment can be compared with a known baseline.

Separate discovery from indexing and serving. A page can exist without being discovered, be discovered without being indexed, or be indexed without receiving meaningful visibility. Diagnose each condition rather than labeling everything “Google cannot find us.” A sitemap is a discovery aid, not evidence of indexing. A page returned by a browser is not necessarily accessible to the relevant crawler through the same infrastructure.

Google's technical guidance identifies crawl access, successful responses, and indexable content as basic eligibility requirements. Eligibility still does not guarantee inclusion. [Google Search technical requirements](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/technical).

For ClimbOS, technical findings need reproducible evidence. Store the response status, observed directives, relevant source excerpt, rendering mode, and test environment. Keep inferred causes separate from directly observed failures. If a crawler times out, report a failed inspection rather than asserting that the production page is broken. Repeat only when a second observation would materially change the conclusion.

## 7. JavaScript, design, and performance

A visually striking site can remain accessible when essential information exists as readable text, links have meaningful destinations, and interactions work across input methods. Avoid placing the entire value proposition inside animation frames or images. A browser may display a dramatic sequence while a reader using assistive technology receives almost no explanation. Search troubleshooting and usability improvement often share the same underlying repair: making the information explicit.

Google documents a rendering process for JavaScript pages and potential differences between initial HTML and rendered output. Inspect both when diagnosing missing content, links, or metadata. [JavaScript SEO basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics).

Treat performance as a user and conversion concern with search implications, not a competition to achieve a perfect synthetic score. First fix failures that stop people using the page: unstable layouts near the purchase action, blocked interactions, unreadable text, and excessively delayed primary content. Then evaluate the cost of remaining enhancements. A decorative effect that adds complexity but no useful communication should earn its place through user benefit.

For an animation-heavy business site, use progressive enhancement. Present the proposition and navigation immediately; layer motion onto those fundamentals. In ClimbOS, distinguish a measured production issue from a design heuristic and show the owner why each proposed repair matters.

## 8. Canonicals, duplicates, and information architecture

Duplicate content is often an architecture problem rather than an authoring problem. Tracking parameters, sorting controls, printer views, language variants, and staging copies can create many addresses for related information. Start by defining which URL should represent each important destination, then align internal links, redirects, canonical signals, and sitemaps with that decision.

Google treats canonicalization signals as inputs to its own canonical selection. A declaration should not be described as a guaranteed instruction to select that URL. [Canonical URL guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls).

Use a decision tree in prose. If an old page has a clear replacement and no distinct continuing purpose, plan an appropriate redirect. If multiple accessible URLs intentionally show equivalent information, consolidate signals around the preferred version. If pages satisfy different customer needs, retain them with distinct substance. If a page exists only for private application behavior, determine whether it should be publicly indexable at all.

Before combining pages, inspect inbound links, conversions, support usage, and contractual obligations. A seemingly low-traffic technical reference might be essential to existing customers. Search cleanup should not erase product documentation merely because it generates fewer new visitors. ClimbOS should produce a proposed URL map and rollback plan, with publishing permission separate from analysis permission.

## 9. Editorial briefs that create useful assets

A strong brief is a decision document. It specifies whom the asset serves, the task it resolves, what the business can credibly contribute, the evidence required, the next useful action, and the conditions under which publication should stop. It does not prescribe an arbitrary word count or require every related phrase to appear.

For a hypothetical guide to selecting an invoicing workflow, require a real workflow diagram, a small worked example, failure cases, and evaluation criteria. Ask an experienced operator where the process breaks. If the product cannot handle a scenario, explain that boundary rather than bending the article around an unsupported sales claim. Useful limitations increase the quality of customer self-selection.

Define the asset's original contribution before drafting. It might be a tested procedure, a carefully explained calculation, an interview synthesis with permission, a downloadable worksheet, or a comparison based on reproducible criteria. If the only contribution is rephrasing existing introductions, reconsider whether a new article is justified.

The brief should name an accountable expert and reviewer. AI can organize interviews, propose missing questions, and turn verified material into a readable draft. The human contribution should include judgment and evidence, not merely a final signature on text nobody examined. Store the approved evidence pack alongside the brief so later updates do not lose the origin of claims.

## 10. A repeatable content production workflow

Start with source collection and a rough argument. Extract business facts from verified pages, product documentation, and owner-provided evidence. Maintain a claim ledger containing claim text, source, retrieval date, scope, and whether the claim describes observed fact, vendor assertion, assumption, or opinion. This prevents a polished draft from accidentally upgrading a tentative observation into certainty.

Draft around the reader's sequence of decisions. Explain the problem, show the procedure, work through an example, discuss exceptions, and offer an appropriate next step. The order can vary, but each section must help the reader act. Editing should remove unsupported generalities and repetition while preserving necessary detail.

Then perform separate reviews. The domain reviewer checks technical correctness and omissions. The commercial reviewer checks fit and claims. The editor checks readability, structure, and consistency. The publication check verifies links, examples, permissions, accessibility, and rendering. These are responsibilities, not necessarily separate employees or model calls.

For a small team, use one structured review session with explicit lenses. ClimbOS can generate a review checklist based on the asset type and record which questions remain unanswered. If an unsupported result appears, the workflow should block that claim or require a rewritten statement. It should never fill an empty case study with invented customer names or fabricated improvements.

## 11. Commercial pages deserve equal attention

Many content programs overproduce educational articles while neglecting the page where a buyer must decide. A strong commercial destination answers who the product serves, what it helps accomplish, how it works, what it costs, what constraints apply, and what happens next. The owner should be able to identify the evidence supporting each meaningful claim.

For an alternative or comparison page, choose criteria before choosing a winner. Include relevant differences in workflow, implementation effort, data portability, support, and total cost. State when a competitor or a manual process is the better option. A comparison that omits the customer's real tradeoffs may attract a click while damaging trust and increasing poor-fit acquisition.

For integration pages, distinguish an actual maintained integration from a proposed one, a generic webhook possibility, and an unsupported connection. Show setup requirements and limitations. Do not publish hundreds of “works with” pages based solely on theoretical compatibility.

In ClimbOS, generate commercial-page recommendations only after building a verified capability matrix. For Formkit, use the canonical website as the starting point and inspect current product evidence before asserting any feature. A hypothetical form-related example is not permission to attribute that feature to the real product. Unknowns should become owner questions or research tasks, not persuasive copy.

## 12. Programmatic SEO with a real value test

Programmatic publishing is justified when a repeatable structure can present genuinely distinct, useful information. Examples include accurate inventory pages, documented compatibility information, or a substantive directory with meaningful filtering. The template is a delivery mechanism; the underlying dataset and customer utility are the asset.

Use five gates before scaling: reliable data, distinct user need, sufficient unique substance, maintainable updates, and an appropriate next action. A failure at any gate should stop expansion. Generating a page for every city or job title is not useful merely because software can do it. Ask whether a person arriving directly at that page receives information unavailable from a generic page.

Google's spam policies cover scaled content made primarily to manipulate rankings, including low-value AI generation, as well as doorway and link abuse. The production method does not excuse a lack of user value. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

Pilot one representative group before generating a large library. Audit factual accuracy, duplication, usability, indexing observations, and downstream value. Maintain deletion and correction procedures from the beginning. ClimbOS should budget maintenance alongside creation, because a thousand outdated pages can create a much larger liability than ten excellent pages create opportunity. Scale only when the pilot demonstrates a repeatable benefit to users and feasible editorial operations.

## 13. Internal linking as guided navigation

Internal links should help a reader choose the next useful step. Build connections between introductory explanations, implementation guides, evaluation pages, and relevant product destinations. A good link has context: the reader understands what it offers and why it matters at that moment. Repeating the same exact phrase across unrelated pages is a weak substitute for thoughtful navigation.

Create a small task-based hub when several assets support one workflow. The hub explains the sequence and sends readers to deeper material. Avoid hubs that merely list dozens of titles without helping someone choose. A buyer evaluating software may need a pricing explanation, migration guide, and security information; a new user may need a setup checklist and troubleshooting reference.

Find orphaned assets by comparing the published URL inventory with navigational and contextual links. Before adding links indiscriminately, verify whether the page is still useful and intended to be public. An obsolete campaign page should not be rescued solely to improve a numerical link score.

ClimbOS can suggest links using semantic matching, but should provide the exact source sentence, destination, and reason. A reviewer can then approve useful relationships. Validate that the destination exists and that the anchor accurately describes it. Treat automatic insertion as a publishing action subject to the tenant's granted permissions, with an inspectable diff and rollback.

## 14. Earned distribution and authority

Content needs a distribution path. Identify who already serves the audience, what they find useful, and why they might reference the asset. Original analysis, a practical tool, a clear explanation of a difficult process, or a documented experiment can provide a legitimate reason. The objective is to help the audience and earn attention, not to manufacture a backlink count.

A hypothetical design software company might publish a carefully tested file-exchange checklist and invite relevant educators to critique it. A service firm might contribute expertise to an industry discussion. A local business might collaborate with a genuine community organization. These activities require relevance, truthful representation, and appropriate permissions; they should not be converted into automated mass messages.

ClimbOS has no assumed prospect database. It can identify public organizations or editorial contact routes through authorized research and prepare an outreach proposal, but it must not pretend a usable contact list already exists. Sending messages requires explicit authorization and recipient verification according to the execution policy.

Judge distribution by qualified engagement, useful conversations, referrals, and assisted opportunities. A link from a relevant specialist with a small audience can be more valuable commercially than a large unrelated audience. That is a business evaluation principle, not a claim about an undisclosed ranking weight.

## 15. AI-assisted work that improves evidence

The strongest use of AI is often before the first draft. A model can classify customer questions, find recurring objections in authorized support records, compare an article with current product documentation, or identify where an explanation assumes too much background. These tasks improve relevance without requiring uncontrolled publishing volume.

Use source-bound generation. Supply a compact evidence pack and require the output to distinguish supported statements from suggested additions. Verify that each cited source actually supports the adjacent claim. A model's confidence or fluent paraphrase is not evidence. Deterministic checks can confirm URLs, numerical consistency, and required fields, while a knowledgeable reviewer evaluates meaning.

For updates, compare old and current source snapshots before rewriting. A product change may invalidate a screenshot, instruction sequence, or pricing example. The workflow should propose a targeted revision with a reason and preserve unchanged, still-correct material. Rewriting an entire guide every week can introduce errors while creating a misleading appearance of freshness.

ClimbOS should measure time saved and reviewer correction rate. A workflow that generates drafts quickly but requires extensive factual repair may cost more overall. Keep model use proportional to the task: a deterministic link check does not require strategic reasoning, and sensitive business context should not be copied into an unnecessary prompt.

## 16. An AI-search measurement discipline

Separate four observations: whether a brand is mentioned, whether a page is cited, whether someone visits, and whether a commercially useful outcome follows. These events are related but not interchangeable. A citation without a click may still inform a buyer, yet its business impact is harder to identify. A referral visit may convert later through another channel.

Build a fixed panel of representative customer questions for directional monitoring. Record provider, model or interface where observable, locale, date, prompt, answer, citations, and collection method. Repeated prompts are not independent samples of the whole market. Personalization, changing models, query wording, and product updates can alter results, so report the panel's boundaries.

Use connected provider reporting where available and permitted. For Google, check the newer official report and the property's actual capability before relying on third-party inference. Automated monitoring must use authorized methods; do not build a product promise on prohibited scraping or an assumption that an unauthenticated interface is an API.

ClimbOS should label “observed citation share in this panel” instead of “AI market share.” Track qualified referral visits and assisted opportunities separately. If an owner asks whether AI visibility caused revenue growth, explain the evidence available, possible confounders, and what a stronger test would require.

## 17. A useful answer format without algorithm folklore

Clear answers help people. Begin with the direct answer when the question permits it, then explain assumptions, procedure, examples, and exceptions. Use tables for real comparisons and formulas for calculations. Definitions should identify what a term includes and excludes. These choices improve usability; they should not be sold as a guaranteed technique for extraction by an AI model.

For a technical tutorial, place prerequisites before commands, identify versions, show a successful outcome, and explain common failure conditions. For a comparison, define the criteria and the date of verification. For a calculator, expose assumptions and units. For a case study, distinguish measured results from the author's explanation of why they occurred.

A hypothetical “Which workflow should I use?” page can start with a short decision guide: choose the simple manual process for occasional needs, a reusable template for repeated tasks with stable structure, and software when volume or coordination costs justify implementation. The recommendation is useful because it makes tradeoffs explicit.

Avoid synthetic authority signals. A fabricated author biography, invented research method, or decorative “expert reviewed” badge does not create expertise. ClimbOS should make provenance available in the editing workflow and encourage transparent authorship appropriate to the content's actual production process.

## 18. Local, international, and multilingual search

Local search begins with operational reality: where the business serves customers, what it offers there, how someone contacts it, and whether its information is consistent. Do not generate location pages for places where service is unavailable. A useful location page can explain local logistics, actual availability, and relevant examples. Replacing only a city name creates little value.

International expansion requires more than translating keywords. Evaluate demand, purchasing power, payment options, support language, service coverage, and the economics of delivery. A language may span multiple markets with different expectations. A technically correct translation can still use unfamiliar vocabulary or promise an unavailable payment method.

Google documents hreflang and related methods for identifying localized alternatives. Implement them according to the site's actual URL structure, not as decorative markup. [Localized versions guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions).

For ClimbOS, create a market-readiness record before recommending localization. Include commercial eligibility, language reviewer, support plan, content maintenance owner, and measurement availability. AI can propose translations and terminology alternatives, but a qualified reviewer should verify customer language and factual details. Launch one coherent market experience rather than translating the top of the funnel while leaving pricing, onboarding, and support inaccessible.

## 19. Content economics and a maintenance reserve

A content asset has an acquisition cost and an operating cost. Production includes research, expert time, writing, design, implementation, and review. Maintenance includes fact checks, link repair, product updates, localization, and retirement decisions. AI may reduce some drafting expense while increasing the number of assets that require oversight.

Calculate total cost of ownership over an explicit period. A hypothetical guide costs $400 to create and $100 per quarter to maintain. Over one year, assuming four maintenance cycles, its planned cost is $800. If it produces ten suitable customers at $120 contribution each, the modeled contribution after content cost is $400 before other channel costs. If it produces three, the same asset is unprofitable on that direct basis.

Do not treat first-touch and assisted revenue as additive without deduplication. A customer who reads three guides and later purchases remains one customer. Use a defined attribution model for reporting and separate causal claims from descriptive touchpoint history.

Set aside capacity for maintenance before approving new output. The reserve should reflect how quickly the topic changes and how consequential errors would be. A stable explanatory essay may need infrequent review; a setup guide tied to changing APIs may require much more attention. This prioritization should be driven by risk and user impact rather than a universal update schedule.

## 20. Experiments and causal restraint

SEO rarely provides a clean laboratory. Demand changes, competitors update, search interfaces evolve, and multiple site changes happen together. A traffic increase after publication is evidence of sequence, not automatic proof of causation. Stronger inference requires a plausible counterfactual, stable measurement, and careful handling of confounders.

For a large set of comparable pages, consider a preplanned split test when implementation and search conditions make it appropriate. Randomly assign eligible units, specify the primary outcome, and avoid changing unrelated elements. Analyze at the unit of assignment. If the sample is too small or pages differ greatly, a formal test may give misleading precision.

For a small business, use a documented intervention with matched observations and qualitative evidence. Record baseline dates, exact changes, publication time, expected mechanism, observation window, and major external events. Compare relevant page groups and query groups, but explain their differences. A well-documented observational result can still guide action when its uncertainty remains visible.

ClimbOS should never label an unimplemented recommendation a failed strategy or a published article a successful acquisition campaign before outcomes mature. Track implementation state separately from outcome state. If there is no reliable conversion data, report visibility and engagement outcomes honestly while keeping revenue impact unresolved.

## 21. Incident response and migration planning

A sudden loss of search traffic deserves triage before new content. Check measurement failures, tracking changes, outages, accidental blocking, redirect problems, canonical changes, and removed pages. Segment the decline by landing page, device, geography, and query type. A sitewide failure suggests a different investigation from a drop limited to one topic.

If a migration is planned, inventory important URLs, map replacements, preserve useful content, test redirects, and verify deployment behavior. Keep a rollback path where feasible. Coordinate analytics annotations with release records so subsequent changes can be interpreted. Do not schedule a major navigation rewrite, domain move, and content consolidation simultaneously unless a genuine constraint requires it.

A hypothetical migration loses half its visits immediately. The first investigation should compare old and new response behavior and indexability, rather than conclude that the brand needs more backlinks. If top destinations redirect to an irrelevant homepage, fixing that mapping is more directly connected to the incident than commissioning new articles.

In ClimbOS, incident workflows should prioritize evidence preservation and human-readable findings. An AI can summarize the affected patterns, but destructive remediation requires the appropriate execution authorization. Restore a broken customer path first, then investigate residual visibility changes with a clear record of what was repaired.

## 22. Decision rules for the next action

Use this decision tree as an operating guide. If the business cannot verify what it sells or whom it serves, resolve positioning and capability evidence first. If commercial pages are inaccessible, repair access. If suitable visitors arrive but do not advance, investigate relevance, trust, and the conversion path. If suitable pages convert but receive little exposure, invest in demand coverage and distribution. If customers acquired through search are unprofitable or leave quickly, improve fit and economics before scaling acquisition.

For individual content ideas, ask whether the business has a credible contribution. If not, commission expertise or decline the topic. Ask whether an existing page can satisfy the need. If yes, improve it before creating a competing destination. Ask whether publication creates a maintenance obligation the team can support. If not, narrow the asset or choose a more stable format.

Evaluate recommendations on strategic fit, evidence quality, expected value, implementation effort, maintenance burden, reversibility, and time to learn. Scores help organize discussion but should not override hard gates such as unsupported product claims or unavailable service.

ClimbOS should show the top few feasible actions and the reason each outranks alternatives. A concise decision with a clear mechanism is more useful than a giant keyword export that leaves the owner to invent a strategy.

## 23. Worked 90-day operating example

Imagine a hypothetical B2B software company with an existing website, one part-time subject expert, and no reliable search-to-customer attribution. It has a $1,200 quarterly content budget and twenty total expert hours. These are scenario assumptions, not Formkit facts or recommended universal budgets.

In the first two weeks, the team verifies core product claims, connects permitted analytics, and inspects its five most commercially important destinations. It discovers that a pricing explanation is unclear and that one tutorial references an outdated workflow. The immediate actions are a factual commercial-page revision and a tutorial correction. The team records baseline observations before publishing.

Over the next month, it interviews three existing customers with permission and builds one task-focused guide containing a tested procedure and a reusable worksheet. The expert spends six hours supplying and reviewing the substantive material. AI helps structure the interview notes, identify gaps, and create a first draft. The owner approves the final page and its distribution plan.

During the remaining weeks, the team shares the useful asset through owned channels and a small set of verified, explicitly authorized partner conversations. It observes qualified visits, assisted inquiries, support usage, and maintenance cost. At day ninety, it decides whether the evidence supports another asset. The deliverable is an improved acquisition system and an inspectable learning record, not an assumed ranking victory.

## 24. ClimbOS implementation architecture

Implement SEO intelligence as a set of evidence-backed objects rather than a single enormous prompt. A proposed Convex schema includes business snapshots, source documents, URL observations, demand tasks, content assets, claim records, recommendations, approvals, publication actions, and outcomes. Every object carries a tenant identifier and a version or immutable snapshot reference.

Keep deterministic checks outside the model. URL validity, cost arithmetic, date comparisons, required-field checks, and duplicate action detection should run in code. Use OpenAI-first generation for synthesis and draft assistance, preserving existing providers where already justified. Retrieve only the relevant evidence and playbook sections for a task so unrelated marketing context does not inflate cost or contaminate decisions.

The Vercel interface should present a small number of clear owner actions: review a factual finding, approve a draft, resolve a missing fact, or inspect a result. Advanced users can open the evidence ledger and technical details. Resend may notify an owner about a completed review task when authorized; it does not imply permission to distribute marketing emails.

Keep strategy generation separate from publishing credentials. A content recommendation should remain useful even when the tenant has connected no CMS. Offer an exportable draft and implementation checklist. A connected publishing adapter must enforce scoped permission, content-version approval, idempotency, audit logs, and reconciliation when an external request times out.

## 25. Machine-readable playbooks

The first playbook diagnoses a search opportunity. Thresholds are proposed defaults for evaluation, not search-engine rules. Unknown conversion data triggers instrumentation rather than fabricated estimates.

```yaml
id: seo.opportunity_diagnosis.v1
scope: tenant
inputs:
  required: [business_snapshot_id, goal, observation_window]
  optional: [search_report_id, analytics_report_id, url_inventory_id]
steps:
  - id: verify_business
    output: capability_claims
    on_missing: request_owner_evidence
  - id: inspect_access
    checks: [http_status, robots_directives, canonical, rendered_content]
    on_failed_inspection: record_unknown
  - id: map_demand
    output: customer_tasks
    exclude: [unsupported_features, unavailable_locations]
  - id: model_value
    method: deterministic_scenarios
    assumptions_required: true
  - id: recommend
    max_actions: 3
    require: [mechanism, evidence_ids, effort, maintenance, measurement]
authorization:
  research: authorized_sources_only
  publish: separate_approval
outputs: [diagnosis, recommendations, unresolved_questions]
```

The second playbook specifies an asset's review contract. It is deliberately independent of a particular CMS so ClimbOS can produce useful work before a publishing integration exists.

These contracts are starter implementation policies inside a complete operating method. Before production use, adapt field names to the repository, validate schemas, and write meaningful tests for unauthorized publication, stale approvals, missing sources, duplicate jobs, and cross-tenant access. A valid YAML document is not proof that an orchestration system implements it correctly.

## 26. Evaluation and an honest completion standard

Evaluate a ClimbOS SEO run on whether it identifies a real business constraint, uses current relevant evidence, recommends feasible work, preserves unknowns, and defines a measurement path. Include fixtures with no analytics, a new domain, a strong site with weak conversion, an unsupported integration claim, and a traffic drop caused by broken tracking. These cases expose whether the system reasons from evidence or repeats a generic publication calendar.

Content quality review should assess task completion, original contribution, factual precision, useful examples, and maintenance feasibility. Do not use word count as a substitute for usefulness. A short answer can be complete; a long guide can fail if it avoids the customer's hard decision. Machine-generated assessments can assist triage but should be calibrated against knowledgeable human review.

A completed search plan contains an evidence snapshot, diagnosed constraint, prioritized feasible actions, responsible owner, resource estimate, implementation instructions, observation window, and explicit success or stop criteria. A completed asset contains verified claims, working references, usable structure, and an approved publication version. A completed experiment contains implementation evidence and a mature observation period, or a clear explanation of why results remain inconclusive.

The durable advantage is disciplined learning: make useful information, connect it to a suitable commercial path, inspect what happened, and improve the system without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

## 27. A practical weekly decision meeting

Run the content program through a short decision meeting that separates activity from learning. Before the meeting, prepare one page showing implemented changes, observations that matured, unresolved measurement issues, and the next feasible decisions. Do not present every available dashboard. The owner needs to understand whether the current work is addressing the diagnosed constraint and what evidence would justify a change in direction.

Review one asset in depth. Ask whether it helped the intended audience complete the intended task. Inspect actual questions, relevant engagement, and the next-step experience. A page may attract the correct reader but send them to an unsuitable general signup flow. Another may receive few visits yet remove a recurring sales objection. Record these mechanisms separately instead of forcing both into a single traffic target.

Then review the backlog against available capacity. If the expert can supply two hours this week, select work that can be completed and reviewed within that limit. Do not approve five drafts that each depend on interviews nobody has scheduled. An unfinished inventory of content ideas has little commercial value and creates coordination cost.

End with explicit decisions: continue, revise, pause, retire, or investigate. Assign one accountable owner and a date for the next meaningful observation. For a paused initiative, state what new evidence would reopen it. This prevents old speculative opportunities from returning every week as if they were fresh discoveries.

ClimbOS can prepare the meeting pack by comparing versioned recommendations with implementation records and outcome windows. It should highlight contradictory evidence rather than merely celebrate positive movement. If impressions rise while qualified activations fall, suggest checking audience mix and page changes. If attribution breaks, flag the affected reporting period before calculating return. If a source document changes, identify which recommendations may need revalidation.

This operating cadence makes the library actionable for a small business. It converts a broad discipline into a manageable cycle of evidence, decisions, implementation, and learning. The automation opportunity is coordination and verification: assemble the relevant facts, identify what deserves attention, and leave a clear record. Publication volume becomes a consequence of useful opportunities and available expertise rather than the definition of progress.
