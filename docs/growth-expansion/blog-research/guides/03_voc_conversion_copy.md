## Durable principles vs dated material

**Durable (author ideas, adopted with conditions):**
- Write from customers' own words, ranked by how often a theme recurs.
- The top of a page must match the message that brought the visitor; the body answers the prospect's questions in their order.
- Proof must fit the claim it supports; one proof per place.
- Diagnose the single biggest risk a buyer feels and put the matching reducer near the call to action.
- Audit a running email flow before rewriting it; protect what works.

**Dated or context-bound (kept as labelled references, never as universal thresholds):**
- Reported copy-test uplifts on single pages (one business, one period).
- An open-rate red-flag threshold for day-zero emails (opens are unreliable; ClimbOS doesn't judge on them).
- Percentages from a 127-trial onboarding-email review (one analyst's sample, 2024).

**How far to trust it:** Uplifts are reported cases, not forecasts. One shortcut (search-operator review scraping) may break site terms and is not adopted.

## CH-C1: Adjacent-outcome review mining that sets message order

**Decision it helps make.** Where to source customer language when a business has few or no own reviews, and how mined themes decide what the page says first.

**Applies when**
- New business or under ~20 own reviews
- Landing page, ad or email brief needs a headline and section order
- A category has public reviews of products or services that serve the same outcome

**Avoid when**
- The business already has 50+ recent own reviews or survey data (use those first)
- Adjacent products serve a clearly different buyer (e.g. luxury vs budget) and language would mislead
- The only sources are behind logins or terms that forbid scraping

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Verbatim snippets with URLs
- Pool label per snippet: own / competitor / adjacent-outcome
- Theme counts as n of N per pool
- Named audience and the outcome they want
- The list of what they use today (including DIY or doing nothing)

**Steps**
1. Write down audience, desired outcome, current alternatives and where they are discussed before searching
2. Add an adjacent-outcome pool: reviews of books, tools or services that solve the same problem, kept separate from competitor reviews
3. Record exact wording into categories (problem, trigger, outcome, objection, proof) without summarising
4. Rank themes by count and source spread; the top recurring concern goes highest in the message order
5. Offer 3 to 5 headline candidates from high-confidence phrases (2+ independent sources); a single striking quote is a candidate only, flagged 'single source'

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: use competitor 2 to 3 star reviews, category forums and adjacent-outcome reviews
- No customers yet: same, plus 3 to 5 short episode-based conversations per guide 05
- Low traffic: test the mined angle in ads or email first, then move the winner to the page

**Metrics**
- Share of phrase-bank entries with 2+ sources
- Downstream: lead-form or booking conversion of the page (not button clicks)

**Guardrails**
- Never present a mined phrase as a testimonial
- Adjacent-outcome evidence is labelled Low/Medium confidence and never blended with own-customer counts
- Do not use Google or Amazon search-operator scraping that breaches terms; fetch only what search results return

**Observation window.** Language bank: refresh every 6 to 12 months or when the offer changes. Page tests: at least two full business cycles (typically 2 to 4 weeks) or until a pre-set conversion count.

**Stop or review when**
- Phrase bank has fewer than 3 themes meeting the finding bar: label thin and propose owner interviews
- Adjacent pool dominates (>70% of snippets): flag that language may not fit this buyer

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** skills/voc-research/SKILL.md (source plan, step 3, add adjacent-outcome pool) and skills/landing-pages/SKILL.md step 4 (theme rank informs section order)

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-01-F1, CH-01-F2, CH-01-F3, CH-01-F4, CH-02-F4. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: Copyhackers' main contributions beyond ClimbOS's current skill are the adjacent-outcome source (reviews of things that solve the same problem, not just competitors) and the explicit link from theme frequency to message order. The 400% click case is a single unverified vendor case with a much smaller downstream lift.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: voc-research adds an 'adjacent-outcome' pool type with its own N and a confidence ceiling of Medium. landing-pages orders problem and objection sections by theme rank from the VOC bank, and states that rank in the brief.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** Strong overlap with voc-research (verbatim, linked, counted, phrase bank, pools separated) and copy-fundamentals (use VoC language). The new parts are the adjacent-outcome pool and the theme-rank-to-section-order link.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** Copyhackers lifts one standout quote as the headline. ClimbOS should require frequency or label it single-source. Copyhackers suggests Google site-search operators on Amazon review pages; ClimbOS should not depend on that, given terms and the fetch-only-from-search constraint.

## CH-C2: Buyer survey and thank-you poll mapped to page sections

**Decision it helps make.** When the business has customers, collect own-customer evidence with a short survey or a one-question post-purchase poll, code it into fixed groups, and map each group to a page section.

**Applies when**
- Business has an emailable customer list with consent for service or research messages
- Few public reviews but real buyers
- A sales page, landing page or onboarding sequence is being written or rewritten

**Avoid when**
- No lawful basis to contact the list
- Fewer than ~10 likely respondents (use 3 to 5 interviews instead)
- Survey would be sent from a marketing-only consent that excludes research

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Raw responses stored privately with respondent count and date
- Coding into: problem, feeling, prior attempts, trigger, desired short-term outcome, hesitation/objection, why it mattered
- Count per code
- Owner approval of questions and send
- Consent basis recorded
- Decision the survey informs (e.g. hook and FAQ for a page)

**Steps**
1. Draft 6 to 10 open questions (not 22) about the last purchase: what was happening, what they tried before, what nearly stopped them, what they expected, what changed
2. Optional one-question thank-you poll at signup or purchase asking what brought them in today
3. Code responses into the fixed groups; keep counts and keep raw text
4. Map: problem and feeling → hook and intro; prior attempts → alternatives and agitation; hesitations → FAQ and the copy near the CTA; triggers → ad and email timing
5. Report respondent count and response rate honestly; mark non-buyers as missing

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: run the survey or poll
- No customers yet: episode interviews per guide 05, or CH-C1 mining
- Low traffic or tiny list: 3 to 5 phone interviews beat a survey with 4 replies

**Metrics**
- Respondent count and response rate
- Share of FAQ items traceable to a coded hesitation
- Page conversion after rewrite vs before (same traffic source)

**Guardrails**
- Never quote a respondent publicly without separate permission
- Never generate synthetic survey responses
- Survey invitation is not a marketing email; keep it free of promotion
- Do not quote the reported 87% response rate as an expectation

**Observation window.** Survey open 7 to 14 days; thank-you poll accumulates continuously, reviewed monthly.

**Stop or review when**
- Under 10 responses after 14 days: stop and switch to interviews
- Responses dominated by one segment: report as segment-specific

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** skills/voc-research/SKILL.md (new 'own-customer survey' source branch) and skills/landing-pages/SKILL.md step 6 (objections sourced from coded hesitations)

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-02-F1, CH-02-F2, CH-03-F1, CH-03-F2, CH-03-F3, CH-03-F4. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: Copyhackers' survey case used a large, unusually engaged paid list. The transferable part for small businesses is the coding groups and the mapping from groups to page sections, not the length or the response rate.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: when own customers exist and consent allows, voc-research offers a short survey or a one-question poll as an owner-approved action. Every FAQ item on a page cites a coded hesitation, a review theme or an owner-confirmed sales objection.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** voc-research covers public reviews only. Guide 05 covers interviews. Neither covers surveys or thank-you polls. landing-pages already asks for objections from the VOC bank.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** Copyhackers shows a 22-question survey. ClimbOS's brevity rule and small lists favour 6 to 10 questions. Copyhackers found marketer forum comments self-promotional; this supports voc-research's caution on incentivised or performative content.

## CH-C3: Top-of-page match, then answer the prospect's questions in order

**Decision it helps make.** How to order a landing or home page: the opening matches awareness and the inbound message; the body follows the prospect's own question sequence; button copy depends on readiness.

**Applies when**
- Any landing, home or service page
- Rewrite of a page that repeats proof or risk reducers in random places

**Avoid when**
- Most-aware traffic on a short offer page, where the sequence collapses to offer, terms, CTA
- Pages whose order is fixed by platform (marketplace listings)

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Traffic source and its promise
- Awareness level
- Proof inventory from confirmed facts
- Objections from the VOC bank
- One conversion goal
- Stated arrival mindset, desired action and the belief change needed between them

**Steps**
1. Write three lines: arrival mindset, desired action, belief change needed
2. Opening (roughly the first screen): message match to the ad or email plus awareness-appropriate framing
3. Body: what this is → why it matters to this reader → people like them use it → how it works → what changes for them → why believe it → next step; skip or merge steps the reader does not need
4. Place each proof once, where its question is asked; do not repeat social proof and guarantees at random points
5. Readiness-based buttons: early buttons state the benefit of continuing; final buttons state the step
6. Judge any test on the end conversion (booking, purchase), not clicks, especially when button count changes

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: the 'people like them' step uses a mechanism, process photos or confirmed counts, never placeholders
- No customers yet: skip peer proof; strengthen mechanism and risk reversal
- Low traffic: no A/B test; ship the ordered version and track the end conversion over time

**Metrics**
- End conversion rate by traffic source
- Scroll depth to the CTA section
- Ratio of clicks to conversions (to catch click-inflating buttons)

**Guardrails**
- Every proof item traces to confirmed facts (lint.ts claim ledger)
- No authority proof that is not confirmed and permitted
- The question sequence is a scaffold; do not print its labels as headings verbatim

**Observation window.** 2 to 4 weeks or a pre-set number of end conversions per variant before judging.

**Stop or review when**
- Clicks rise but end conversions do not: revert button changes
- Bounce rate on mobile rises after the rewrite: review the opening match

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** skills/landing-pages/SKILL.md steps 3 to 5 (hero, section order, proof placement) and the page brief format

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-04-F1, CH-04-F2, CH-04-F3, CH-04-F4. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: the 108% case is a bundled change with no reported statistics, so it shows the approach can coincide with large gains, not that it causes them. The durable parts are the ordering logic and judging on end conversions.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: the landing-page brief includes the arrival-mindset / action / belief-change triple, and each proof item is assigned to exactly one question slot. Critic flags duplicated proof blocks and tests judged on clicks.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** landing-pages already has message match, an awareness table, a default section order and proof-near-claim. The new parts are the question-order rationale, the single placement of proof, readiness-based button copy, and measuring on end conversion when button count changes.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** landing-pages bans 'Learn more' and asks for verb + outcome CTAs. Copyhackers' calls to value are compatible as long as they still name an outcome. Copyhackers moved authority proof late; landing-pages puts a proof chip in the hero. Reconcile: the hero proof chip should be a relevance proof (e.g. local rating), with authority or influencer proof later.

## CH-C4: Sweep-style critic: clarity, so-what, matched proof

**Decision it helps make.** How the ClimbOS LLM critic reviews a draft after lint.ts: staged passes that flag (not rewrite) unclear lines, benefits with no consequence, and claims whose proof type does not match the claim.

**Applies when**
- Any publishable copy after deterministic lint passes
- Owner-supplied copy audits

**Avoid when**
- Very short assets (one-line ad headline) where a single combined check suffices

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Confirmed facts list
- Draft with line or field paths
- lint.ts has already removed banned phrases, untraced numbers and unsupported claims

**Steps**
1. Clarity pass: flag lines a stranger could misread, and blocks joining two unrelated ideas
2. So-what pass: for each claim, check that the reader's consequence is stated; flag benefit labels with no result
3. Proof-match pass: classify each claim (ease, result, speed, trust, workflow, price) and check that adjacent proof is of the matching type; a generic screenshot or rating does not prove an ease or speed claim
4. Timing check: proof that a step is easy sits before the step's CTA
5. Return to clarity after fixes
6. Output highlights with reasons; the writer agent fixes; re-lint

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: proof-match accepts mechanism explanations and demonstrations as proof for ease claims
- No customers yet: claims needing peer proof are softened or reframed, not padded
- Low traffic: same critic; no testing implications

**Metrics**
- Critic flags per 100 words, before and after
- Share of claims with matched proof
- Owner edit rate after critic

**Guardrails**
- Critic never adds facts; it flags
- A flagged claim without available proof is cut or reframed, never backed with invented proof
- Do not run the full seven-pass loop on every asset; keep latency low

**Observation window.** Per draft; review critic flag rates monthly in evals.

**Stop or review when**
- Critic suggests a proof item absent from confirmed facts: hard fail
- Critic rewrites voice rather than flagging: tighten prompt

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** LLM critic stage after shared/agents/lint.ts; skills/copy-fundamentals/SKILL.md step 9 and quality bar

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-05-F1, CH-05-F2, CH-05-F3, CH-06-F3. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: lint.ts checks that a claim is supported somewhere in the evidence. It cannot judge whether the nearby proof actually proves that kind of claim. The sweep method fills that gap and fits an LLM critic.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: the critic adds a 'proof_type_mismatch' warning, with a claim type and a proof type per flag, and a 'missing_consequence' warning for benefit labels with no reader result.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** copy-fundamentals has the proof hierarchy, proof within two sentences, the so-what chain, and specificity tests. lint.ts blocks unsupported claims and untraced numbers. The new parts are claim-type to proof-type matching and proof-before-commitment timing.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** Copyhackers lists live-usage notifications as proof. ClimbOS should allow them only from real event data and never simulate them. Copyhackers' seven-pass loop at about 30 minutes is a human process; ClimbOS should compress it to 2 to 3 critic passes.

## CH-C5: One dominant risk, matched reducer near the CTA

**Decision it helps make.** Which risk reducer an offer, page or email uses: diagnose the single biggest perceived risk and match the reducer to it, instead of defaulting to a guarantee or testimonials.

**Applies when**
- Offer pages, booking pages, trial sign-ups, checkout-adjacent copy
- VOC shows hesitation themes

**Avoid when**
- Free, low-commitment actions where extra reassurance adds clutter
- No VOC or owner input to tell which risk dominates (then ask the owner a single question)

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Coded hesitations or objections with counts (voc-research)
- Confirmed reducers the business actually offers (trial, guarantee, demo, insurance, named reviews)
- Owner-confirmed list of existing risk reducers

**Steps**
1. Rate decision risk for one reader: low, medium, high
2. Classify hesitations into: will it work, is it worth it, will it fit my life, will others approve, will I be satisfied
3. Pick the top risk by VOC count (or owner judgement, labelled)
4. Match the reducer: demo or mechanism for will-it-work; guarantee, price clarity or trial for worth-it; setup help or flexible terms for fit; peer proof from the same segment for approval
5. Place it at or just above the primary CTA; consider removing copy that raises new doubts
6. Any reducer the business does not offer goes to the owner as a suggestion, never into copy

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: use owner's top three hesitations heard in sales calls, labelled owner-reported
- No customers yet: rely on mechanism and reversible first step
- Low traffic: pick one reducer and track booking or purchase rate over time rather than A/B testing

**Metrics**
- Conversion at the step where the reducer sits
- Form abandonment
- Pre-purchase questions received on the topic

**Guardrails**
- Never invent a guarantee, trial, user count or peer logo
- Peer counts (e.g. 'N businesses this week') only from confirmed, current data
- One reducer focus per page, not all five risk types

**Observation window.** 2 to 4 weeks or a pre-set conversion count.

**Stop or review when**
- Owner cannot confirm the reducer: drop it from copy
- Conversion falls after adding reducer copy: review whether it surfaced a new doubt

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** skills/landing-pages/SKILL.md step 6 (objection mapping) and step 3 (micro-copy under CTA); skills/voc-research/SKILL.md step 8 (counter types)

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-06-F1, CH-06-F2, CH-06-F4. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: ClimbOS maps counters by JTBD force (anxiety, habit, price). The risk-type lens is finer-grained for anxiety, and it adds 'pick one'. They are complementary.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: the voc-research objection list gains a 'risk type' column. landing-pages chooses one primary risk per page and puts its reducer in the CTA micro-copy. Reducers not owner-confirmed are emitted only in owner notes.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** voc-research step 8 maps anxiety to risk reversal or proof. landing-pages step 6 places objections near the CTA. lint.ts blocks unsupported claims. The new parts are the risk taxonomy and the single-risk focus.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** Copyhackers says choosing the risk is a gut check. ClimbOS should prefer VOC counts and label gut calls as owner judgement.

## CH-C6: Audit an existing email automation before rewriting

**Decision it helps make.** When an owner has a running flow, ClimbOS first maps it and scores per-email performance and sending practices, protects what works, and only then rewrites.

**Applies when**
- Owner connects an ESP with an existing automation
- Request to 'improve my emails' or 'fix my welcome flow'

**Avoid when**
- No existing flow (write fresh per lifecycle-email)
- Fewer than ~100 recipients per email (metrics too noisy; audit structure only)

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Flow export or screenshots: triggers, delays, branches, tags
- Per-email recipients, clicks, conversions, unsubscribes, complaints, attributed revenue
- Domain authentication status
- Read-only ESP access or exports
- Named conversion goal

**Steps**
1. Map the flow outside the ESP: triggers, delays, branches, action and inaction triggers, exits
2. Find black holes: contacts who finish or stall with no next step
3. Record per-email click rate, conversion rate, unsubscribe and complaint rates, revenue per recipient; treat opens as a deliverability hint only
4. Classify: protect (clear standouts), fix first (high unsubscribe or complaints, no exit on conversion, broken consent), queue (cadence, too many or too few segments, dead ends)
5. Audit sending: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, real reply-to, postal address, list hygiene
6. Report to owner before any rewrite; rewrite only red emails first, keeping green ones as controls

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: unaffected
- No customers yet: no flow to audit; build fresh
- Low traffic: structural audit only; judge on replies and conversions over a longer window

**Metrics**
- Clicks, conversions and revenue per recipient per email
- Unsubscribe and complaint rates (complaints <0.1%)
- Number of black-hole contacts before and after

**Guardrails**
- No rewrite of a green email without the owner's say-so
- No open-rate targets; opens are distorted by Apple Mail Privacy Protection
- Read-only access; never send or edit flows without explicit approval
- Last-touch revenue is labelled as over-crediting late emails

**Observation window.** Baseline: last 30 and 90 days. After changes: 30 days or a pre-set number of conversions.

**Stop or review when**
- Complaint rate reaches 0.3%: stop sends and review list and consent
- No conversion data available: audit structure and deliverability only, and say so

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** skills/lifecycle-email/SKILL.md (new 'optimise an existing flow' mode before step 5)

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-08-F1, CH-08-F2, CH-08-F3, CH-08-F4, CH-08-F5. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: lifecycle-email covers writing new flows well, but has no mode for diagnosing an existing one. The Copyhackers audit order (map, measure, classify, then rewrite) fills that gap.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: when an existing flow is connected, lifecycle-email must output a flow map, a per-email protect/fix/queue table and a deliverability checklist before any new copy.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** lifecycle-email already has deliverability requirements, exits, branches and click-, reply- and conversion-based metrics, and ignores opens. The new parts are the audit-first sequence, black-hole detection and protect/fix/queue classification.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** Copyhackers uses a day-zero open-rate threshold (<40%) as a red flag. ClimbOS ignores opens as a success metric and should not adopt that threshold; at most, a sharp drop in opens is a deliverability hint.

## CH-C7: Next-step onboarding with stuck questions and usage recaps

**Decision it helps make.** Which onboarding emails a trial or new-account flow needs: one next step per email triggered by what the user has or hasn't done, plus a usage recap and a 'where did you get stuck' question for inactive users.

**Applies when**
- SaaS, app, membership or booking-portal onboarding
- Product has trackable activation events

**Avoid when**
- No event data (fall back to time-based with clear labelling)
- One-off service purchases with no product use (use post-purchase type instead)

**Evidence and prerequisites**
- Activation milestone confirmed by owner
- Events available to the ESP (did X, did not do Y)
- Event tracking connected
- Named sender and read reply-to

**Steps**
1. Define the activation event and the 2 to 4 steps before it
2. One email per missing step, triggered by its absence, with one CTA
3. For users inactive after N days, send a plain question asking where they got stuck (open, not yes/no)
4. Send a usage recap (what they did, one thing they missed) when data supports it
5. Exit on activation; move to conversion or value emails

**When data, traffic, budget or integrations are missing**
- No reviews: unaffected
- No customers yet: build the flow from the product's own steps; mark windows as assumptions
- Low traffic: judge on replies and activation counts, not rates

**Metrics**
- Activation rate
- Time to activation
- Reply count to stuck-question emails
- Trial-to-paid conversion

**Guardrails**
- Recaps only from real usage data, never estimated
- Don't cite the study's shares or third-party churn stats as facts in copy
- Trial auto-renew notice still required

**Observation window.** One full trial length plus 14 days per cohort.

**Stop or review when**
- Stuck-question emails get complaints: soften cadence
- Activation flat after 2 cohorts: revisit the activation definition with the owner

**Where it belongs in ClimbOS.** skills/lifecycle-email/SKILL.md sequence table (Onboarding row) and step 5

**Author guidance (source findings):** CH-07-F1, CH-07-F2, CH-07-F3. See the findings in [source_registry.json](../source_registry.json).

**Interpretation (ours):** LABELLED INTERPRETATION: the 127-trial study shows what companies send (from one mostly inactive recipient), not what performs. It is useful as a gap signal (few send usage recaps), not as a benchmark.

**Proposed ClimbOS rule:** PROPOSED RULE: the lifecycle-email onboarding row adds 'stuck question' and 'usage recap' as optional jobs, both gated on real event data.

**Overlap with existing guidance:** lifecycle-email already says onboarding fires on behaviour, one email per step to the activation event. The new parts are the open stuck-question email and the usage recap.

**Disagreements and what decides them:** None material. The article leans on secondhand statistics, which ClimbOS should not reuse.
